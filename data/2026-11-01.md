<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Galaxy-scale simulations typically cannot resolve supernova cooling radii, so momentum-based subgrid models underresolve the hot gas around young stars and misrepresent thermal feedback, hot superbubble interiors, and the thermal/kinetic energy partition.

Method: Anchor-and-refine feedback: each star particle is assigned a host cell that is prevented from being displaced (anchoring), preserving a thermal-feedback reservoir. If the cooling radius is resolved, all supernova energy is injected as thermal energy into the host cell; if not, the host cell is refined and terminal momentum is deposited into surrounding gas cells. Validated with idealized tests and an isolated Milky Way-mass galaxy simulation.

Result: Idealized tests match high-resolution simulations, giving consistent superbubble properties and hot interiors without artificial cooling. In the isolated Milky Way-mass galaxy, the scheme resolves finer X-ray-emitting structures and a sharply defined X-ray-bright central mid-plane, and yields a smoother star formation history than pure hybrid feedback. Hot gas dominates outflow mass loading, with mass-loading factor plateauing at ~0.2 above z>1 kpc; phase decomposition shows hot gas sustains a steady energy-driven wind while cooler outflows decline with height due to radiative cooling and gravitational fallback.

Conclusion: Thermal feedback plays a key role in driving galactic winds. By preserving hot superbubble interiors and the thermal/kinetic energy partition, anchor-and-refine improves treatment of hot gas, star formation, and feedback at limited resolution, supporting future galaxy-scale and cosmological zoom-in simulations.
