### [1] [Gaia FGK benchmark stars: abundances of \textit{n}-capture elements of the third version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23786)
*S. Vitali,P. Jofré,L. Casamiquela,U. Heiter,C. Aguilera Gómez,D. Barrios López,S. Blanco Cuaresma,A. Escorza,I. Hernández Araya,T. Signor,C. Soubiran,H. Sinclair Wentworth,C. Worley*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This study extends the Gaia Benchmark Stars (GBSv3) by providing homogeneous neutron-capture element abundances using high-resolution spectra and the iSpec code, addressing challenges like weak lines and saturation, to establish a robust reference for calibrating spectroscopic surveys and studying Milky Way chemical evolution.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Precise and accurate stellar parameters and chemical abundances are crucial for studies of stellar structure, evolution, and Galactic chemical history; however, reliable heavy-element (n-capture) abundances were lacking for the GBSv3 sample, motivating this work.

Method: High-resolution spectra from GBSv3 were analyzed using the iSpec code. A clustering algorithm grouped stars by stellar parameters and metallicities to tailor line assessments. An in-depth line selection addressed weak lines, saturation, and atomic data sensitivity to derive homogeneous n-capture abundances.

Result: Homogeneous abundances of n-capture elements were obtained across the GBSv3 sample, agreeing well with literature. The approach overcame challenges like paucity of usable lines and saturation, yielding reliable measurements.

Conclusion: The extended abundance set establishes a robust reference scale, supports using chemical abundances as precise tracers of Milky Way star formation and chemical evolution, and serves as a legacy calibration sample for current and future spectroscopic surveys.

Abstract: In the current era, in which an unprecedented wealth of data is available for the study of the Milky Way, the Gaia Benchmark Stars (GBS) have become an established reference and calibration sample. Studies of stellar structure and evolution, as well as the chemical history of our Galaxy, largely rely on large spectroscopic surveys and their output catalogs. In this context, deriving precise and accurate stellar parameters and chemical abundances is of paramount importance. This study provides the determination of neutron(n)-capture element abundances, extending the set of chemical abundances available for the third GBS release (GBSv3). Based on the compilation of high-resolution spectra assembled for GBSv3 and consistently with the spectral analysis adopted for the chemical abundances of GBSv3, we used the iSpec code to derive heavy-element abundances. We infer homogeneous abundances of n-capture elements across the GBSv3 sample using an in-depth line assessment tailored to different groups identified through a clustering algorithm that accounts for the diversity in stellar parameters and metallicities. This approach addresses key challenges in the spectral analysis of these elements, including the paucity of usable lines, weak line strengths, saturation effects, and sensitivity to atomic data. It yields reliable measurements, establishing an extended and robust reference scale in good agreement with the literature. This compilation of these abundances is based on the GBS's robust and accurate atmospheric parameters, together with the analysis of a large sample of stellar spectra per star, which provides a reliable and homogeneous spectral analysis. It supports the use of chemical abundances as precise tracers of the Milky Way's star formation history and chemical evolution, and constitutes a legacy sample for the calibration of current and future spectroscopic surveys.

### [2] [The first 3D MHD core-collapse progenitors II: Rotation, magnetic-field amplification, and magnetic topology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22938)
*Adam Griffiths,Miguel-Ángel Aloy,Martin Obergaulinger*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper uses 3D MHD simulations of two Wolf-Rayet progenitors to show that pre-collapse angular momentum reorganizes toward cylindrical profiles (Ω∝ϖ⁻² in convective regions) and magnetic fields amplify with comparable toroidal/poloidal components, challenging 1D stellar evolution assumptions and providing improved initial conditions for magnetorotational core-collapse simulations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The most energetic core-collapse supernovae are thought to arise from rapidly rotating, magnetised progenitors, but the 3D pre-collapse structure of their angular momentum and magnetic fields remains poorly constrained, limiting the realism of magnetorotational core-collapse simulations.

Method: Used Aenus-Alcar to evolve two compact Wolf-Rayet progenitors (computed with GENEC and MESA) through the final minutes before core collapse via 3D magnetohydrodynamic simulations.

Result: Rotation profiles near the inner core reorganize from shellular to cylindrical structure. Convective regions show constant specific-angular-momentum profiles (Ω∝ϖ⁻²). Seed magnetic fields amplify with comparable toroidal/poloidal components and substantial small-scale power, linking magnetically disconnected regions from 1D models.

Conclusion: Multidimensional evolution substantially modifies both angular-momentum distribution and magnetic topology of pre-collapse progenitors, providing a physically motivated basis for constructing more realistic initial conditions for magnetorotational core-collapse simulations and improving prescriptions of magneto-convective angular-momentum transport in late stellar evolution.

Abstract: The most energetic core-collapse supernovae are thought to arise from rapidly rotating, magnetised progenitors. However, the three-dimensional pre-collapse structure of their angular momentum and magnetic fields remains poorly constrained, limiting the realism of magnetorotational core-collapse simulations. We investigate the angular-momentum distribution, magnetic-field amplification and magnetic topology of physically consistent three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic pre-supernova progenitors. We used Aenus-Alcar to evolve two compact Wolf--Rayet progenitors, computed with the stellar-evolution codes GENEC and MESA, through the final minutes before core collapse. Our models suggest that the rotation profile near the inner core can depart from a purely shellular distribution and reorganise toward a more cylindrical structure. In convective regions, hydrodynamic Reynolds stresses drive the flow toward an approximately constant specific-angular-momentum profile, corresponding to an average rotation profile close to $Ω\propto \varpi^{-2}$ ($\varpi$ denotes the cylindrical radius). Convective regions amplify seed magnetic fields, transported from neighbouring radiative layers, producing saturated fields with comparable toroidal and poloidal components and a topology containing substantial small-scale power. As a result, regions that are magnetically disconnected in the original one-dimensional stellar-evolution description become magnetically linked in the multidimensional models. Multidimensional evolution can substantially modify both the angular-momentum distribution and magnetic topology of pre-collapse progenitors. They provide a physically motivated basis for constructing more realistic initial conditions for magnetorotational core-collapse simulations and for improving prescriptions of magneto-convective angular-momentum transport in late stellar evolution.

### [3] [The Solar Dynamics Observatory in the Living With a Star Era: From Solar Observations to Predictive Heliophysics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22999)
*Madhulika Guhathakurta*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: SDO (launched 2010) transitioned heliophysics from event-based analysis to continuous dynamical characterization of the solar atmosphere, enabling space-weather prediction, producing 8,400+ papers, and supporting operational forecasting and machine-learning via SHARPs datasets.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To shift heliophysics from discrete event identification to characterizing the continuously evolving solar atmosphere as the upstream boundary of a coupled Sun-heliosphere-geospace system, enabling predictive capabilities for space weather and human exploration.

Method: Co-registered, high-cadence, full-disk measurements from three instruments (HMI, AIA, EVE) on SDO, combined with an open-data policy producing a uniformly calibrated archive, enabling statistical, model-driven, and machine-learning approaches.

Result: Over 8,400 peer-reviewed publications; SHARPs datasets used operationally for flare probability forecasting and as standard training data for machine-learning prediction systems.

Conclusion: SDO's archive serves as a foundational resource for data-driven approaches and foundation models in predictive heliophysics, with direct impacts on space-weather specification, ionospheric/thermospheric modeling, heliospheric transport, and radiation environment assessment.

Abstract: The Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO), launched in 2010 as part of NASA's Living With a Star (LWS) program, represents a methodological transition in heliophysics: from identifying discrete solar events to characterizing the continuously evolving state of the solar atmosphere as the upstream boundary of a coupled Sun-heliosphere-geospace system. By providing co-registered, high-cadence, full-disk measurements via the Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager (HMI), Atmospheric Imaging Assembly (AIA), and Extreme Ultraviolet Variability Experiment (EVE), SDO has enabled the solar atmosphere to be treated as a dynamical system evolving in time. This capability has direct consequences for space-weather specification, ionospheric and thermospheric modeling, heliospheric transport, and the radiation environment relevant to human exploration beyond low Earth orbit. The mission's open-data policy has produced a uniform, continuously calibrated archive contributing to more than 8,400 peer-reviewed publications, enabling statistical, model-driven, and machine-learning approaches. The Space-weather HMI Active Region Patches (SHARPs) are used directly in operational flare probability forecasting and serve as the standard training dataset for machine-learning prediction systems. This perspective traces SDO's scientific lineage, infrastructure role, and the emerging applications of its archive to data-driven approaches and foundation models in the service of predictive heliophysics.
