TL;DR: This paper catalogs 35 cases of 43 GHz SiO maser spectra dominated by isotopologue (29SiO) emission (iso-dom spectra) from the BAaDE survey and literature, finding these occur in ~0.2% of SiO maser-bearing AGB stars.   <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To catalog rare cases where 29SiO maser emission dominates over 28SiO at 43 GHz (iso-dom spectra), explore commonalities among these sources, and explain the physical cause of such unusual maser signatures in AGB stars.

Method: Analysis of SiO maser line ratios from the infrared-color-selected BAaDE survey, supplemented with literature detections; identification of iso-dom spectra based on dominant isotopologue transitions; comparison of infrared colors, stellar periods, and variability of spectral signatures.

Result: 35 iso-dom spectra identified (~0.2% of SiO maser-bearing stars); these sources have bluer infrared colors than other stars of the same period; the iso-dom nature is variable but not clearly tied to stellar phase; no large abundance abnormality is favored.

Conclusion: Iso-dom spectra likely arise in AGB stars that have undergone third-dredge up (slightly enhancing 29SiO/30SiO abundances) and are experiencing very low turbulence velocity (<1 km/s), enabling efficient maser pumping through line overlap rather than requiring extreme isotopic abundance anomalies.

Abstract: J=1--0 SiO masers at 43 GHz have a well-established distinctive signature in asymptotic giant branch (AGB) stars. 28SiO transitions typically dominate these spectra with the v=1 and v=2 emission being especially prominent and ubiquitous. Several predictions about enhanced 29SiO abundances in exotic stars prompt us to catalog the cases where 29SiO maser emission is enhanced compared to 28SiO. Our purpose is to catalog the known cases of 43 GHz spectra dominated by emission from isotopologue transitions (iso-dom spectra), to explore the commonalities in these sources, and to explain the cause of such maser signatures. Our catalog is drawn from SiO maser line ratios in the infrared-color-selected BAaDE survey and supplemented with a literature detection. The BAaDE catalog has cemented the typical signature of 43 GHz SiO masers, showing it is dominated by the v=1 and v=2 lines. Thirty-five iso-dom spectra are identified, meaning that this signature is seen in about 0.2% of our SiO maser-bearing stars. Their infrared colors are blue compared to other sources of the same period, similar to all sources displaying isotopologue masers. It is clear that the iso-dom nature of sources is variable, but unclear whether this is tied to stellar phase. A large abundance abnormality among the iso-dom sources is disfavored as the iso-dom sources do not appear significantly different from other stars which host isotopologue masers. Maser pumping, affecting the population inversions of specific transitions, can instead explain the enhanced signatures. We posit that iso-dom spectra appear in AGB stars which have undergone third-dredge up (enhancing the 29SiO and 30SiO abundance slightly) and which, in addition, are experiencing very low turbulence velocity (<1 km/s), creating a line overlap which pumps the maser transitions very efficiently.

TL;DR: Using Gaia DR3 photometry, detached eclipsing binaries, and 10,000 Monte Carlo isochrone sets, the authors determine NGC 6791's age to be 8.46±0.66 Gyr, with associated metallicity, helium, distance, and reddening parameters, favoring an inner-Galaxy origin with subsequent outward migration.

Gaia DR3 photometry together with detached eclipsing binaries (DEBs) in NGC 6791 are combined with 10,000 Monte Carlo isochrone sets (marginalizing over uncertainties in composition, convective mixing processes, opacities, diffusion, nuclear reaction-rates, distance modulus, and reddening) to determine the age of NGC 6791. Our error budget shows no single dominant contributor, and highlights differences between open-cluster and globular-cluster age errors. Combined with its super-solar metallicity, our age estimate favors an inner-Galaxy origin for NGC 6791 and subsequent outward migration, provides a benchmark for asteroseismic calibration at high metallicity, and extends the absolute cluster age--metallicity relation to an old, metal-rich open cluster.

Abstract: Altair is an A-type star known to have an appreciably oblate shape owing to its very fast rotation (~300 km/s). Despite of numerous publications on this star, its chemical abundances have been scarcely investigated so far, presumably because of the practical difficulty that spectral lines are considerably broadened by rapid rotation and badly blended with each other. Motivated by this situation, a spectroscopic analysis was conducted to study the photospheric abundances of Altair by using the synthetic spectrum-fitting technique, in order to clarify whether or not any chemical peculiarities exist.
