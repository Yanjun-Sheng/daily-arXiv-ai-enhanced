  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Intrinsic 3D shapes of galaxies are key to understanding their formation and evolution, but they cannot be observed directly: inferring them from projected sizes and axis ratios requires assuming a population distribution for the shape parameters. The validity of the commonly adopted assumptions in Bayesian shape inference for high-redshift galaxies has not been systematically tested, and misspecified priors risk biasing claims of morphological evolution.

Method: The authors analyze FIREboxHR galaxies with log(M*/Msun) = 6.5-9.5 at z ~ 6-15. Intrinsic shapes are measured from stellar particles with the minimum covariance determinant (MCD) estimator. They then generate mock JWST/NIRCam images, measure half-light radii and projected axis ratios, and perform Bayesian forward modeling of the joint distribution of projected axis ratio q and size log a to recover the intrinsic ellipticity E, triaxiality T, and size A distributions.

Result: Low-mass high-redshift galaxies are predominantly prolate and contain a substantial spheroidal component. Intrinsic ellipticity E and triaxiality T are well described by Johnson SB distributions, while intrinsic size A is approximately log-normal; a correlated multivariate normal in (logit(E), logit(T), log A) therefore outperforms a multivariate normal in (E, T, log A), recovering particle-measured distributions more faithfully. Triaxiality-related parameters remain weakly constrained from projected data unless large samples or informative priors are available.

Conclusion: Bayesian forward modeling can recover intrinsic shape distributions only when its population model adequately describes the underlying galaxy population; an unsuitable assumed distribution can bias inferences about morphological evolution at high redshift. The choice of population parametrization is thus a first-order systematic rather than a technical detail.

Abstract: Context. The three-dimensional shapes of galaxies provide clues to their formation and evolution. Inferring intrinsic shapes from projected sizes and axis ratios requires assumptions about the population distribution of shape parameters.
