Motivation: To establish an improved distance to the long-period Cepheid SV Vul and its host cluster Alicante 13, and investigate their possible association with a broader coeval stellar complex.

Method: The analysis uses deeper UKIDSS-DR6 photometry and Gaia DR3 observations for distance determination, along with ultraviolet UVEX color-color analyses, Gaia XP spectroscopically differentially dereddened color-magnitude diagrams, and Padova isochrones for age and membership assessment. Astrometric comparisons (parallaxes, proper motions) were performed across the candidate stellar groups.

Result: A distance of d=2.30±0.13 kpc was derived. SV Vul, Alicante 13, and potentially Liu-Pang 1738 share comparable astrometry (σ_π≈0.02 mas, σ_μδ≈0.06 mas yr⁻¹) and similar turnoff ages. However, SV Vul appears comparatively overluminous relative to the cluster sequence.

Conclusion: The clusters and Cepheid likely belong to a coeval stellar complex based on astrometric and age evidence, but SV Vul's overluminosity raises questions. Robust radial velocity measurements are needed to confirm or refute the physical association hypothesis.

Abstract: A new distance was established to the Cepheid SV Vul ($P\simeq45^{d}$) and its host cluster Alicante 13, and is tied in part to deeper UKIDSS-DR6 photometry and Gaia DR3 observations ($d=2.30\pm0.13$ kpc). SV Vul and Alicante 13 possibly belong to a broader coeval stellar complex, which could include Liu-Pang 1738. The clusters and Cepheid share comparable astrometry (e.g., $σ_π\simeq0.02$ mas, $σ_{μ_δ}\simeq0.06$ mas yr$^{-1}$), and similar cluster turnoffs (ages) exist as indicated by ultraviolet UVEX color-color analyses, Gaia XP spectroscopically differentially dereddened color-magnitude diagrams, and Padova isochrones. Yet SV Vul may be comparatively overluminous. Robust radial velocities for both clusters could substantiate certain hypotheses.
