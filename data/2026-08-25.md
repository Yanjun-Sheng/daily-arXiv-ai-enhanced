### [1] [BSN: Light Curve Modeling and Orbital Period Analysis of the Contact Binaries OV Leo and V339 Leo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20772)
*Razieh Aliakbari,Atila Poro,Raul Michel,Sabrina Baudart*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper performs a combined photometric and orbital period analysis of two W-subtype contact binary systems, OV Leo and V339 Leo, using ground-based observations and TESS photometry. Light curves are modeled with BSN software via MCMC, revealing both systems are shallow-contact binaries; OV Leo shows total eclipses without spots, while V339 Leo shows partial eclipses with an O'Connell effect modeled by a cool starspot. Opposite long-term period variations suggest ongoing conservative mass transfer, and absolute parameters are derived from Gaia parallaxes and standard astrophysical equations.

Motivation: To characterize the physical and orbital properties of two contact binary systems (OV Leo analyzed photometrically for the first time, and V339 Leo reexamined with updated methods) using high-quality TESS photometry combined with ground-based observations, thereby improving our understanding of mass transfer and eclipse geometry in W-subtype contact binaries.

Method: Combines ground-based photometric observations with TESS photometry; light curves are modeled using the BSN software via a Markov Chain Monte Carlo approach for physical parameter estimation with uncertainties. Starspot modeling is applied to reproduce the O'Connell effect. Three-dimensional geometric models are constructed to assess eclipse geometry. Orbital period analysis is performed using eclipse timing extractions, and absolute parameters are derived from Gaia parallaxes combined with photometric solutions and standard astrophysical equations.

Result: Both OV Leo and V339 Leo are identified as W-subtype contact binaries in shallow-contact configurations. The O'Connell effect in V339 Leo is reproduced with a cool starspot model, while OV Leo requires no spot. OV Leo exhibits total eclipses, whereas V339 Leo undergoes partial eclipses. Orbital period analysis reveals opposite long-term period variations, interpreted as ongoing mass transfer under conservative mass-transfer assumptions.

Conclusion: The two contact binary systems show distinct physical properties—differing in eclipse type and spot activity—yet share a W-subtype shallow-contact morphology. The opposite period variations imply mass transfer occurring in opposite directions between the two systems, and the derived absolute parameters provide updated physical constraints for both binaries.

Abstract: We present a combined photometric and orbital period analysis of the contact binary systems OV Leo and V339 Leo using ground-based observations together with TESS photometry. The light curves were modeled with the BSN software through a Markov Chain Monte Carlo approach, providing physical estimates of the system parameters and their uncertainties. OV Leo is analyzed photometrically for the first time, while V339 Leo is reexamined using new observations and an updated modeling methodology. The light curve analysis show that both targets are W-subtype contact binaries in shallow-contact configurations. The O'Connell effect detected in V339 Leo is reproduced with a cool starspot model, whereas no spot is required for OV Leo. Three-dimensional geometric models further indicate that OV Leo undergoes total eclipses, while V339 Leo exhibits partial eclipses. Orbital period analysis based on eclipse timing extractions reveals opposite long-term period variations in the two systems, suggesting ongoing mass transfer under the assumption of conservative mass transfer. The absolute parameters were derived using the Gaia parallaxes, the photometric light curve solution, and standard astrophysical equations.
