Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: High-resolution NOEMA observations (~100 au at 1.3mm) of the high-mass protostar CepA HW2 reveal a fragmented accretion disk that is almost edge-on, hosts at least a binary/multiple system driving multiple outflows, and where sub-structures are consistent with disk fragments per Toomre Q analysis and 3D radiation hydrodynamic simulations.

Motivation: Physical properties and fragmentation of accretion disks around high-mass protostars remain poorly constrained due to observational limitations.

Method: NOEMA long baseline array (~1700m) at ≤0.2″ or ≤100 au resolution at 1.3mm in dust continuum and spectral line emission; Toomre Q stability analysis based on CH₃CN and continuum data; comparison to 3D radiation hydrodynamic simulations.

Result: (1) Mm continuum resolves the disk into several sub-structures consistent with fragments in an almost edge-on disk. (2) CO and SiO reveal a second bipolar outflow (in addition to the known jet) from the central peak, indicating at least a binary/multiple system. (3) CH₃CN shows outflow contamination, complicating kinematic analysis of the disk.

Conclusion: CepA HW2 contains a multiply fragmented disk that drives several outflows, supporting the picture that high-mass star formation involves multiplicity and fragmentation at the smallest spatial scales of inner accretion disks.

Abstract: Context: Although there has been significant progress, the physical properties and potential fragmentation of accretion disks around high-mass protostars remain poorly constrained. Aims: We characterize at high angular resolution one of the most nearby (~700pc) high-mass accretion disk candidates CepA HW2. Methods: Using the new long baseline array configuration (~1700m) of the Northern Extended Millimeter Array (NOEMA), we study CepA HW2 with a resolution of <=0.2'' or <=100au at 1.3mm in dust continuum and spectral line emission. Results: The mm continuum emission resolves the central disk candidate into several sub-structures. Conducting a Toomre Q stability analysis based on CH_3CN and continuum data, and a comparison to 3D radiation hydrodynamic simulations shows that the data are consistent with an almost edge-on disk where the observed sub-structures may represent fragments within the disk. The CO and SiO spectral line data confirm a second bipolar outflow (in addition to the well-known jet) emanating from the central peak position. This indicates that this central peak should host at least a binary if not even a higher order multiple system. The usually assumed dense gas tracer CH_3CN shows also contributions from the outflows which complicates further kinematic analysis of the disk. Conclusions: The high-resolution outflow-disk data of CepA reveal a multiply fragmented disk that drives several outflows. These observations enforce the picture of high-mass star formation where multiplicity and fragmentation can happen on the smallest spatial scales related to the inner accretion disks.
