### [1] [Study of the 1/f spectrum using virtual spacecraft sampling in MHD turbulence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27728)
*Maia Brodiano,Facundo Pugliese,Nahuel Andrés,Pablo Dmitruk*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper investigates the low-frequency 1/f magnetic spectrum in 3D MHD turbulence through simulations and virtual spacecraft sampling, finding that the clarity of the 1/f range depends on guide field strength, probe speed, and sampling angle relative to the magnetic field.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To determine how measured temporal 1/f spectra in MHD turbulence depend on the mean magnetic guide field, probe velocity relative to Alfvén speed, and sampling angle, in order to better interpret low-frequency in situ measurements in the solar wind.

Method: Direct numerical simulations (DNS) of three-dimensional incompressible MHD turbulence combined with virtual spacecraft sampling to analyze temporal spectra under varying conditions of guide field strength, probe speed, and sampling angle. A quantitative score was introduced to measure the quality and spectral coverage of detected 1/f intervals.

Result: The clearest 1/f ranges are obtained with stronger guide fields and faster sampler trajectories oriented more perpendicular to the mean magnetic field. Higher probe speeds yield temporal spectra more consistent with direct spatial-to-temporal mapping, especially for nearly perpendicular sampling in strong guide fields.

Conclusion: The presence and clarity of a temporal 1/f range depend on both the underlying turbulent dynamics and the geometry and speed of the sampling process, with important implications for interpreting low-frequency in situ solar wind measurements.

Abstract: We investigate the appearance of a low-frequency 1/f magnetic spectrum in three-dimensional incompressible magnetohydrodynamic turbulence using direct numerical simulations and virtual spacecraft sampling. Our goal is to determine how the measured temporal spectra depend on the mean magnetic guide field, the probe velocity relative to the Alfvén speed, and the sampling angle with respect to the guide field. We find that the clearest 1/f ranges are obtained for stronger guide fields and are favored by faster sampler trajectories oriented more nearly perpendicular to the mean magnetic field. To characterize this behavior, we introduce a quantitative score that measures the quality and spectral coverage of the detected 1/f interval. We further show that, as the probe speed increases, the measured temporal spectra become progressively more consistent with a direct mapping between spatial and temporal fluctuations, particularly for nearly perpendicular sampling in the strong guide field case. These results indicate that the presence and clarity of a temporal 1/f range depend not only on the underlying turbulent dynamics, but also on the geometry and speed of the sampling process, with implications for the interpretation of low-frequency in situ measurements in the solar wind.

### [2] [Comparing explodability predictions from a parameter-optimized semi-analytic model with structure-based progenitor criteria](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27867)

The 1D model reproduces 3D trends with physically meaningful parameters, offering a more direct way to understand explodability physics than structure-based criteria, while clarifying the strengths and limitations of the latter.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>

Method: A parameter-optimized semi-analytic 1D explosion model (based on Müller et al. 2016) is calibrated to the 3D reference results of Burrows et al. (2024). The model's explodability predictions are compared with structure-based criteria: compactness, free-fall mass coordinate, and the μ4-M4 two-parameter criterion.

Result: The semi-analytic 1D model reproduces trends seen in the 3D calibration set by adjusting physically meaningful parameters. It agrees with structure-based criteria in some regimes but differs in others, and the differences can be explained by underlying physical trends.

Conclusion: The semi-analytic model provides a more direct way to examine the physics controlling explodability than traditional structure-based criteria. Abstract: Three-dimensional (3D) simulations of neutrino-driven core-collapse supernovae are among the most reliable tools for predicting explosion outcome. However, their high computational cost limits systematic surveys over large progenitor samples. We test how well a fast one-dimensional (1D) approach captures progenitor explodability. We use a parameter-optimized semi-analytic 1D explosion model based on Müller et al. (2016), calibrated to the 3D results of Burrows et al. (2024) as an adopted reference set. We compare the model's explodability predictions with commonly used structure-based criteria: compactness, the free-fall mass coordinate, and the two-parameter $μ_4$-$M_4$ criterion. Our analysis shows that the semi-analytic model can reproduce the trends seen in this adopted 3D calibration set by adjusting physically meaningful parameters. This provides a more direct way to examine the physics that controls explodability than traditional structure-based criteria. We identify where the semi-analytic model agrees with these criteria, where it differs, and which physical trends explain the differences. This work clarifies the strengths and limitations of structure-based explodability criteria by evaluating them against a parameter-optimized, neutrino-driven semi-analytic model.

TL;DR: This paper reports the first definitive detection of a magnetic field on the Herbig Ae/Be star HD179218, constrains its dipole geometry (obliquity β=79.9±0.7°, dipole strength Bd=2142±52 G, rotation period Prot=1.34102 d), and presents the first magnetosphere snapshot for this star — making it only the second Herbig Ae/Be star (after HD190073) with a characterized magnetic field geometry.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Understanding the magnetic field geometry of Herbig Ae/Be stars is crucial for explaining how magnetospheric accretion operates in these intermediate-mass pre-main-sequence stars. Only two such stars (HD101412 and HD190073) had previously characterized field geometries, motivating the need to study additional objects like HD179218.

Method: The authors used high-resolution spectropolarimetric observations (both newly acquired and archival) of HD179218. The mean longitudinal magnetic field ⟨B_z⟩ was measured via least-squares deconvolution (LSD) of Stokes V spectra. Spectral variability of hydrogen lines (Hα, Hβ) was studied using dynamical spectra. Rotation period was refined, and the magnetic geometry (obliquity β, dipole strength Bd) was modeled from the ⟨B_z⟩ variation.

The ⟨B_z⟩ variation is best fitted by a sine wave plus its first harmonic. The strongest Hα and Hβ emission in 2025 medium-resolution spectra occurs near phases of best magnetic pole visibility.

Conclusion: HD179218 becomes the second Herbig Ae/Be star (after HD190073) for which a first snapshot of a magnetosphere is presented, advancing the understanding of magnetospheric accretion in these stars. However, more spectropolarimetric data are needed to mitigate limitations from measurement precision and uneven rotational phase coverage.

Abstract: While about two dozen Herbig Ae/Be stars have been reported to be magnetic, only two of them, HD101412 and HD190073, have had their magnetic field geometries studied in the past. The knowledge of the magnetic field structure is important to understanding how magnetospheric accretion works in these stars. We aim to study in detail the spectral and magnetic variability of HD179218, which is necessary to put constraints on its magnetic field geometry. We measured the mean longitudinal magnetic field, $\langle B_{\rm z}\rangle$, from newly acquired and archival high-resolution spectropolarimetric observations of HD179218 using the least-squares deconvolution technique. Additionally, we studied the spectral variability of the hydrogen lines using dynamical spectra. Based on our analysis of the Stokes V spectra of HD179218, we report for the first time the definite detection of a magnetic field. Using a slightly refined rotation period of P$_{rot}$ = 1.34102 d, we constrained its geometry as follows: an estimated magnetic obliquity angle of $β$= 79.9 $\pm$ 0.7 deg and a dipole strength of B$_d$ = 2142 $\pm$ 52 G. The $\langle B_{\rm z}\rangle$ variation is best fitted by the superposition of a sine wave and of its first harmonic, but more spectropolarimetric observations are necessary to test the impact of the limited measurement precision and the uneven coverage of the rotation cycle. The strongest emission in the H$α$ and H$β$ line profiles in the medium-resolution spectra acquired in 2025 was detected close to the phases of the best visibility of the magnetic poles. HD179218 is the second Herbig Ae/Be star after HD190073 for which a first snapshot of a magnetosphere is presented.

### [4] [Evolution of the dusty nova QY Mus from eruption to quiescence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28771)

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: QY Mus is a slow, dust-forming classical nova (t₂=87 days) with a D-type light curve, Fe II spectral classification, and nebular transition at ~233 days. Photoionization modeling reveals a hot central source (~7×10⁵ K) with enhanced N, O, and moderate Ne, but not a neon nova. Mid-IR dust at ~400 K and a subgiant secondary are identified.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To comprehensively characterize the spectrophotometric evolution of QY Mus from outburst to quiescence, including dust formation timing, spectral classification, elemental abundances, and the nature of the secondary star.

Method: Light curve analysis (MMRD distance, t₂ classification), optical spectroscopy (94–1348 days), photoionization modeling with Cloudy (41 emission lines on day 590), mid-infrared WISE photometry, and Gaia-based color-magnitude diagram analysis for 34 quiescent novae.

Result: Dust formation at ~123 days (τ~3.2); t₂=87±6 days; M_V=-6.55±0.54; Fe II-type; nebular phase at ~233 days; central source T_eff=(7.08±0.20)×10⁵ K; enhanced N, O, moderate Ne; cool dust at ~400 K; secondary is a subgiant.

Abstract: We present a comprehensive study of the spectrophotometric evolution of the classical nova QY Mus from eruption to quiescence. The light curve shows a notable dust dip, classifying it as a D (137)-type nova, with dust formation beginning at $\sim$123 days post-outburst and reaching a maximum optical depth of $τ\sim 3.2$. We classify QY Mus as a slow nova with $t_2 = 87 \pm 6$ days, and derive an absolute magnitude of $M_V = -6.55 \pm 0.54$ using the MMRD relation. The spectroscopic evolution, traced from 94 to 1348 days, shows prominent P-Cygni profiles in Balmer and Fe II lines during the early decline, consistent with an Fe II-type nova. The transition to the nebular phase occurs around $\sim$233 days, marked by the emergence of [O III] emission. Photoionization modeling using \textsc{Cloudy} of 41 emission lines on day 590 yields a central source temperature of $(7.08 \pm 0.20)\times10^{5}$ K, with enhanced nitrogen and oxygen abundances and moderate neon enrichment, suggesting that QY Mus is not a neon nova. Mid-infrared WISE observations at $\sim$502 days indicate the presence of cool dust at $\sim$400 K. Using a Gaia-based color magnitude diagram constructed in this work for 34 quiescent novae, we find that QY Mus occupies a region consistent with systems hosting main-sequence or subgiant secondaries; its orbital period further supports a subgiant companion. These results establish QY Mus as a slow, dust-forming nova with well-characterized evolution and a subgiant secondary.

### [6] [Isochrone Fitting of Galactic Globular Clusters -- VIII. Homogeneous estimates of parameters for 27 clusters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28505)
*G. A. Gontcharov,O. S. Ryutina,C. J. Bonatto,S. S. Savchenko,S. V. Troitsky*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Using Stetson, HST, and Gaia DR3 data for 22 Galactic globular clusters, we estimate metallicity, age, distance, reddening, and identify variable stars. Combined with 5 earlier clusters (27 total), we study parameter relationships. The $R$-parameter (HB/RGB ratio) is measured with high precision. Accreted clusters reject constant $R$; in-situ clusters yield $R=1.31^{+0.06}_{-0.05}$, implying possible axion-photon coupling $g_{aγ}=(0.66^{+0.11}_{-0.13})\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$. Peripheral HB depletion suggests disk-crossing effects with ~60-80 Myr recovery.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To derive homogeneous parameters for 27 Galactic globular clusters, estimate the $R$-parameter (HB/RGB ratio) with unprecedented accuracy, and investigate its relation to cluster properties, including implications for axion physics and dynamical evolution.

Method: Select members and fit CMDs with DSED and BaSTI isochrones ([α/Fe]=+0.4, adopted Y) using Stetson, HST, and Gaia DR3 data for 22 clusters; identify variable stars; combine with 5 earlier clusters; count giants on RGB, HB, and AGB with clean cross-identified data covering entire cluster fields.

Result: Accreted clusters show strong $R$-parameter dependencies on other parameters, rejecting constant $R$. In-situ clusters are consistent with constant $R=1.31^{+0.06}_{-0.05}$, which may imply axion-photon coupling $g_{aγ}=(0.66^{+0.11}_{-0.13})\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$. Comparing HST (centres) and Gaia (peripheries) suggests peripheral HB depletion from Galactic disk crossing with ~60-80 Myr recovery.

Conclusion: The $R$-parameter is not universal across all globular clusters; in-situ clusters support a constant value with possible axion implications, while accreted clusters show complex dependencies needing further theoretical study. Dynamical effects from disk crossing affect HB populations in cluster peripheries.

Abstract: We use the Stetson, HST, and Gaia DR3 data sets for 22 Galactic globular clusters to select their members and fit their CMDs with isochrones from DSED and BaSTI for $[α/$Fe$]=+0.4$ and an adopted helium mass fraction $Y$. As a result, we estimate the metallicity [Fe/H], age, distance from the Sun, and reddening E(B-V) for these clusters. Special attention is paid to identify variable stars among the cluster members. We combine these results with our earlier estimates for five other clusters into a homogeneous set of parameters for 27 clusters and investigate relationships among these parameters and their statistical properties. In particular, we count the giants on the red (RGB), horizontal (HB), and asymptotic branches using the clean data sets cross-identified to cover the entire cluster fields and count each giant only ones. This allows us to calculate the $R$-parameter, the ratio between the numbers of the HB and RGB, with unprecedented accuracy and to examine its relations with other parameters. These relations are much stronger for accreted clusters rejecting a constant $R$ value for them, possibly because of their heterogeneous origins and environments. For all in-situ clusters, a constant value of $R=1.31^{+0.06}_{-0.05}$ is consistent with the data. According to previous calculations, this value may imply anomalous energy losses in HB stars corresponding to an axion--photon coupling of $g_{aγ}=(0.66^{+0.11}_{-0.13})\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$. However, the observed dependencies of $R$ on cluster parameters require improved statistics and a theoretical understanding of these dependencies. By comparing $R$-parameter estimates from HST and Gaia in cluster centres and peripheries, respectively, we suggest that the peripheral HB population is depleted by about half when the cluster crosses the Galactic disk, then the HB recovers over 60-80 Myr.
