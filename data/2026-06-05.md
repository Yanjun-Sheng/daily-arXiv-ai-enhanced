### [1] [Hot Degenerate Components in Blue Stragglers: A Multi-Wavelength SED Analysis of Nine Open Clusters with Swift/UVOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05321)
*Deniz Cennet Çınar,D. Bisht,Selçuk Bilir,Songmei Qin,Leila Saker*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The paper analyzes 35 blue straggler star candidates in nine open clusters using multi-wavelength data from Swift/UVOT, Gaia DR3, and optical-to-infrared photometry. About 43% show ultraviolet excess indicative of hot companions (white dwarfs or pre-ELM white dwarfs), supporting a binary evolution origin. The study also finds mass segregation, low-eccentricity disk-like orbits, and a positive relation between BSS spatial extent and population size.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To identify signatures of hot companions around blue straggler stars (BSSs) in open clusters via multi-wavelength SED fitting, in order to constrain the binary mass transfer formation channel and post-mass-transfer evolutionary phases.

Method: Homogeneous multi-wavelength analysis combining Swift/UVOT near-UV data, Gaia DR3 astrometry, and optical-to-infrared photometry; two-component SED fitting to detect ultraviolet excess; radial distribution and mass segregation analysis using half-mass relaxation timescales; Galactic orbit computation via galpy.

Result: 15 out of 35 BSSs (~43%) show UV excess consistent with two-component SEDs; companions are hot white dwarfs and pre-ELM white dwarf candidates; evidence of mass segregation; low orbital eccentricities (e ≤ 0.1) and disk-like orbits; positive correlation between BSS half-number radius (r50) and total BSS count.

Conclusion: The results support a binary evolution origin for the BSS population in these open clusters. The identified systems provide observational constraints on post-mass-transfer phases, though limited detections and SED degeneracies remain, warranting future spectroscopic confirmation.

Abstract: We present a homogeneous multi-wavelength analysis of 35 blue straggler star (BSS) candidates in nine open clusters, combining Swift/UVOT near-ultraviolet data with Gaia DR3 astrometry and optical-to-infrared photometry. We construct spectral energy distributions (SEDs) to search for signatures of hot companions associated with past mass transfer. Among the sample, 15 BSSs (~43%) show ultraviolet excesses that are better described by two-component SED fits. The inferred companions are consistent with hot white dwarfs and pre-extremely low-mass (pre-ELM) white dwarf candidates, suggesting systems observed at different stages following mass transfer. We examine the radial distribution of the BSSs and find evidence for mass segregation in dynamically evolved clusters, a result that is broadly consistent with the estimated half-mass relaxation timescales of the host systems. To place the clusters in a Galactic context, we compute their orbits using galpy, obtaining low eccentricities (e <= 0.1) and disk-like trajectories. We also find a positive relation between the half-number radius of the BSS population (r50) and the total number of BSSs. Overall, our results are consistent with a scenario in which the BSS population in these clusters is dominated by binary evolution. The systems identified here provide observational constraints on post-mass-transfer evolutionary phases. While the number of robust detections is limited and intrinsic degeneracies remain in SED-based decomposition, these results provide a useful foundation for future spectroscopic confirmation.

### [2] [Metal-Poor Gas Accretion Drives Giant Clump Formation at 0.6 < z < 2.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05284)
*Visal Sok,Adam Muzzin,Ben Forrest,Gillian Wilson,Jialu Chen,Vivian Yun Yan Tan,Sunna Withers,Roberto Abraham,Maruša Bradač,Vicente Estrada-Carpenter,Kartheik G. Iyer,Nicholas S. Martis,Gaël Noirot,Ghassan T. E. Sarrouh,Marcin Sawicki,Chris J. Willott,Aurélien Henry,Naadiyah Jagga,Danilo Marchesini,Ian McConachie,Katherine Myers,Nelson Nunes,Luke Robbins*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Analysis of ~300 star-forming galaxies at z=0.6-2.6 shows clumpy galaxies have lower gas-phase metallicities than the mass-metallicity relation, with their clumps being younger and having higher SFRs, suggesting clumpy structure is linked to metal-poor gas accretion rather than mergers.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how kiloparsec-scale clumps assemble in high-redshift star-forming galaxies by investigating chemical abundance patterns, as metallicity offsets in clumpy galaxies provide constraints on assembly mechanisms.

Method: Analyzed gas-phase metallicities of ~300 SFGs at 0.6<z<2.6 using LEGA-C, MOSDEF, and CANUCS NIR spectroscopy; examined resolved stellar properties of clumps vs. integrated metallicity via CANUCS; computed merger statistics using Gini-M20 morphological parameters.

Result: Clumpy galaxies have lower metallicities relative to the mass-metallicity relation; nonclumpy galaxies have higher metallicities. Clumps in low-metallicity (ΔZ≤0) hosts are younger and have higher SFRs but no significant mass difference. Most clumpy galaxies are not classified as mergers.

Conclusion: The clumpy nature of cosmic noon galaxies is linked to metal-poor gas accretion events that trigger star formation and dilute metallicities, rather than merger-driven processes.

Abstract: The physical properties of kiloparsec-scale clumps in high-redshift star-forming galaxies (SFGs) contain crucial constraints on how they assemble. Building on recent work that indicates the presence of a metallicity offset in clumpy galaxies compared to nonclumpy SFGs, we analyze the chemical abundance in a large sample of ${\sim}300$ SFGs between $0.6<z<2.6$ using LEGA-C, MOSDEF and CANUCS near-infrared spectroscopic observations. We find that clumpy galaxies generally have lower gas-phase metallcities compared to the mass-metallicity relation, while nonclumpy galaxies have higher metallicities. We further investigate the relationship between the resolved stellar properties of clumps and the integrated gas-phase metallicity of the host galaxies using a subset of galaxies observed in the CANUCS fields. In particular, clumps in SFGs with metallicity below the mass-metallicity relation (i.e., $ΔZ \leq 0$) are generally younger and have higher SFRs, compared to clumps whose host galaxies have $ΔZ > 0$. We do not find a significant mass difference between these two clump populations. Finally, we compute the merger statistic using the Gini-M20 morphological parameters and find that the majority of clumpy galaxies are not classified as mergers based on their stellar mass maps. The results suggest that the clumpy nature of cosmic noon galaxies is linked to metal-poor gas accretion events that trigger star formation and dilute metallicities.

### [3] [Dynamical models of cluster members to probe the total mass properties of cluster subhalos. I. A comparison with parametric strong lensing models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05298)
*Nicola Bianchetti,Claudio Grillo,Giovanni Granata,Pietro Bergamini,Massimo Meneghetti,Amata Mercurio,Piero Rosati,Eros Vanzella,Gabriel B. Caminha*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper presents dynamical models of early-type cluster members in two strong lensing galaxy clusters (Abell 2744 and MACS J0416.1-2403) using MUSE spectroscopic data. By measuring LOS velocity dispersion profiles of 109 galaxies and modeling them with dPIE and Jaffe mass distributions, the authors recover central stellar velocity dispersion (σ₀) and truncation radius (rₜ). Their dynamical models fit the data better than SL models, and they calibrate Faber-Jackson scaling relations with higher normalization than previous works, concluding their measurements are more robust and should inform future SL models.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To independently probe the persistent discrepancy between strong lensing models and cosmological simulations regarding total mass properties of cluster subhalos.

Method: Used MUSE spectroscopic data with HFF photometry; applied a spectral-fitting pipeline for kinematic measurements of LOS velocity dispersion profiles of 109 cluster members; modeled galaxies with dPIE total mass density and Jaffe stellar mass density distributions; calibrated Faber-Jackson scaling relations.

Result: σ₀ is accurately recovered for all members; rₜ is reliably measured only for galaxies with sufficient radial kinematic coverage; dynamical models fit LOS velocity dispersion profiles better than SL models; Faber-Jackson relations show systematically higher normalization but compatible slope and scatter.

Conclusion: Dynamical measurements of σ₀ and rₜ and the calibrated scaling relations are more robust than previous kinematic estimates (which were biased by PSF effects) and should be adopted as improved initial prescriptions in future SL models.

Abstract: In this series of papers, we present dynamical models of cluster members in strong lensing (SL) galaxy clusters to independently probe the persistent discrepancy reported between SL models and cosmological simulations, in terms of total mass properties for the cluster subhalos. In this work, we focused our study on early-type galaxies within Abell 2744 ($z=0.309$) and MACS J0416.1-2403 ($z=0.397$). We took advantage of deep MUSE spectroscopic data, complemented with HFF photometry. We used a pipeline based on spectral fitting to perform kinematic measurements of the LOS velocity dispersion profiles of 109 cluster members. We modeled the galaxies assuming a dPIE total mass density distribution and a Jaffe stellar mass density distribution. From the models, we inferred the values of the central stellar velocity dispersion, $σ_0$, and the truncation radius, $r_t$, for the galaxies in our sample. We found that $σ_0$ is accurately recovered for all of the cluster members, while $r_t$ is reliably measured for a fraction of galaxies in our sample, with sufficiently extended radial kinematic coverage. Our dynamical models predicted LOS velocity dispersion profiles that fit the measured ones better than those inferred from SL models. We then exploited the $σ_0$ measurements obtained from the dynamical models to calibrate the Faber-Jackson scaling relations for the cluster members in both galaxy clusters. When comparing our relations to those obtained in previous kinematics and SL works, we found systematically higher normalization and compatible slope and scatter values. We conclude that our dynamical measurements of $σ_0$ and $r_t$, along with calibrated scaling relations, are more robust than previous kinematic estimates which are biased by not taking into account the effects of the PSF, and should therefore be adopted as improved initial prescriptions in future SL models.

TL;DR: The study quantifies ionizing photon production at z~7 using ultra-deep JWST UNCOVER observations of strong [OIII]+Hβ emitters in Abell 2744. They identify a population of young, metal-poor, dust-poor galaxies with extreme rest-frame EW>740 Å. Correcting for spectroscopic confirmation, these emitters constitute 56±12% of the UV-selected population and their ionizing budget (log(ṅion)=50.63±0.05) accounts for ~70% of the total required for reionization, establishing [OIII]+Hβ selection as a powerful dust-insensitive probe.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To quantify the contribution of extreme [OIII]+Hβ emitters to the ionizing photon budget at z~7 and assess whether known galaxy populations can power cosmic reionization, using deeper observations than previously possible.

Method: Ultra-deep JWST UNCOVER F410M imaging (5σ~29 AB) + gravitational lensing in Abell 2744 to reach log(L)=41.3 erg/s; rest-frame optical emission-line selection of high-EW (>740 Å) sources; NIRSpec follow-up of a representative subset (72% confirmation, 18 emitters); Balmer decrements for dust; strong-line diagnostics for metallicity; [OII]/[OIII] ratios to infer Lyman continuum escape fraction (fesc~20%).

Abstract: We quantify the ionizing photon production at $z\sim7$ using the deepest spectroscopically confirmed sample of strong [OIII]$+$H$β$ emitters (rest-frame EW$>740$ A) in the Abell 2744 field. Leveraging ultra-deep UNCOVER F410M imaging ($5σ\sim29$ AB) and gravitational lensing, we probe an order of magnitude deeper than previous JWST WFSS [OIII] studies, reaching a luminosity limit of $\log(L_{\rm{[OIII]}+\rm{H}β}/\text{erg s}^{-1})=41.3$. Our rest-frame optical emission-line selection probes some of the youngest, metal- and dust-poor galaxies, identifying a large population of continuum-faint, ionizing candidates. NIRSpec follow-up of a luminosity-representative subset confirms $72\%$ of targets, providing detailed characterization of 18 emitters. Correcting for the spectroscopic confirmation rate, we find that these high-EW emitters represent $56\pm12\%$ of the total UV-selected population by number density. Integrated to our survey limits, the ionizing budget of these emitters ($\log(\dot{N}_{\rm ion}/{\rm s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-3})=50.63\pm0.05$) accounts for $\sim70\%$ of the total budget required for reionization at $z\sim7$. This result is consistent with empirical benchmarks. These results establish [OIII]$+$H$β$ selection as a powerful, dust-insensitive probe, showing that known galaxy populations significantly power reionization.

### [5] [Bulk vs. turbulent motions at the centres of galaxy clusters: AGN-driven turbulence according to TNG-Cluster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05355)
*Bipradeep Saha,Annalisa Pillepich,Joey Braspenning,Marine Prunier,Dimitris Chatzigiannakis,Dylan Nelson*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Analysis of 352 galaxy clusters from TNG-Cluster simulations reveals that ICM turbulence is mostly subsonic with broad distributions, turbulent velocity dispersions of 50-75 km/s in cores, and SMBH feedback is a key driver of core turbulence, with implications for interpreting XRISM observations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Quantifying non-thermal motions (bulk and turbulent) in the intracluster medium (ICM) is essential for understanding cluster thermodynamic evolution, key physical processes, and interpreting high spectral-resolution X-ray observations from XRISM.

Method: Multi-scale filtering Reynolds decomposition applied to 352 galaxy clusters at z=0 (M_200c = 10^14.3–10^15.4 M_sun) from TNG-Cluster magneto-hydrodynamical simulations, separating total gas velocities into bulk (coherent) and turbulent (small-scale fluctuations) components, focusing on hot X-ray emitting gas in central core regions.

Result: ICM has subsonic turbulence but with broad velocity distributions up to M_Turb~10 and large cluster-to-cluster variations. Turbulence contributes <50% of total velocity dispersion in most cluster centres, with typical turbulent velocity dispersions of 50-75 km/s and sub-percent turbulent pressure support. Strong cool cores, X-ray cavities, or recent SMBH feedback show larger turbulent dispersions. Turbulent velocity dispersion peaks in centres, dips at 0.1-0.2 R_500c, then rises.

Conclusion: SMBH feedback is a key driver of turbulence in cluster cores, generating strong but short-lived motion with high-velocity outflows. Results call for caution when interpreting XRISM observations.

Abstract: The highly dynamic intracluster medium (ICM) influences cluster thermodynamic evolution and probes key physical processes. Quantifying the non-thermal motions is therefore essential for understanding cluster physics and interpreting high spectral-resolution X-ray observations from telescopes like {\it XRISM}.
  We quantify bulk and turbulent gas motions in 352 galaxy clusters at $z=0$ (${\rm M_{200c}=10^{14.3-15.4}\, M_\odot}$) from the TNG-Cluster suite of magneto-hydrodynamical galaxy simulations. We use a multi-scale filtering Reynolds decomposition to separate total gas velocities into bulk (coherent) and turbulent (small-scale fluctuations) components. We primarily focus on the hot X-ray emitting gas in the central core regions.
  According to TNG-Cluster, majority of the ICM has subsonic turbulence but with broad velocity distributions reaching $\mathcal{M}_{\rm Turb}\sim 10$ and large cluster-to-cluster variations. In cluster centres, turbulence contributes less than half of the total velocity dispersion $(σ_{v\rm,Turb } \sim 0.5 ~σ_{v,\rm Total})$ for most clusters, with typical turbulent velocity dispersions of $50-75$ km s$^{-1}$ across the mass range, and with sub per cent levels of turbulent pressure support. Clusters that are strong cool cores, or have X-ray cavities, or experienced recent SMBH feedback energy injections exhibit systematically larger turbulent velocity dispersions and more prominent turbulent velocity tails. On average, the turbulent velocity dispersion peaks in cluster centres, decreases slightly to a minimum at $0.1-0.2 \, R_{\rm500c}$, then rises again.
  Our analysis shows that SMBH feedback is a key driver of turbulence in cluster cores, generating strong but short-lived motion alongside high-velocity outflows. It also calls for caution for interpreting {\it XRISM} observations.

Motivation: To determine the binary fraction of Wolf-Rayet stars in the Galactic center region through long-baseline kinematic monitoring, as binarity affects stellar evolution, mass loss, and the production of gravitational wave progenitors.

Method: Radial velocity (v_z) measurements from Keck Observatory (primary) and Gemini North Observatory (supplementary) over 1994-2024, combined with literature v_z data, to detect velocity variations indicative of binarity among 27 WR stars within 0.5 pc of the Galactic center.

Result: 5 out of 27 WR stars show v_z variations suggesting companions: 2 newly identified (IRS 13E4, S8-181) and 3 previously known (IRS 16SW, IRS 16NE, S4-258). The inferred binary fraction is 0.56±0.18; combining with previous photometric work yields 0.69±0.17.

Abstract: We report the binary fraction of Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars within 0.5~pc of the Galactic center obtained through the longest time-baseline (1994-2024) kinematic study of this population of stars. The new radial velocity ($v_{z}$) data we present is primarily from the W. M. Keck Observatory, with additional $v_{z}$ measurements from Gemini North Observatory. When combining our new $v_{z}$ measurements with literature measurements, we find $v_{z}$ variations suggesting the presence of a companion for five out of 27 WR stars, of which two are newly identified here (IRS~13E4, S8-181), along with three previously detected binaries (IRS~16SW, IRS~16NE, S4-258). Based on our experimental sensitivity and expected properties of the underlying population, we infer the binary fraction of the WR stars in the Galactic center to be 0.56$\pm$0.18. This is consistent with previous photometric studies of the young stars in the Galactic center, and with the binary fraction of field WR stars. When our results are combined with the results of previous photometric work, we find a binary fraction of 0.69$\pm$0.17 for the WR stars in the Galactic center.
