### [1] [Search for magnetic fields in seven slowly rotating A stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28258)
*Pablo de Frutos Rull,Coralie Neiner,Jonathan Labadie-Bartz,Frédéric Royer,Richard Monier,Keegan Thomson-Paressant*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The paper evaluates the reliability of indirect indicators for identifying magnetic A-type stars by magnetically characterizing seven slowly rotating, chemically peculiar stars selected via two complementary strategies. Magnetic fields were detected in all four stars selected via the 5200 Å depression plus TESS rotational modulation (145–2900 G), while none of the three stars selected via abundance analysis and slow rotation showed a Zeeman signature. The authors conclude that combining the 5200 Å depression with stable rotational modulation is a highly efficient selection strategy for future spectropolarimetric surveys, whereas slow rotation with chemical peculiarity alone is unreliable.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: A small fraction of A-type stars host strong fossil magnetic fields, but identifying them relies on indirect indicators (chemical peculiarities, slow rotation, rotational modulation) that are also shared by non-magnetic stars. Assessing the reliability of these indicators is essential for designing future large-scale spectropolarimetric surveys.

Method: Seven slowly rotating, chemically peculiar A-type stars were selected through two complementary strategies: (1) three from abundance analysis combined with potential intrinsically slow rotation, and (2) four from a spectral depression at 5200 Å combined with rotational modulation in TESS photometry. The authors obtained high-resolution spectropolarimetry, applied least squares deconvolution (LSD), and measured longitudinal field strengths for detections. For non-detections, they derived upper limits on polar field strength and evaluated the critical fields of Zahn and Spruit to distinguish magnetic from non-magnetic stars.

Result: Magnetic fields were detected in all four stars selected via the spectral depression and photometric criterion, with longitudinal field strengths of approximately 145–2900 G. None of the three stars selected via abundance and slow rotation showed a Zeeman signature. Combined upper limits for two non-detections lie below or near the magnetic desert boundary, but only one is also below the critical fields required for rigid rotation. The non-detections are most naturally interpreted as Am stars. Among the detections, HD 63843 stands out as a magnetic δ Scuti pulsator, promising for magneto-asteroseismology.

Conclusion: Slow rotation combined with chemical peculiarity alone does not reliably trace large-scale magnetic fields. Adding the 5200 Å depression and stable rotational modulation in space photometry appears to select magnetic A-type stars with high efficiency, providing a practical guideline for future spectropolarimetric surveys.

Abstract: A small fraction of A-type stars host strong fossil magnetic fields. Identifying them relies on indirect indicators such as chemical peculiarities, slow rotation, and rotational modulation, which are also shared by non-magnetic stars. Assessing their reliability is essential for future surveys. We magnetically characterise seven slowly rotating, chemically peculiar A-type stars selected through two complementary strategies. Three were selected from abundance analysis combined with potential intrinsically slow rotation, and four from a spectral depression at 5200 Angstrom combined with rotational modulation in TESS photometry. We obtained high-resolution spectropolarimetry, applied least squares deconvolution, and measured longitudinal field strengths for detections. For non-detections, we derived upper limits on polar field strength and evaluated the critical fields of Zahn and Spruit. We detect magnetic fields in all four stars selected via the spectral depression and photometric criterion, with longitudinal field strengths of approximately 145-2900 G, while none of the three stars selected via abundance and slow rotation shows a Zeeman signature. Combined upper limits for two non-detections lie below or near the magnetic desert boundary, but only one is also below the critical fields required for rigid rotation. The non-detections are most naturally interpreted as Am stars. Among the detections, HD 63843 stands out as a magnetic $δ$ Scuti pulsator, promising for magneto-asteroseismology. In conclusion, slow rotation combined with chemical peculiarity alone does not reliably trace large scale magnetic fields, whereas adding the 5200 Angstrom depression and stable rotational modulation in space photometry appears to select magnetic A-type stars with high efficiency, providing a practical guideline for future spectropolarimetric surveys.

### [2] [Probing magnetic fields in stars: A 2D oscillation framework including rotation and magnetism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28290)
*Antoine Fort,Rhita-Maria Ouazzani,Lucas Barrault,Louis Manchon,Ludovic Petitdemange*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper presents a new modular implementation of a 2D oscillation code (ACOR) that incorporates both stellar rotation and magnetic fields, validated against perturbative predictions and the TARM approach, demonstrating that internal magnetic fields leave detectable signatures in g-mode period spacings that serve as seismic diagnostics for probing deep stellar magnetism in γ Dor stars.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Understanding internal magnetic fields in stars is crucial for describing angular momentum transport and stellar evolution, but probing these fields remains challenging. Asteroseismology offers a powerful method to detect and characterize these internal magnetic fields.

Method: The authors implement a new modular version of the 2D oscillation code (ACOR) that incorporates both stellar rotation and magnetic fields. The modular design uses symbolic calculus to specify equations and assumptions. The full adiabatic, non-radial pulsation equations are solved using a spectral approach for angular mode structure and high-order finite differences for the radial part. Initially, a purely toroidal, axisymmetric magnetic field aligned with the rotation axis is considered, and results are validated against first-order perturbative predictions (weak-field regime) and the TARM (traditional approximation of rotation and magnetism) for stronger fields.

Result: The new code implementation shows good agreement with both perturbative and TARM approaches, establishing validity limits for these approximate methods. The results demonstrate that internal magnetic fields leave characteristic signatures in the period spacing of g modes, providing promising seismic diagnostics for probing deep stellar magnetism in γ Dor stars.

Conclusion: The validated code provides a robust framework for studying the combined effects of rotation and magnetic fields on stellar oscillations. The identified seismic signatures offer new observational avenues to probe deep stellar magnetism. Future work will extend the framework to more realistic magnetic field topologies and a broader range of pulsating stars, including red giants.

Abstract: Understanding the role of internal magnetic fields in stars remains a major challenge for the description of angular momentum transport and stellar evolution. It is therefore essential to probe these magnetic fields within the star, and asteroseismology provides a powerful means to do so. In this work, we present a new implementation of the 2D oscillation code ( ), which incorporates the effects of both stellar rotation and magnetic fields. Adaptive Code of Oscillations towards Realistic modeling The code has been rendered modular, making it possible to specify the set of equations to solve and the assumptions through a symbolic calculus approach. The full set of adiabatic, non-radial pulsation equations is solved using a spectral approach for the angular part of the modes and high-order finite differences for the radial part. As a first step, we focus on a magnetic field that is purely toroidal and axisymmetric about the star's rotation axis. The numerical results are compared against first-order perturbative predictions in the weak-field regime and with the traditional approximation of rotation and magnetism (TARM) in the case of stronger magnetic fields. We validate this new implementation of against perturbative and TARM approaches, showing good agreement and demonstrating the robustness of the code. The breakdown of these methods provides general validity limits. We show that internal magnetic fields leave signatures in the period spacing of g modes. These features provide a promising seismic diagnostic to probe deep stellar magnetism in γ Dor stars. Future work will aim to extend this framework to more realistic magnetic field topologies and a broader range of pulsating stars, including red giants.

### [3] [A high-frequency type II radio burst associated with an X2.3 class flare](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28370)
*Divya Paliwal,Anshu Kumari,Vishwa Vijay Singh,Dinesh Mishra,Pritam Das,Nadiya K*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper presents a multiwavelength analysis of a rare high-frequency type II radio burst (starting ~750 MHz) associated with an X2.3 solar flare on 6 November 2024, showing that a compact magnetic flux rope eruption—even without a detectable white-light CME—can generate low-coronal shocks producing high-frequency type II radio emission.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the origin of high-frequency type II radio bursts, which are rare and provide diagnostics of shock formation in the low corona, particularly in events where no large-scale CME is detected.

Method: Multiwavelength analysis combining radio spectrograph observations (type II burst over 750–45 MHz), NRH radio imaging (southward-propagating sources), SDO/AIA EUV imaging (propagating disturbance), HEL1OS (Aditya-L1) and STIX (Solar Orbiter) X-ray spectroscopy, NLFFF extrapolation (pre-eruptive flux rope), and stereoscopic EUV observations to derive eruption speed, compared with shock speed from radio dynamics.

Result: A rare high-frequency type II burst with starting frequency ~750 MHz and drift rate ~0.5 MHz/s was observed. Radio sources propagated southward. Efficient non-thermal electron acceleration was detected. An NLFFF extrapolation revealed a pre-eruptive magnetic flux rope, but no detectable white-light CME was observed. The flux rope eruption speed from stereoscopic EUV observations was consistent with the inferred shock speed.

Conclusion: Compact flux rope eruptions can generate low-coronal shocks that produce high-frequency type II radio bursts even in the absence of a detectable white-light CME.

Abstract: Radio observations provide a powerful diagnostic of the solar corona, enabling investigations of dynamic phenomena associated with solar flares, coronal mass ejections (CMEs), and shock waves. We present a multiwavelength analysis of a rare high-frequency type II radio burst (starting frequency of $\sim$750 MHz and frequency drift rate of $\sim$0.5 MHz s$^{-1}$) associated with the X2.3-class solar flare that occurred on 6 November 2024. A propagating EUV disturbance was observed shortly after the flare onset in the SDO/AIA field of view, while radio spectrographs recorded the type II burst between 13:46 and 13:56 UT over a frequency range of $\sim$750 to 45 MHz. Radio imaging observations from the Nançay Radioheliograph (NRH) show that the radio sources propagate southward during the event. X-ray spectroscopy from HEL1OS onboard Aditya-L1 and imaging observations from STIX onboard Solar Orbiter reveal signatures of efficient non-thermal electron acceleration associated with the flare. An NLFFF extrapolation identifies a pre-eruptive magnetic flux rope in the source region, while white-light coronagraph observations obtained during the event show no detectable large-scale CME. The speed of the erupting flux rope, derived from stereoscopic EUV observations, is consistent, within measurement uncertainties, with the shock speed inferred from the radio dynamic spectrum. Together, these observations suggest that compact flux rope eruptions, even in the absence of a detectable white-light CME, can generate low-coronal shocks capable of producing high-frequency type II radio emission.

### [4] [An All-Sky Catalog of 6.5 Million Primary Red Clump Stars from Gaia DR3 XP Spectra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28522)
*Zheng Yu,Bingqiu Chen*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper constructs two all-sky catalogs of red clump (RC) stars—a high-purity Tier 1 sample (532,189 stars, 97% purity) and a high-completeness Tier 2 sample (6,534,931 stars, 86% completeness)—by training a mixture density network on Gaia DR3 XP spectra to estimate asteroseismic parameters (ΔΠ₁ and Δν). The catalogs include distances and extinctions, achieve 6% median photometric distance precision, enable independent calibration of the Gaia parallax zero-point, and trace the Milky Way's 3D density distribution to R≈25 kpc.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Red clump stars serve as excellent standard candles for mapping the Milky Way's three-dimensional structure, but an all-sky, homogeneous catalog of primary RC stars with reliable distances has been lacking. Constructing such a catalog is essential for Galactic archaeology and for tracing the chemo-dynamical evolution of the Galaxy.

Method: The authors train a mixture density network (MDN) on a cross-matched sample to map 343-dimensional corrected Gaia DR3 XP spectra to asteroseismic parameters, namely ΔΠ₁ (asymptotic period spacing of dipole gravity modes) and Δν (large frequency separation). These two estimates are used to select primary RC stars via two distinct parameter-space cuts, yielding complementary Tier 1 (high-purity) and Tier 2 (high-completeness) catalogs covering the full celestial sphere.

Result: The Tier 1 catalog contains 532,189 stars with 97% purity, while the Tier 2 catalog contains 6,534,931 stars with 86% completeness. Median photometric distance precision is 6% for Tier 1 stars. The distances enable independent calibration of the Gaia DR3 parallax zero-point offset, and the Tier 2 sample traces the 3D density distribution of the Milky Way continuously to R≈25 kpc. Both catalogs are publicly released.

Conclusion: These publicly available catalogs provide a homogeneous, all-sky dataset of RC stars with accurate distances and extinctions, serving as a valuable resource for Galactic archaeology, chemo-dynamical studies of the Milky Way, and calibration of stellar population models.

Abstract: Red clump (RC) stars are excellent standard candles for mapping the three-dimensional structure of the Milky Way. We construct an all-sky catalog of 6.5 million primary RC stars using spectroscopic estimates of asteroseismic parameters inferred from Gaia DR3 low-resolution XP spectra. We train a mixture density network (MDN) on a cross-matched sample. The network maps each 343-dimensional corrected XP spectrum to estimates of $ΔΠ_{1}$, the asymptotic period spacing of dipole gravity modes, and $Δν$, the large frequency separation. We select primary RC stars using these two estimates. We release two complementary catalogs defined by different parameter-space cuts and thresholds: a high-purity Tier 1 sample of 532,189 stars with 97% purity and a high-completeness Tier 2 sample of 6,534,931 stars with 86% completeness. Both catalogs cover the full celestial sphere. We provide distances and extinction values for all stars. We obtain a median photometric distance precision of 6% for Tier 1 RC stars. Using the resultant distances, we independently calibrate the Gaia DR3 parallax zero-point offset. The three-dimensional density distribution traced by the Tier 2 sample extends continuously to $R\approx25$ kpc. Both catalogs are publicly available. These catalogs provide a valuable resource for Galactic archaeology, delivering a homogeneous dataset to trace the chemo-dynamical evolution of the Milky Way and to calibrate models of stellar populations.
