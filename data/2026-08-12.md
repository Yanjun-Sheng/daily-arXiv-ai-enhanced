*Jun Chen,Bernhard Kliem,Tibor Török,Rui Liu*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper presents a parametric numerical study of the torus instability threshold for arched current channels using the force-free Titov-Démoulin equilibrium. The critical decay index n_cr is found to scatter in the range ~1-2.5 depending on external toroidal field strength and line-tying, with both factors stabilizing the instability. The potential field approximation using the horizontal field component perpendicular to the flux rope axis agrees well with the exact numerical threshold.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The torus instability threshold (critical decay index) for solar and stellar eruptions is insufficiently known, with observed and simulated values scattering about the canonical value n_cr = 3/2 in the range ~1-2. A systematic parametric study is needed to explain this scatter and identify the physical factors governing the threshold.

Method: The authors perform parametric numerical stability calculations using the force-free Titov-Démoulin equilibrium of a line-tied partial toroidal current channel and flux rope. They vary the external toroidal (guide/shear) field and geometry (approximating semicircular geometry for minimal photospheric line-tying), and compare exact stability thresholds against a potential-field-based approximation.

Result: For zero external toroidal field and approximately semicircular geometry, the threshold lies in the range ~1-1.5 as theoretically expected. An external toroidal field significantly stabilizes the instability, raising n_cr up to ~2.5, explaining values above the canonical 3/2. Line-tying also acts stabilizing. The potential field approximation agrees very well with the exact numerical threshold when using the horizontal field component perpendicular to the flux rope axis.

Conclusion: The scatter of the observed torus instability threshold can be explained by variations in the external toroidal (guide) field strength and line-tying effects. The stabilizing role of the external toroidal field accounts for thresholds exceeding the canonical value of 3/2, while the potential field approximation provides a reliable practical estimate for the threshold.

Abstract: The torus instability of an arched current channel has been suggested to initiate and drive major solar and stellar eruptions. Its threshold, given by the critical decay index of the equilibrium external poloidal field (the so-called strapping field) at the position of the current channel, is insufficiently known. Here, we carry out a parametric numerical study of the threshold, employing the force-free Titov-Démoulin equilibrium of a line-tied partial toroidal current channel and flux rope. This addresses the scatter of the threshold about its canonical value, $n_\mathrm{cr}=3/2$. Values scattering in the range $n_\mathrm{cr}\approx$\,1--2 are typically found in numerical and observational studies of flux rope eruptions on the Sun. For zero external toroidal (guide, or shear) field and approximately semicircular geometry (corresponding to minimal photospheric line-tying), we find the threshold to lie in the theoretically expected range of $\approx$\,1--1.5. An external toroidal field introduces a strong stabilizing effect on the instability, raising the threshold up to $\sim$\,2.5, which can explain observational and numerical results above the canonical value. Line-tying is found to act stabilizing as well. We also consider the approximate threshold based on the potential field and find a very good agreement with the exact numerical value, provided the horizontal component perpendicular to the flux rope axis is used to approximate the external poloidal field.
