### [1] [Direct Evidence of Non-Ideal Dissipative Dynamics in Solar Wind Magnetic Switchbacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14114)
*Forrest Mozer,Oleksiy Agapitov,Kyung-Eun Choi,Richard Sydora*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Using Parker Solar Probe electric field measurements (13-40 solar radii), this paper shows that magnetic switchbacks have non-zero electric fields in the plasma frame and enhanced three-component Poynting vectors, proving they are Hall-MHD (not Ideal MHD) structures and active sites of in-situ evolution rather than simple outward-propagating pulses.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To settle fundamental controversies about whether magnetic switchbacks obey Ideal MHD or Hall-MHD physics and whether they originate at the solar surface or evolve in the solar wind.

Method: Analysis of Parker Solar Probe electric field measurements at 13-40 solar radii to examine plasma-frame electric fields and Poynting vectors inside switchbacks.

Result: Switchbacks exhibit non-zero electric fields in the plasma frame (proving Hall-MHD physics) and enhanced three-component Poynting vectors existing only inside switchbacks, contradicting the simple outward-propagating pulse model.

Conclusion: Switchbacks in the young solar wind are a non-MHD (Hall-MHD) process and sites of active in-situ evolution, providing a new framework for understanding energy transport and dissipation in astrophysical plasmas.

Abstract: Magnetic switchbacks, large-amplitude, localized Alfvenic like rotations of the solar wind magnetic field, have been the subject of intensive investigation, with approximately 200 refereed papers published in the last decade. Yet, fundamental controversies persist regarding whether switchbacks can be described with Ideal MHD (magnetohydrodynamic) physics or Hall-MHD physics and whether their origin is at the solar surface or in the solar wind. To settle these controversies, we present Parker Solar Probe electric field measurements between 13 and 40 solar radii, which show that switchbacks have non-zero electric fields in the plasma frame, a finding that definitively settles the physics controversy by proving that switchbacks are Hall-MHD, not Ideal MHD, structures. Along with these electric fields, there are enhanced Poynting vectors having three components with similar magnitudes that exist only inside the switchbacks. These facts contradict the view of switchbacks as simple outward-propagating pulses. Together, they resolve one controversy by showing that switchbacks in the young solar wind are a non-MHD process. They contribute to the second (source) controversy by identifying switchbacks as sites of active, in-situ, evolution. These findings provide a new framework for understanding energy transport and dissipation in astrophysical plasmas.

### [2] [Analysing the highly irregular boundaries of solar pores](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15112)
*T. J. Duckenfield,D. B. Jess,S. Jafarzadeh,L. A. C. A. Schiavo,S. S. A. Silva,S. D. T. Grant*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The paper introduces a convex hull framework for analyzing MHD waves in solar pores with irregular boundaries, finding that fundamental sausage and kink modes remain reliable while higher-order fluting modes degrade. Sausage modes dominate and show upward frequency shifts (freely propagating compressive waves), while kink motions are weak, low-frequency, and likely forced by granular buffeting.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Solar pores have irregular, evolving boundaries that deviate from the ideal circular flux tubes assumed in standard MHD oscillation models, requiring a consistent framework for wave analysis in realistic geometries.

Method: The convex hull of the pore boundary (from intensity minimum error thresholding) is used as the analysis domain. Modal assurance criterion validates shape approximations, and Proper Orthogonal Decomposition is applied to multi-height observations (4170 Å, G-band, Na I, Ca II K).

Result: Sausage (m=1) and kink (m=2) modes remain reliable; higher-order fluting modes (m≥3) degrade due to boundary irregularity. Sausage modes dominate variance, show upward frequency shifts (propagating compressive waves). Kink modes are weak with a persistent low-frequency peak, consistent with forced granular buffeting rather than propagation.

Conclusion: A practical methodology for boundary-mode analysis in realistic, structured pores is established, providing new constraints on MHD wave nature and height evolution in the lower solar atmosphere.

Abstract: Solar pores possess irregular and evolving boundaries that are often far from the ideal circular flux tubes assumed in many magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) oscillation models. To tackle this irregularity in a consistent way for wave analysis, we introduce a framework that employs the convex hull of the pore boundary - derived from intensity minimum error thresholding - as the domain to perform further analysis. Using the modal assurance criterion, we find the range of pore shapes for which this approximation is valid. We demonstrate the usefulness of this framework by applying it to multi-height, high-cadence observations (4170 angstrom continuum, G-band, Na~\textsc{i}, and Ca~\textsc{ii}~K) of a solar pore, and apply Proper Orthogonal Decomposition of the convex hull to determine wave modes. The fundamental sausage ($m=1$) and kink ($m=2$) mode is found to remain reliable, while higher-order fluting modes ($m\ge3$) are strongly degraded by small-scale boundary irregularity. As expected, sausage-like modes dominate the variance at all heights and exhibit a systematic upward shift in frequency, consistent with freely propagating compressive waves. In contrast, the kink-like motions appear weak, confined to a persistent low-frequency peak, and most plausibly interpreted as a forced response to granular buffeting rather than a propagating mode. Together, these results establish a practical methodology for boundary-mode analysis in real, highly structured pores and provide new constraints on the nature and height evolution of MHD waves in the lower solar atmosphere.

### [3] [Lyman-alpha Radiation Pressure in Dense Star Clusters: Implications for Star Formation and Winds at Cosmic Dawn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13982)
*Shyam H Menon,Aaron Smith*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Using radiation hydrodynamic simulations and Monte Carlo radiative transfer, this study shows that Lyα radiation pressure has mild effects (~10%) on star formation efficiency in dense star clusters at cosmic dawn, but can drive rapid, powerful outflows in diffuse gas, especially in low-metallicity environments.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: JWST observations reveal that galaxies at cosmic dawn concentrate star formation in compact, dense star clusters. The low metallicities and high gas columns in these environments make Lyα radiation pressure a potentially crucial but poorly understood mechanism for their formation and evolution.

Method: The authors post-process snapshots from radiation hydrodynamics (RHD) simulations of dense star cluster-forming clouds (Σ* ≳ 10³ M⊙ pc⁻²) with dust abundances Zd = 0–0.1 Z⊙, using the COLT Monte Carlo code to compute Lyα radiative transfer and force multipliers.

Result: Lyα radiation pressure has mild (~10%) effects on gas-to-star conversion efficiencies (ε* ≳ 60%) for Zd ≳ 0.01 Z⊙, and ε* ≳ 25% even in dust-free environments—much higher than local values (<10%). Dense filaments (n ≳ 10⁴ cm⁻³) remain sub-Eddington (fEdd < 1), while diffuse gas (n ≲ 10³ cm⁻³) has fEdd > 1 with fractions having fEdd ≳ 10, driving rapid winds (<4 Myr). The Lyα force multiplier MF is highly sensitive to dust: MF ≲ 3 for 0.1 Z⊙ and ≲ 500 for dust-free. Lyα dominates over UV/IR radiation pressure by factors of ~3–500 across all Zd ≲ 0.1 Z⊙.

Conclusion: Lyα radiation pressure reinforces the emerging picture of locally efficient, bursty star formation accompanied by rapid outflows in galaxies at cosmic dawn, with implications for ionizing photon escape and galactic-scale winds.

Abstract: Observations with the JWST in lensed fields have revealed that galaxies at cosmic dawn may concentrate their star formation in highly dense, compact, star clusters. The high columns and low metallicities encountered in their birth environments suggest that Lyman-alpha (Ly$α$) radiation pressure may be crucial to their formation and evolution. In this study, we address this question by post-processing snapshots from radiation hydrodynamic simulations of dense star cluster-forming clouds ($Σ_*\gtrsim10^3{M_\odot{pc}^{-2}}$) with a range of dust abundances ($Z_d=0-0.1Z_{d,\odot}$) using the COLT Monte Carlo code. We infer that Ly$α$ is likely to have mild (~10%) effects on the gas-to-star conversion efficiencies ($ε_*\gtrsim60$%) for $Z_d\gtrsim0.01Z_{d,\odot}$, and even in dust-free environments, $ε_*\gtrsim25$% - much higher than the <10% values typical of star-forming regions in the local Universe. This is because the densest filaments dominating stellar mass assembly ($n\gtrsim10^4{cm}^{-3}$) remain sub-Eddington ($f_{Edd}<1$). On the other hand, the bulk of the gas volume ($n\lesssim10^3{cm}^{-3}$) has $f_{Edd}>1$, with noticeable fractions having $f_{Edd}\gtrsim10$, implying that Ly$α$ can launch dynamically significant winds from these systems rapidly ($\lesssim$4Myr), with possible implications for ionizing photon escape and galactic outflows. The Ly$α$ force multiplier $M_F$ is highly sensitive to $Z_d$, with $M_F\lesssim3$ ($\lesssim 500$) for $0.1Z_{d,\odot}$ (dust-free) environments respectively. Nevertheless, Ly$α$ dominates over UV and IR radiation pressure at all values of $Z_d\lesssim0.1Z_{d,\odot}$, by factors of ~3-500. Our results suggest that Ly$α$ radiation pressure reinforces the emerging picture of locally efficient, bursty star formation accompanied by rapid outflows in galaxies at cosmic dawn.

### [4] [Identifying group galaxies merging with massive clusters using machine learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14930)
*Rhys Jordan,Meghan E. Gray,Alfonso Aragón-Salamanca,Steven P. Bamford,Frazer R. Pearce,Roan Haggar*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: A supervised machine learning framework classifies galaxies into main cluster, group, and neither categories using observable properties, achieving 81% overall accuracy. Group galaxies are hardest to identify (30% completeness, 76% purity). Performance improves beyond R200 and is robust across cluster masses and dynamical states, outperforming Friends-of-Friends and Gaussian Mixture Models.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Galaxy pre-processing in cluster infall regions is poorly understood due to difficulty identifying environmental substructures (especially groups) caused by projection effects and fingers-of-god distortions.

Method: Supervised machine learning classifier trained on mock observations from cosmological simulations, using positions, line-of-sight velocities, and stellar mass as features. Three-class classification: main cluster, group, neither. Evaluated with resampling and first-past-the-post thresholding.

Result: Overall accuracy 81%, weighted precision 81%. Neither and main cluster reliably recovered; groups achieved 30% completeness and 76% purity. Performance suppressed within 1×R200, improved beyond it (40% completeness, 80% purity). Consistent across cluster masses and dynamical states; outperforms FoF and GMM.

Conclusion: The framework is flexible and suitable for upcoming spectroscopic surveys of cluster infall regions, providing a robust statistical tool for disentangling environmental influences on galaxy evolution.

Abstract: The environment plays a critical role in galaxy evolution, with galaxy clusters and their infall regions offering diverse conditions that shape galaxies before they enter the dense cluster core, a process known as ``pre-processing''. However, identifying environmental substructures, particularly galaxy groups in these transitional zones, remains challenging due to projection effects and ``fingers-of-god'' distortions. In this work, we present a supervised machine learning framework for classifying galaxies into three environmental categories: main cluster, group, and neither, using observable galaxy properties such as positions, line-of-sight velocities, and stellar mass. The model is trained on mock observations derived from cosmological simulations designed to replicate survey conditions and achieves an overall accuracy and class-size-weighted precision of 81%. The neither and main cluster populations are reliably recovered, whereas group galaxies remain the most challenging to identify, achieving 30% completeness and 76% purity. Within $1\times R_{200}$, classification performance is suppressed, but it improves beyond this radius, reaching 40% completeness and 80% purity. Resampling and thresholding strategies allow the model to be tuned toward either higher purity or higher completeness; in this study, we adopt first-past-the-post thresholding to emphasise purity. Model performance is consistent across cluster masses and dynamical states, and it outperforms both Friends-of-Friends and Gaussian Mixture Modelling. This flexibility makes it well suited to upcoming spectroscopic surveys of cluster infall regions, providing a robust statistical tool for disentangling environmental influences on galaxy evolution.

TL;DR: Using JWST/NIRCam observations of Boötes I ultra-faint dwarf galaxy, the authors resolve over 10,000 stars down to <0.15 M⊙ and apply machine learning + forward modeling to constrain the low-mass IMF. Motivation: The dependence of the stellar IMF on star-formation environment, especially at low metallicities and high redshifts, remains poorly constrained. UFDs serve as local fossils of high-redshift galaxies, offering a unique opportunity to probe the sub-solar IMF in extreme conditions.

Method: JWST/NIRCam resolved photometry of Boo I UFD (>10,000 stars down to <0.15 M⊙); forward modeling of synthetic CMDs combined with simulation-based inference and machine learning to test three IMF functional forms (single power-law, broken power-law, lognormal).

Abstract: The dependence of the stellar initial mass function (IMF) on star-formation environment, particularly at low metallicities and high redshifts, remains poorly constrained. Ultra-faint dwarf galaxies (UFDs) are local fossils of high-redshift galaxies hosting old, metal-poor populations, and their resolved stellar populations provide unique pathways to constrain the sub-solar IMF. We investigate the low-mass IMF in the Boötes I (Boo I) UFD with JWST/NIRCam, leveraging its capability to resolve over 10,000 stars reaching $\lesssim0.15 M_{\odot}$, obtaining one of the largest, deepest resolved stellar samples for UFDs. By treating Boo I as a local relic analogous to a high-redshift galaxy with a stellar mass of $\lesssim10^5 M_{\odot}$ at $z\gtrsim6$, our results provide evidence for the universality of the IMF across both local and high-redshift environments.

### [6] [Impact of stellar population models on the estimated physical properties of galaxies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15096)
*Bozhidara Stoyanova,Médéric Boquien,Paola Santini,Francesco Tombesi,Emiliano Merlin,Pietro Bergamini,Véronique Buat,Denis Burgarella*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper investigates how the choice of stellar population synthesis (SPS) models affects star formation rate and stellar mass estimates derived from SED modeling of 17,230 galaxies using JWST and HST photometry. Results show systematic differences up to ~0.6 dex in stellar mass and ~0.4 dex in SFR between models, highlighting significant model-induced uncertainties.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Accurate estimates of galaxy properties like SFRs and stellar masses are essential for constraining galaxy formation models. However, the impact of underlying SPS model choice on these derived parameters remains poorly quantified, motivating a systematic investigation.

Method: Four widely used SPS models were applied to a sample of 17,230 galaxies with spectroscopic redshifts from HST and JWST photometric catalogs. SEDs were modeled using the Code for Investigating GALaxy Emission (CIGALE) in two steps: (i) estimating galaxy properties with each SPS model, and (ii) using synthetic catalogs to assess the relative impact of model choice.

Result: Systematic differences were found between models, with stellar mass estimates varying by up to ~0.6 dex and SFRs by up to ~0.4 dex between certain model pairs.

Conclusion: The choice of stellar population model introduces significant systematic uncertainties in derived galaxy properties. This dependence should be accounted for when interpreting SED-based measurements and comparing results across different galaxy evolution studies.

Abstract: Accurate estimates of fundamental physical properties of galaxies, such as star formation rates (SFRs) or stellar masses, are essential for testing and constraining models of galaxy formation and evolution. Spectral energy distribution (SED) modeling has become the standard method for deriving these quantities. However, the influence of the underlying stellar population synthesis (SPS) models on the inferred parameters remains poorly quantified. This work investigates how the choice of SPS models affects the estimation of SFRs and stellar masses derived from SED modeling. Four widely used SPS models are applied to a sample of 17 230 galaxies with spectroscopic redshifts, selected from recently published Hubble Space Telescope and James Webb Space Telescope photometric catalogs. SEDs are modeled using the Code for Investigating GALaxy Emission. The choice of stellar population model introduces significant systematic uncertainties in derived galaxy properties. This dependence should be accounted for when interpreting SED-based measurements and comparing results across different studies of galaxy evolution.
