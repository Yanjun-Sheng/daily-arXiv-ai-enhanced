### [1] [BIND (Baryonic INpainting with Deep learning): A Field-level Emulator for Galaxy Groups and Clusters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10709)
*Max E. Lee,Shy Genel,Zoltan Haiman,Greg L. Bryan,Christopher C. Lovell,Boryana Hadzhiyska*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: BIND is a conditional flow-matching model that maps dark-matter-only halos to their hydrodynamical counterparts at the field level, trained on CAMELS simulations, achieving percent-level mass accuracy and reproducing power spectrum suppression efficiently.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Baryonic feedback is a dominant systematic uncertainty for upcoming weak-lensing surveys, yet existing modeling tools rely on spherical approximations and density profiles calibrated almost entirely on two-point statistics, which is insufficient for field-level analyses.

Method: BIND uses conditional flow matching to learn a field-level mapping from dark-matter-only halos to hydrodynamical counterparts. It is trained on 1024 paired hydrodynamical and dark-matter-only CAMELS $50\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ SB35 simulations, sampling dark matter, gas, and stellar mass fields over redshift across the full 35-dimensional $Λ$CDM and IllustrisTNG galaxy formation parameter space. Halo mass is not supplied as conditioning.

Result: BIND recovers dark matter, gas, and stellar masses at the percent level, reproduces azimuthally averaged profiles to $\lesssim10\%$ at all radii, and matches halo shape distributions with high fidelity. It captures rank correlations between generated fields and subgrid parameters, and the field-level response to individual parameter variations in both sign and morphology. It reproduces baryon fraction, the stellar-to-halo mass relation, inter-component scaling relations, and joint covariance of residuals. Applied halo-by-halo to a $(50\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc})^3$ $N$-body volume with $512^3$ particles, it reproduces projected matter power spectrum suppression to the accuracy ceiling set by pasting in hydrodynamical halos themselves, in minutes on one GPU.

Conclusion: BIND provides an accurate, efficient field-level emulation of baryonic feedback, released as an open-source tool with all generated halos. A companion paper extends BIND to thermodynamic fields and non-Gaussian weak-lensing statistics.

Abstract: Baryonic feedback is a dominant source of systematic uncertainty for upcoming weak-lensing surveys, but current tools for modeling its effect rely on spherical approximations and density profiles calibrated almost entirely on two-point statistics. We introduce BIND (Baryonic INpainting with Deep learning), a conditional flow-matching model that learns a field-level mapping from dark-matter-only halos to their hydrodynamical counterparts. BIND is trained on halos from the 1024 paired hydrodynamical and dark-matter-only simulations of the CAMELS $50\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ SB35 suite and samples dark matter, gas, and stellar mass fields over redshift across the full 35-dimensional $Λ$CDM and IllustrisTNG galaxy formation parameter space. BIND recovers dark matter, gas, and stellar masses at the percent level, reproduces azimuthally averaged profiles to $\lesssim10\%$ at all radii, and matches halo shape distributions with high fidelity. The learned parameter dependence captures the rank correlations between the generated fields and the subgrid parameters, and the field-level response to individual parameter variations is recovered in both sign and morphology. Halo mass is never supplied as conditioning, yet the baryon fraction, stellar-to-halo mass relation, inter-component scaling relations, and the joint covariance of their residuals are all reproduced. We finally show that, applied halo-by-halo to a $(50\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc})^3$ $N$-body volume with $512^3$ particles, BIND reproduces the projected matter power spectrum suppression to the accuracy ceiling set by pasting in the hydrodynamical halos themselves, in minutes on one GPU. We release the trained BIND models and all generated halos as open-source tools. A companion paper extends BIND to thermodynamic fields and non-Gaussian weak-lensing statistics.

### [2] [SPURS: An Ultra-deep View Inside the Compact, Nitrogen-Enriched Nuclei of Little Red Dots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11094)
*Mengtao Tang,Daniel P. Stark,Charlotte A. Mason,Zuyi Chen,Tucker Jones,Sarah Searle Grannis,Peter Senchyna,Lily Whitler,Keerthi Vasan G. C.,Viola Gelli*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Ultra-deep rest-UV spectroscopy of four UV-bright Little Red Dots reveals broad CIV, high narrow-line densities, nitrogen enhancement, deep neutral-gas embedding, and an orientation-dependent view of the central engine within a compact nuclear star cluster.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Little Red Dots (LRDs) are enigmatic UV-bright objects, but their nature is poorly constrained due to a lack of deep rest-UV spectroscopy probing the central engine, gas conditions, and abundances.

Method: The authors analyze ultra-deep rest-UV spectra of four UV-bright LRDs from the SPURS Cycle 4 Large Program, measuring broad and narrow emission lines (CIV, HeII, CIII]), P-Cygni absorption, interstellar absorption, Ly-alpha damping wings, fluorescent FeII/OI emission, and fine-structure absorption. They compare nitrogen and CIII] incidence with archival z>4 galaxy samples.

Result: Broad CIV (FWHM ~2700-2800 km/s) appears in two LRDs; narrow-line densities are elevated (n_e~1e4-1e5, up to 1e6 cm^-3); nitrogen is enhanced in all four. Broad HeII (FWHM ~930 km/s) and fast P-Cygni absorption (>2200 km/s) are also seen. All four show deeply embedded UV continuum (N_HI>1e22 cm^-2), with fluorescent FeII/OI and fine-structure absorption indicating gas near the UV-emitting region. Nitrogen and strong CIII] are more common in LRDs than in the general galaxy population. CIV transmission depends on rest-optical color, supporting an orientation-dependent picture. The UV-emitting region lies within ~8 pc of the nucleus.

Conclusion: The UV-emitting region resides within <8 pc of the LRD nucleus, consistent with an actively assembling nuclear star cluster. Dynamical interactions in this dense environment, including tidal disruption of stars, may explain the high incidence of nitrogen enhancement, with possible contributions from very massive star winds; other supermassive-star abundance patterns remain inconclusive.

Abstract: We present the first ultra-deep rest-UV spectroscopy of four UV-bright Little Red Dots (LRDs), obtained from the SPURS Cycle 4 Large Program. The spectra reveal broad CIV (FWHM $\approx2700-2800$ km s$^{-1}$) in two LRDs, alongside narrow-line densities elevated above star-forming galaxies ($n_e\sim10^4-10^5$ cm$^{-3}$, reaching $10^6$ cm$^{-3}$ in the most extreme source) and nitrogen-enhancements in all four LRDs. We detect broad HeII emission (FWHM $\approx930$ km s$^{-1}$) in one LRD, and two others with fast P-Cygni absorption ($\gtrsim2200$ km s$^{-1}$). Strong interstellar absorption lines and Ly$α$ damping wings reveal the UV continuum is deeply embedded in neutral gas ($N_{\rm HI}\gtrsim10^{22}$ cm$^{-2}$) in all four LRDs. Detections of fluorescent FeII and OI emission and fine-structure absorption indicate this gas lies close to the UV-emitting region. In archival $z>4$ samples, we find nitrogen and strong CIII] emission are significantly more common in LRDs than in the galaxy population. The transmission of broad CIV, tracing the broad-line region or cocoon, depends on rest-optical color within our sample, consistent with an orientation-dependent picture in which bluer, less obscured sightlines offer a more direct, polar view of the central engine and its outflows. We find several potential signatures of very massive stars, whose winds may contribute to nitrogen enhancement. We investigate other abundance patterns expected from supermassive stars but our results are inconclusive. Our results place the UV-emitting region within $\lesssim8$ pc of the LRD nucleus, consistent with an actively assembling nuclear star cluster. Dynamical interactions in this extremely dense environment, including tidal disruption of stars, may explain the high incidence of nitrogen enhancements in LRDs.

### [3] [Black Hole-Galaxy Correlations in Cluster Zoomed-in Simulations: GIZMO-SIMBA and TNG-Cluster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11191)
*Hangxin Pu,Antonios Katsianis,Weiguang Cui,Romeel Davé,Massimo Gaspari,Weishan Zhu,Xiaohu Yang,Jinyi Shangguan,Qingshan Wang,Ying Tang,Yuchang Li*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Using GIZMO-SIMBA and TNG-Cluster zoom-in simulations (z=0–5), the study shows that distinct subgrid physics produce fundamentally different SMBH–galaxy co-evolution pathways in massive clusters, yet both reproduce local scaling relations and the observed BHAR–SFR decoupling.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The co-evolution of supermassive black holes and their central galaxies in massive clusters remains poorly constrained, and it is unclear how different subgrid prescriptions for black hole accretion and feedback shape this co-evolution and the resulting scaling relations.

Method: Compare GIZMO-SIMBA (torque-limited accretion) and TNG-Cluster (feedback-regulated growth) zoom-in simulations of massive clusters over z=0–5, analyzing the halo mass–BH mass relation, BH mass–gas mass ratio relations, the BHAR–SFR relation, and the role of BH mass in regulating atomic and molecular gas depletion.

Result: GIZMO-SIMBA predicts a supply-driven scenario with rapid, synchronized SMBH assembly tied to M200c growth, fixing the halo mass–BH mass relation by z=3.0 similar to today's; TNG-Cluster shows feedback-regulated growth delayed by early thermal suppression. Both reproduce some local BH–galaxy scaling relations but imply different evolution. TNG-Cluster's isotropic kinetic winds deplete cold gas, causing a "hard quench" of star formation. Both reproduce the BHAR–SFR decoupling seen in massive cluster ellipticals, and BH mass is a primary regulator of atomic and molecular gas depletion in both models.

Conclusion: Divergent evolutionary trends underscore the importance of the multiphase intracluster medium. Although the subgrid models lack resolution and use distinct formalisms, sustained BHAR in quenched systems is broadly consistent with modern multiphase feeding paradigms such as chaotic cold accretion in turbulent cluster cores. Black hole mass emerges as a primary regulator of gas depletion in galaxy clusters.

Abstract: We investigate the co-evolution of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) and central galaxies in massive clusters using the GIZMO-SIMBA and TNG-Cluster zoom-in simulations at $z=0-5$. We find that the distinct subgrid physics of these two models suggest fundamentally different evolutionary pathways. On the one hand, GIZMO-SIMBA, employs torque-limited accretion and predicts a supply-driven scenario where the SMBHs rapidly assemble synchronized with dark matter halo ($M_{200c}$) growth (i.e. the halo mass-BH mass relation is set by $z=3.0$ and similar to the present-day relationship). On the other hand, TNG-Cluster, exhibits a feedback-regulated growth phase delayed by an early thermal suppression. While both models successfully reproduce some local black hole-galaxy scaling relations, they imply significantly different evolution for these relations. Analysis of the BH mass-gas mass ratio relations suggests that TNG-Cluster's isotropic kinetic winds efficiently deplete cold gas, resulting in a "hard quench" of star formation. In the black hole accretion rate (BHAR)-star formation rate (SFR) relation we find that both simulations successfully reproduce the decoupling of BHAR and star formation observed in recent massive cluster ellipticals. The divergent evolutionary trends emphasize the importance of the multiphase intracluster medium; while these subgrid models do not have the necessary resolution and employ distinct formalisms, the sustained BHAR in quenched systems resemble outcomes broadly consistent with modern multiphase feeding paradigms such as chaotic cold accretion in turbulent cluster cores. Furthermore, we demonstrate that for both models, black hole mass is a primary regulator of atomic and molecular gas depletion in galaxy clusters.

### [4] [Uncovering the Origin of Slow Rotators among Intermediate-Mass Stars in the Star-Forming Cluster Trumpler 14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11053)
*Khushboo K. Rao,Wen-Ping Chen,Hsiang-Yu Chen,Kristen Dage,Stella S. R. Offner*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Slow rotation in intermediate-mass stars in Trumpler 14 is linked to disk presence and X-ray emission, suggesting disks drive early spin-down while high-energy processes provide an additional angular-momentum-loss channel.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Intermediate-mass stars (1.5-8 solar masses) show a wide range of rotation rates and are linked to extended main-sequence turnoffs in young/intermediate-age clusters, yet the existence of slow rotators is puzzling given expectations of rapid rotation from radiative envelopes.

Method: The authors examined disk and X-ray signatures among 118 intermediate-mass members of the star-forming cluster Trumpler 14, identifying disk-bearing members via infrared spectral indices from spectral energy distributions, and analyzing X-ray sources with available v sin i data.

Result: Of 118 intermediate-mass members, 24 are disk-bearing (mostly outside the irradiated core) and are slow rotators except three fast-rotating Class III objects. Among the 118, 37 are X-ray sources, and 22 with v sin i data rotate systematically slower than X-ray quiet sources.

Conclusion: Young stellar disks in intermediate-mass stars contribute to early spin-down, and X-ray-traced high-energy processes may be an additional channel for angular-momentum loss during this phase.

Abstract: Intermediate-mass (about 1.5-8 M$_{\odot}$) stars exhibit a wide range of rotation rates and have gained attention for their roles in the extended main-sequence turnoffs (eMSTO) seen in some young and intermediate-age clusters (ages $< 2$ Gyr). Although rapid rotation is expected due to their radiative envelopes, the presence of slow rotators remains puzzling. In this study, we examine disk and X-ray signatures among the intermediate-mass members of the star-forming cluster Trumpler 14. Of the 118 intermediate-mass members, 24 are considered disk-bearing on the basis of their infrared spectral indices derived from spectral energy distributions. The majority of these reside outside the heavily irradiated cluster core and are all slow rotators ($\lessapprox 100$ km s$^{-1}$) except for three fast-rotating Class III objects. Additionally, in the same sample of 118, 37 are X-ray sources, and 22 having available $v \sin i$ data show a systematically slower rotation than X-ray quiet sources. Our findings suggest that, while young stellar disks in intermediate-mass stars contribute to early spin-down, high-energy processes traced by X-ray emission during this phase could be an additional channel for angular-momentum loss in these stars.

</details>
