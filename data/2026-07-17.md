### [1] [Enabling Cosmic Web Analysis at Gigaparsec Scales: A Multi Block Approach for DisPerSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14785)
*Ankit Singh,Frazer Pearce,Meghan Gray,Gustavo Yepes*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: The paper presents a frozen-core method that overcomes the memory bottleneck of DisPerSE for cosmic filament detection by decomposing the volume into overlapping blocks with circumsphere-based tessellation filtering, enabling gigaparsec-scale filament catalogues. Applied to the MDPL2 simulation (92 million haloes, 1 Gpc³), it achieves 99.6% length recovery, and confirms a power-law mass-connectivity relation for haloes across three decades in mass (10¹²–10¹⁵.⁵ M⊙).


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: DisPerSE, the most common topological filament finder, cannot scale to large simulations due to the memory bottleneck of Delaunay tessellation on the full point set, and naive volume splitting produces inconsistent tessellations and filament networks.

Method: A frozen-core approach: decompose the volume into overlapping blocks, tessellate each block, filter tetrahedra using a circumsphere criterion to retain only globally valid ones, then merge tiled outputs via core filtering, deduplication, and boundary stitching.

Result: 99.6% total length recovery, 100% recovery of density maxima/minima, 94.7% individual filament matching (unmatched ones are short, low-significance); applied to full (1 Gpc)³ MDPL2 box, producing a filament catalogue from 92 million haloes and confirming a power-law mass-connectivity relation across three decades in halo mass.

Conclusion: The frozen-core method successfully removes the memory bottleneck of DisPerSE, enabling accurate gigaparsec-scale filament reconstruction, and provides the first confirmation in an N-body halo catalogue that the theoretically predicted mass-connectivity scaling holds from group to cluster scales.

Abstract: Cosmic filaments are the longest structures in the Universe and the dominant element of the cosmic web, channelling matter onto clusters and shaping the environments in which galaxies form and evolve. Accurate reconstructions of this network across gigaparsec volumes are increasingly important for cosmology and galaxy evolution. However, the most commonly used topological filament finder, DisPerSE, faces a memory bottleneck: it requires a Delaunay tessellation of the full input point set, preventing application to large simulations. Naively splitting the volume fails, as different sub-volumes yield inconsistent tessellations and filament networks. We present a frozen-core method that overcomes this bottleneck while preserving the global topology. The volume is decomposed into overlapping blocks whose tessellations are filtered by a circumsphere criterion retaining only globally valid tetrahedra; a post-processing pipeline merges the tiled outputs through core filtering, deduplication, and boundary stitching. Validation against a monolithic reference on a $300\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ MDPL2 subvolume shows 99.6 per cent total length recovery, 100 per cent recovery of density maxima and minima, and 94.7 per cent individual filament matching (the ${\sim}$5 per cent of unmatched filaments are predominantly short, low-significance structures). We apply the method to the full $(1\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Gpc})^3$ MDPL2 box (92 million haloes), producing a gigaparsec-scale filament catalogue. As a first application, we measure the connectivity ($κ$) for 22,900 haloes spanning $M_{200\mathrm{c}} = 10^{12}$-$10^{15.5}\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$, finding a power-law mass-connectivity relation that extends from group to cluster scales, providing the first confirmation in an $N$-body halo catalogue that the theoretically predicted scaling holds across three decades in halo mass.

### [2] [Cluster characteristics of Galactic open clusters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15149)
*K. Neumannová,J. Supíková,N. Faltová,P. Mondal,E. Paunzen,T. Ramezani,G. Szász*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper analyzes ~7000 open clusters to estimate their total masses, radii, and 3D shapes using a conservative member-summation approach with evolutionary tracks from 2MASS, Gaia, and Pan-STARRS. It finds significant needle-like shape artifacts beyond 500 pc due to parallax uncertainties, and detects tidal tails in many clusters.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Star cluster parameters (mass, radius, shape) are critical for understanding stellar evolution, dynamics, and host galaxy evolution, but published values for these parameters show large disagreements, motivating a systematic and conservative re-estimation.

Method: Conservative mass estimation by summing individual member masses; evolutionary track interpolation tested on synthetic clusters via ASteCA; 3D shape analysis using Galactic coordinates and inverse parallaxes; data from 2MASS, Gaia, and Pan-STARRS.

Result: Total masses and radii estimated for ~7000 open clusters; tidal tail features detected; 3D shape analysis limited to 500 pc; beyond 500 pc, parallax uncertainties produce artificial needle-like shapes.

Conclusion: The derived cluster parameters are useful for future Gaia data releases; current DR3 limits robust 3D shape analysis to within 500 pc due to parallax uncertainty artifacts.

Abstract: Star clusters are perfectly suited for studying the formation, evolution, and dynamics of stars and their host galaxies. For this, we need to estimate total masses, radii and shapes of star clusters. These parameters strongly influence their fates in the host galaxies. However, there is no agreement about these characteristics in the literature. The published values widely disagree. We estimated total masses and cluster radii using a very conservative approach: calculating member masses and summing them. Furthermore, we investigated the three-dimensional characteristics of open clusters and their limitations. We used a commonly used grid of evolutionary tracks for different photometric sources, including 2MASS, Gaia, and Pan-STARRS. The interpolation method was tested using synthetic clusters built with ASteCA. To estimate three-dimensional shapes, we used the distribution of the well-known Galactic coordinates and inverse parallaxes. We estimated the total masses and radii of about 7000 open clusters and observed traces of tidal tails. Individual values can be used in future $Gaia$ data releases to obtain even more precise values. The current DR3 allows the study of only three-dimensional shapes up to 500 pc. Beyond this distance, we find a significant needle-like shape due to uncertainties in observed parallaxes.

### [3] [Runaway OB stars within 1 kpc of the Sun](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14633)
*Juan Martinez Garcia,Nicholas Wright,Alexis Quintana*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: A study analyzing runaway star probabilities for 40 O-type and 24,487 B-type stars within 1 kpc of the Sun, finding runaway fractions of 17.5% for O-type and 6.9% for B-type stars using a fixed velocity threshold method, consistent with prior work but with differences due to sample selection and methodology.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Runaway stars provide insights into the kinematic history of their birth stellar clusters, motivating the need to identify and characterize such stars in a volume-complete sample of OB stars within 1 kpc of the Sun.

Method: Fit a Galactic rotation model to observed proper motions of OB stars; identified runaway stars using both a fixed 2D peculiar velocity threshold of 23 km/s and by comparing individual peculiar velocities to the whole-sample dispersion.

Result: Runaway fractions: 17.5% for O-type stars (with asymmetric uncertainties) and 6.9±0.1% for B-type stars using the fixed velocity threshold.

Conclusion: Results are consistent with previous studies, but differences are largely attributable to variations in underlying OB star samples and methods used to select runaway stars.

Abstract: Runaway stars are high-velocity stars ejected from their birth environments that can provide insights into the kinematic history of the stellar cluster they were ejected from. We derived runaway star probabilities for 40 O-type stars and $24,487$ B-type stars taken from a recently published volume-complete sample of OB stars within 1 kpc of the Sun. We fit a Galactic rotation model to the observed proper motions of these stars and identify runaway stars using both a fixed 2D peculiar velocity threshold of $23\,km\,s^{-1}$ and by comparing individual peculiar velocities to the dispersion of the whole sample. We find runaway fractions of $17.5^{+0.1}_{-2.5}\%$ for O-type stars and $6.9\pm0.1\%$ for B-type stars; both using the fixed velocity threshold method. These values are consistent with previous studies, but with differences that are largely attributable to the underlying samples of OB stars used in various studies to identify runaway stars and to variations in the methods used to select them.

### [4] [Stellar Multiplicity in Open Clusters: Investigating Binary Fractions and Their Relationship with Cluster Properties](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14987)
*Ruan P. Alves,Hektor Monteiro,Wilton S. Dias,Gabriel R. Hickel*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Analysis of 773 open clusters reveals binary fractions of 0.10-0.85 overall and 0.06-0.79 for high mass-ratio systems. Binary fraction shows anticorrelation with metallicity but no strong age dependence. Young and old clusters have distinct mass-ratio distributions. Binary probability increases with mass below 1 solar mass, stabilizing at 60-70% for higher masses. Binaries concentrate in cluster inner regions; mean mass ratio decreases with distance from center. Results suggest binary fraction is set early and persists through dynamical evolution.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate how binary fraction and mass ratios change across different open cluster environments, since most stars form in multiple systems and understanding how environmental properties affect their occurrence is crucial for stellar formation and evolution studies.

Method: Analysis of a sample of 773 open clusters, identifying binary systems and estimating binary fractions across different cluster environments including age, evolutionary stage, and metallicity.

Result: Binary fractions range 0.10-0.85 overall and 0.06-0.79 for high mass-ratio systems. No strong dependence on age or evolutionary stage; anticorrelation between metallicity and binary fraction. Young and old clusters have distinct mass-ratio distributions. Binary probability increases with mass below 1 solar mass, stabilizes at 60-70% for higher masses. Binaries concentrate in inner cluster regions; mean mass ratio decreases with distance from center.

Conclusion: Binary fraction appears to be set at early times and remains largely unaffected by cluster dynamical evolution. The concentration of binaries in inner cluster regions likely reduces their susceptibility to dynamical escape.

Abstract: Stellar multiplicity is key in dynamical evolution of open clusters (OCs) and processes of stellar formation and evolution. Since most stars form in multiple systems, investigating how environmental properties affect their occurrence is crucial. We aim to investigate how binary fraction and mass ratios change across different OC environments. Using a sample of 773 OCs, we identified binary systems and estimated binary fractions, obtaining values in the ranges 0.10-0.85 overall and 0.06-0.79 for high mass-ratio systems. No strong dependence on age or evolutionary stage is found, while an anticorrelation between metallicity and the binary fraction is identified. However, young and old clusters exhibit distinct mass-ratio distributions. We also find that the probability of a star being the primary component increases with stellar mass. In contrast, the probability of a star belonging to a binary system increases with mass for masses below 1 solar mass, and remains between 60% and 70% for high masses. Binary systems are preferentially located in the inner regions of clusters compared to single stars, and the mean mass ratio decreases with increasing distance from the cluster center. These results suggest that the binary fraction appears to be set at early times and remains largely unaffected by cluster dynamical evolution. The concentration of binaries in the inner regions of clusters likely reduces their susceptibility to dynamical escape.

### [5] [The non-LTE abundances of magnesium and yttrium and asteroseismic ages for the chemical clock calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15017)
*Š. Mikolaitis,G. Tautvaišienė,E. Pakštienė,A. Drazdauskas,V. Bagdonas,C. Viscasillas Vázquez,M. Ambrosch,Y. Chorniy,R. Minkevičiūtė,E. Stonkutė,B. Bale,B. Ćurjurić,A. Sharma,K. Diktanaitė*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This study expands on previous work by analyzing 528 Galactic field stars (plus 208 from prior study, total 736) to investigate spatial variations in the [Y/Mg]-age relation across the Galactic disc, using NLTE-corrected abundances and asteroseismic/isochronal ages, finding systematic spatial variations that reflect different star-formation and chemical-enrichment histories, with [Y/Mg] generally increasing with metallicity but flattening at supersolar metallicities.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate spatial variations in the empirical [Y/Mg]-age relation across the Galactic disc using a substantially larger stellar sample, building on previous work that highlighted the importance of accounting for NLTE effects and using asteroseismic ages for abundance determinations.

Method: High-resolution stellar spectra were analyzed; Mg and Y abundances were determined via spectral synthesis of multiple features with rigorous NLTE corrections. Asteroseismic ages were derived for stars with solar-type oscillations (307 stars), while isochrone-based ages (cross-checked) were used for the remaining 221 stars. A total sample of 736 stars (528 new + 208 from prior study) was analyzed. Spectroscopic binaries were identified (2 triple-lined, 9 double-lined).

Result: The [Y/Mg]-age relation shows systematic spatial variations across the Galactic disc, likely reflecting differences in star-formation and chemical-enrichment histories. [Y/Mg] tends to increase with metallicity over the investigated age range. At supersolar metallicity, the trend weakens and the [Y/Mg]-age relations become flatter compared to solar-metallicity stars, which show lower [Y/Mg] at young ages and higher values at old ages.

Conclusion: The [Y/Mg]-age relation is not universal across the Galactic disc; it exhibits spatial variations linked to local star-formation and enrichment histories. The metallicity-dependent behavior (flattening at supersolar metallicities) suggests that chemical evolution models must account for such complexities when using [Y/Mg] as a chemical clock.

Abstract: Building on our previous study, which demonstrated the importance of accounting for departures from local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) in elemental-abundance determinations and of using asteroseismic ages, we investigate spatial variations in the empirical [Y/Mg]-age relation across the Galactic disc using a substantially larger stellar sample. We analysed high-resolution stellar spectra and determined Mg and Y abundances through spectral synthesis of multiple spectral features, rigorously accounting for non-LTE (NLTE) effects. We derived asteroseismic ages for stars exhibiting solar-type oscillations and used cross-checked isochrone-based methods for the remaining stars. We determined atmospheric parameters and Mg and Y abundances for 528 Galactic field stars, together with asteroseismic ages for 307 stars and isochronal ages for 221 stars. We also identified two new triple-lined and nine double-lined spectroscopic systems. Combining the present sample with that of our previous study yielded a total of 736 stars, which we used to examine the [Y/Mg]-age relation across the Galactic disc. The relation shows systematic spatial variations that likely reflect differences in star-formation and chemical-enrichment histories. In general, [Y/Mg] tends to increase with metallicity over the investigated age range. At supersolar metallicity, however, this trend may weaken, and the [Y/Mg]-age relations become flatter than those of solar-metallicity stars, which show lower [Y/Mg] values at young ages and higher values at old ages.
