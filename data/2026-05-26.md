### [1] [Mergers via failed common envelope as a route towards intermediate-mass stripped stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22911)
*Annachiara Picco,Pablo Marchant,Daniel Pauli,Hugues Sana*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper investigates a merger scenario ("failed common envelope") for forming intermediate-mass stripped stars, using binary simulations at Galactic metallicity. The merger products span 6-14 solar masses, appear as core He-burning stars with thin hydrogen envelopes, and can appear as single stars or in wide binaries from hierarchical triples.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The recent discovery of intermediate-mass stripped stars provides a continuous evolutionary sequence and test beds for binary interaction models. A merger scenario was suggested to explain the magnetic intermediate-mass stripped star HD 45166, motivating a detailed investigation of this channel.

Method: Detailed interacting binary simulations at Galactic metallicity, modeling stripped merger products from a merger scenario (failed common envelope) between a stripped star and a post-main sequence companion.

Result: These merger products contribute to the population of stripped stars across the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram, produce a blue-straggler effect, with merger masses spanning 6-14 solar masses. They are consistent with long-lived core He-burning stars retaining thin hydrogen envelopes after a partial CE ejection, appearing as single or members of wide binaries from hierarchical triples.

Conclusion: The merger channel (failed common envelope) can produce intermediate-mass stripped stars, adding to the diversity of formation pathways for these objects.

Abstract: Stripped stars are a common product of binary stellar systems and span a wide mass range from Wolf-Rayet stars to hot subdwarfs and helium white dwarfs. The recent discovery of intermediate-mass stripped stars, with masses between those of WRs and subdwarfs, provides a continuous evolutionary sequence and valuable test beds for binary interaction models. Population synthesis studies explain the formation of stripped products through stable mass transfer or common envelope evolution. Recently, however, a merger scenario (or, "failed common envelope") between a stripped star and a post-main sequence companion was suggested to explain the formation of the magnetic intermediate-mass stripped star HD 45166. In this Letter, we investigate this channel using detailed interacting binary simulations at Galactic metallicity and model their stripped merger products. We find that these merger products can contribute to the population of stripped stars across the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram and produce a blue-straggler effect, with merger masses spanning from 6 to 14 solar masses. These objects would be consistent with long-lived core He-burning stars retaining thin hydrogen envelopes after a partial CE ejection, and they would appear as single or members of wide binaries that were originally hierarchical triples.

### [2] [The first 3D MHD core-collapse progenitors I: General properties, convection and nuclear burning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22927)
*Adam Griffiths,Miguel-Ángel Aloy,Martin Obergaulinger*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper presents the first 3D MHD simulations of pre-supernova Wolf–Rayet progenitors, showing that turbulent velocities in oxygen-burning shells exceed mixing-length theory predictions by ~2×, while thin silicon-burning shells exhibit reduced mixing and non-standard diffusion profiles, significantly impacting nuclear burning efficiency and spatial extent.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The 3D interior structure (magnetic fields, angular momentum, convective asphericities) of rapidly rotating magnetised core-collapse supernova progenitors remains poorly constrained; realistic multidimensional models are lacking for subsequent collapse and explosion calculations.

Method: 3D MHD simulations using Aenus-ALCAR of two compact Wolf–Rayet progenitors from GENEC and MESA stellar evolution codes, mapped into 3D minutes before collapse and evolved until core-collapse onset.

Result: Extended oxygen-burning shells: turbulent velocities ~2× MLT predictions. Thin silicon-burning shells: mixing reduced near boundaries, effective diffusion profile deviates from 1D prescription. These differences alter nuclear burning efficiency and spatial extent.

Conclusion: Multidimensional effects significantly modify turbulent mixing and shell burning in final stages of massive-star evolution; proposed prescriptions to account for these effects in advanced stellar evolution phases; models suitable for subsequent collapse and explosion simulations.

Abstract: The most energetic core-collapse supernovae are thought to arise from rapidly rotating, magnetised progenitors, yet the three-dimensional structure of their pre-collapse interior remains poorly constrained, and realistic distributions of magnetic fields, angular momentum, and convective asphericities are still lacking. We construct physically consistent three-dimensional pre-supernova progenitors including rotation and magnetic fields. In this first paper, we focus on the behaviour of turbulence and nuclear burning in the shells surrounding the stellar core, and assess their deviations from one-dimensional stellar-evolution models. We used Aenus-ALCAR to perform three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations of two compact Wolf--Rayet progenitors obtained from the stellar evolution codes GENEC and MESA. The models were mapped into the multidimensional domain several minutes before collapse and evolved until the onset of core collapse. We find that in extended oxygen-burning shells, turbulent velocities exceed the standard mixing-length-theory (MLT) predictions by approximately a factor of two. In contrast, a thin silicon-burning shell is poorly described by MLT: mixing is reduced near both shell boundaries, and the inferred effective diffusion profile departs significantly from the standard one-dimensional prescription. These differences directly affect the spatial extent and efficiency of nuclear burning. We present the first 3D MHD pre-supernova progenitors of this kind, suitable for subsequent collapse and explosion calculations, and show that multidimensional effects can significantly modify turbulent mixing and shell burning during the final stages of massive-star evolution. We propose prescriptions to account for these effects in the advanced phases of stellar evolution.

### [3] [Ensemble asteroseismology: An ensemble approach to detecting signatures of solar-like oscillations in K-dwarfs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23515)
*William J. Chaplin,Tiago L. Campante,Mikkel N. Lund,Martin B. Nielsen,Guy R. Davies,Emily Hatt,Rachel Howe,Amalie Stokholm*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The authors propose an ensemble strategy for PLATO data to detect solar-like oscillations in hundreds of K-dwarfs by combining weighted frequency power spectra, enabling measurements of granulation, magneto-convection, and magnetic activity effects, even though individual oscillation frequencies cannot be extracted.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Solar-like oscillations have been detected in only a handful of K-type dwarfs from Kepler, TESS, and ground-based RV observations; PLATO will also only add a modest number of individual detections, motivating a new ensemble approach to probe this regime more broadly.

Method: Weighted combination of frequency power spectra across hundreds of K-dwarfs in constrained effective temperature ranges; discrete numerical predictions using PLATO Input Catalogue data; derivation of a simple analytical approximation for quick predictions.

Result: Predictions indicate PLATO can provide solid ensemble detections well into the K-dwarf regime, significantly improving oscillation detectability compared to individual-star approaches.

Conclusion: PLATO offers an ideal opportunity to exploit the ensemble strategy for studying oscillations, granulation, and magnetic activity in K-dwarfs, complementing individual-star detection methods.

Abstract: Solar-like oscillations have to date been observed in hundreds of main-sequence and sub-giant stars. However, only a handful of detections have been made in K-type dwarfs, using ground-based extreme precision radial velocity observations and space-based photometric observations made by the NASA \emph{Kepler} and TESS missions. Whilst the upcoming ESA PLATO Mission promises to add to these individual detections, it will do so only in a similar, modest number of stars. Here, we propose a new ensemble strategy to exploit the PLATO data, in which frequency power spectra on hundreds of K-dwarfs lying in constrained ranges of effective temperature are combined in a weighted manner to significantly improve the detectability of the oscillations. Whilst this approach means it is not possible to extract usable constraints on individual oscillation frequencies, it provides a way to detect and measure the characteristics of the composite envelope of oscillation power given by the ensemble, which in turn provides diagnostics of granulation and magneto-convection and the impact of magnetic activity on the modes. We use data in the PLATO Input Catalogue (PIC) to make discrete numerical predictions of the detectability of the ensemble spectra. We also derive a simple analytical approximation of our method that obviates the need to perform numerical calculations over a discrete sample of targets, and which serves as a useful tool to make quick predictions for other future or planned missions. Our predictions indicate that PLATO has the potential to provide solid ensemble detections well into the K-dwarf regime. In summary, PLATO offers an ideal opportunity to exploit this new approach.

</details>
