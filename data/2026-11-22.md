  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The RAR model explains galactic dark matter as a degenerate fermion core plus classical halo, but earlier Milky Way constraints on the fermion mass (48–345 keV, with S-stars favoring 56 keV) conflict with a newer thermodynamic stability analysis permitting only ~194–387 keV. Conclusion: The RAR fermionic dark matter model can account for M31's central compact object and rotation curve with masses below ~60 keV, but consistency with the Milky Way and the thermodynamic stability constraint narrows the viable window to 48–60 keV (or excludes a fermion-core explanation of M31 entirely under the thermodynamic range).   <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The abundance of UV-bright galaxies at cosmic dawn is unexpected, and a top-heavy stellar initial mass function (IMF) has been proposed as an explanation. The authors test this hypothesis quantitatively.

Abstract: A top-heavy stellar initial mass function (IMF) has been proposed to explain the unexpected abundance of ultraviolet (UV)-bright galaxies at cosmic dawn. We compare this IMF with a Salpeter IMF, self-consistently including the associated changes in UV emission, supernova feedback, metal enrichment, and dust production. Thus, a top-heavy IMF alone cannot explain the bright galaxy excess. Conversely, the predicted pair-instability supernova abundance differs by factors of $2$-$6$ between the two IMFs, providing a potentially cleaner observational diagnostic of the high-mass stellar populations at these early epochs.

### [38] [Tracing Magnetic Helicity in Interplanetary Space. A Multi-Spacecraft Analysis of the 2024 March 23 ICME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26455)

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Tracking the magnetic helicity of a March 2024 ICME from the solar corona through four radially aligned spacecraft shows a negative-helicity flux rope with a helicity budget of about −2×10^41 Mx² that is roughly conserved from Sun to Earth, though in-situ estimates depend strongly on spacecraft crossing geometry.

Motivation: Understanding whether and how the magnetic helicity of an interplanetary coronal mass ejection (ICME) is conserved from the corona into the heliosphere is a key test of CME/flux-rope evolution. The rare availability of multiple radially aligned spacecraft for a single event provides an opportunity to track helicity across the Sun–Earth journey.

Method: The authors studied an eruptive X1.1 flare/CME from NOAA AR 13614 (2024 March 23, 00:58 UT). Coronal helicity was inferred from magnetogram data using non-linear force-free (NLFF) modeling. In-situ helicity was derived by fitting force-free flux-rope models (Lundquist, Gold-Hoyle) to ICME magnetic-field signatures measured by four radially aligned spacecraft—Solar Orbiter (0.39 AU), BepiColombo (0.58 AU), STEREO-A (0.96 AU), and Wind (0.99 AU). The Gold-Hoyle-based expansion-modified model (3DCORE) was used to reconstruct the ICME and its heliospheric propagation. Estimates from each model and spacecraft were then compared with the coronal values.

Result: NLFF modeling reveals a left-handed (negative helicity) flux rope oriented along a slightly NE–SW axis, consistent with H-alpha and SDO/AIA observations, yielding a helicity budget of −2.16 ± 1.32 × 10^41 Mx². In-situ methods give comparable values (−2.0 ± 1.0 × 10^41 Mx²) but are highly sensitive to the spacecraft's crossing position through the ICME. The 3DCORE results are the most consistent across all four spacecraft and with the coronal value.

Conclusion: The event's magnetic helicity is approximately conserved from the corona to 1 AU, with a consistently negative sign. While classical force-free fits are sensitive to crossing geometry, the expansion-modified 3DCORE reconstruction provides the most robust and self-consistent helicity estimates across spacecraft and against the AR-based coronal budget.

Abstract: We study the evolution of the magnetic helicity content of an ICME event by inferring its magnetic-field-related quantities at different stages between the Sun and Earth using remote sensing and in-situ observations. We study an eruptive X1.1 flare/CME event and its associated ICME originating from NOAA active region (AR) 13614 on 2024 March 23 00:58 UT. Using magnetogram data of the AR during the flaring event, along with non-linear force-free (NLFF) magnetic field modeling, we reconstruct the magnetic field in the solar corona and analyze its magnetic helicity budget. The helicity is also tracked in-situ in the heliosphere using four well radially-aligned spacecraft (Solar Orbiter at 0.39 AU, BepiColombo at 0.58 AU, STEREO-A at 0.96 AU and Wind at 0.99 AU) measuring the ICME magnetic field signature. We use different force-free magnetic field models (Lundquist, Gold-Hoyle) to evaluate the helicity values at each spacecraft. With the Gold-Hoyle-based model (expansion-modified force-free model, 3DCORE), we also reconstruct the ICME and its propagation in the heliosphere. We ultimately compare the results obtained for each model and spacecraft to the values computed in the corona to give a general understanding of how the event's magnetic field evolved. The NLFF modeling of the AR indicates a left-handed (negative helicity) flux rope (FR) positioned along a slightly northeast-southwest axis, in agreement with chromospheric and coronal observations (H-alpha, SDO/AIA), and yields a helicity budget computed of $-2.16\pm1.32\times10^{41}~\text{Mx}^2$. The helicity inferred from in-situ modeling methods reveals values in a similar range ($-2.0\pm1.0\times10^{41}~\text{Mx}^2$), but is highly sensitive to the spacecraft crossing position through the ICME. It appears that the results given by 3DCORE are more consistent between all spacecraft, as well as with the values inferred at the AR.
