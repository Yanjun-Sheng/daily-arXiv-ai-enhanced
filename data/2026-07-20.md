### [1] [A JWST, ALMA and VLA survey of the Ophiuchus-A star-forming region: Unveiling hidden dust mass and connecting infrared outflows to their radio origins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15468)
*Isaac C. Radley,John D. Ilee,Gemma Busquet,Hauyu Baobab Liu,Klaus M. Pontoppidan,Alvaro Ribas,Marc Audard,Eleonora Bianchi,Tyler L. Bourke,Claudio Codella,Audrey Coutens,Josep M. Girart,Melvin G. Hoare,Izaskun Jiménez-Serra,Doug Johnstone,Laurent Loinard,Olja Panić,Jaime E. Pineda,Linda Podio,John J. Tobin,David J. Wilner*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper presents a multi-wavelength (infrared to radio) survey of 20 young stellar objects in Ophiuchus A, using VLA, JWST, and ALMA data. The authors model dust and ionised gas properties, finding circumstellar dust disks tens to hundreds of times more massive than millimetre-only estimates, with millimetre-sized grains present even at Class 0 stages. They detect protostellar jet emission and propose a solution to the 'missing disk mass' problem, though resolution at <40 GHz remains limited.

Motivation: To address the 'missing disk mass' problem—where millimetre-only observations underestimate disk masses—by combining multi-wavelength data (infrared, millimetre, radio) to derive more accurate dust and ionised gas properties in young stellar objects.

Method: A homogeneous survey of 20 Class 0-III YSOs in Ophiuchus A L1688, combining high-resolution VLA (7-25 au) and JWST observations with archival ALMA data. Physically motivated models were used to fit the millimetre-centimetre radio SED to derive dust and ionised gas properties, spectral indices, and relative contributions.

Result: Circumstellar dust disks are tens to hundreds of times more massive than millimetre-only estimates (with uncertainties from dust opacity choices). Millimetre-sized grains are present even at Class 0. Protostellar jet emission was detected on scales of 10s–1000s au, enabling outflow-origin connections.

Conclusion: The results offer a potential solution to the 'missing disk mass' problem via multi-wavelength characterisation. However, resolution and sensitivity at <40 GHz remain limiting. Future facilities (SKA, ngVLA) are needed to fully resolve emission at <0.18" even in nearby star-forming regions.

Abstract: We present an infrared, millimetre, and radio survey of 20 Class 0-III young stellar objects in the Ophiuchus A L1688 star-forming cluster, combining high-resolution (7-25 au) VLA and JWST observations with archival ALMA data. We implement physically motivated models to derive dust and ionised gas properties, spectral behaviour and their relative contributions through the millimetre-centimetre radio spectral energy distribution. Our models reveal circumstellar dust disks that are, on average, tens to hundreds of times more massive than millimetre-only estimates (subject to uncertainties arising from the choice of dust opacity) and contain millimetre-sized grains even at the Class 0 stage. Owing to the VLA's high resolution we are able to connect outflows to their origins, detecting protostellar jet emission on scales of 10s-1000s au. Our results represent a homogeneous characterisation of the dust and ionised gas properties in Ophiuchus and present a potential solution to the long-standing 'missing disk mass' problem. However, our understanding is still limited by resolution and sensitivity at frequencies <40 GHz. Future facilities like the SKA and ngVLA are needed to provide the necessary capabilities to fully spatially resolve this emission (<0.18") even in one of the closest star-forming regions.

### [2] [Infrared spectroscopy of gas-phase hydrogenated and methylated pyrenes: from laboratory spectra to the simulated 3.4 $μ$m emission band](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16018)
*Karine Demyk,Christine Joblin,Louan de Bentzmann,Dominique Toublanc,Giacomo Mulas*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper derives synthetic mid-IR emission spectra for aliphatic-containing pyrene derivatives (dihydropyrene, tetrahydropyrene, hexahydropyrene, methylpyrene) from gas-phase laboratory measurements (373–673 K) and Monte Carlo emission modeling. Comparing with JWST/PDRs4All observations of the Orion Bar, they identify 1,2,3,6,7,8-hexahydropyrene as the carrier of the 3.403 μm component of the 3.4 μm band, with 1-methylpyrene potentially contributing to the underlying plateau.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To refine our understanding of the contribution of aliphatic-containing PAH-like species to the observed 3.4 μm aromatic infrared emission band by deriving state-of-the-art emission spectra and comparing them with astronomical observations.

Method: Gas-phase mid-IR spectra (1.4–25 μm) of pyrene and its hydrogenated/methylated derivatives were recorded at 373–673 K. Band profiles were analyzed via multi-component fitting, empirical anharmonicity laws were derived, and Monte Carlo emission modeling simulated UV-excited emission spectra up to ~6 eV. Synthetic spectra were compared with JWST observations of the Orion Bar (PDRs4All).

Result: 1,2,3,6,7,8-hexahydropyrene is identified as the carrier of the 3.403 μm red component of the 3.4 μm band; 1-methylpyrene may contribute to the underlying emission plateau but lacks a sufficiently strong infrared band for clear detection.

Conclusion: Aliphatic-rich hexahydropyrene is a viable candidate for the 3.4 μm satellite emission feature. Experimental data and temperature-dependent analyses are publicly available in the cosmicPAH-IRDB database.

Abstract: Observations of the aromatic infrared emission band at 3.3 $μ$m often reveal satellite emission features in the 3.4 - 3.6 $μ$m range. While the 3.3 $μ$m band is attributed to the CH stretching vibration of polycylic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), the satellite bands - particularly its prominent 3.4 $μ$m component - is assigned to aliphatic CH stretching vibrations in hydrogenated and methylated PAH-like species. Our aim is to derive state-of-the-art infrared emission spectra for aliphatic-containing pyrene derivatives and compare them with astronomical observations. This will help refine our understanding of the contribution of these species to the 3.4 $μ$m emission band. Mid-infrared spectra (1.4-25 $μ$m) of gas-phase dihydropyrene, tehtrahydropyrene, hexahydropyrene methylated pyrene, and pyrene were recorded at temperatures ranging from 373 to 673 K, depending on the species. The band profiles were analyzed using a multi-component fitting tool, and empirical anharmonicity laws were derived to quantify the evolution of the band positions and widths with temperature. The obtained spectral data was combined with the results of a Monte Carlo emission model to simulate the emission spectra following UV-photon absorption, up to the dissociation limit ($\lesssim$6 eV). The resulting synthetic spectra were compared with James Webb Space Telescope observations of the Orion Bar region (PDRs4All program). Based on these state-of-the-art simulated spectra, we propose 1,2,3,6,7,8-hexahydropyrene as the carrier of the red component of the 3.4 $μ$m band observed at 3.403 $μ$m. While 1-methylpyrene may also contribute to the underlying emission plateau, its lack of a strong infrared band complicates detection in observed spectra, unlike hexahydropyrene. All experimental spectra and their temperature-dependent analyses are available in the new cosmicPAH-IRDB database.
