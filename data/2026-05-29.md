### [1] [A Deep Study of the Spiral Galaxy W2246f](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29014)
*Evelyn J. Johnston,Sorya Lambert,Aparna Nair,Alejandra Z. Lugo-Aranda,Manuel Aravena,Roberto J. Assef,Tanio Diaz Santos,Román Fernández Aranda,Hyunsung D. Jun,Guodong Li,Mai Liao,Devika Shobhana,Chao-Wei Tsai*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: A deep MUSE study of spiral galaxy W2246f (z~0.09) reveals a cLIER galaxy with a retired, old metal-poor stellar population in the central kpc (powered by hot evolved stars) while the disc hosts ongoing star formation, consistent with inside-out quenching.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To recover the lost beauty of individual galaxy details in the era of large surveys, and to understand the formation and evolution of the spiral galaxy W2246f through unusually deep, spatially-resolved IFU observations.

Method: MUSE IFU observations; analysis of stellar and gas kinematics, spatially-resolved stellar populations (luminosity- and mass-weighted ages/metallicities), ionized gas properties (E(B-V), Hα extinction, dust-corrected Hα flux, O3N2 oxygen abundance, Hα-based SFR), and WHAN/WHaD diagnostic diagrams.

Result: Negative metallicity gradient; flat mass-weighted ages across disc but negative luminosity-weighted age gradient; central region shows lower gas metallicity, lower SFR density, older luminosity-weighted stellar populations; central region classified as retired (LIER) while disc is star-forming.

Conclusion: W2246f is a cLIER galaxy with a central kpc dominated by old, metal-poor stars and LIER emission from hot evolved stars, while the disc undergoes ongoing star formation. This supports an inside-out quenching scenario.

Abstract: In this era of large surveys and statistical studies of galaxies, the beauty in the details of individual galaxies is often lost. We present a deep study of the spiral galaxy W2246f with MUSE, exploring the spatially-resolved stellar and ionized gas properties to understand how it formed and evolved over time. The unusually deep observations of this galaxy give us a rare opportunity to study this phenomenon with better spatial resolution than can normally be achieved with the current IFU surveys of galaxies at a similar redshift ($z\sim0.09$). We analyse the stellar and gas kinematics, as well as the spatially resolved stellar populations and gas properties, including gas metallicity and the dominant ionization sources. The derived properties include the stellar mass, radial profiles of luminosity- and mass-weighted mean ages and metallicities, and ionized gas characteristics such as E(B-V), H$α$ extinction, dust-corrected H$α$ flux, oxygen abundance using the O3N2 calibrator, and H$α$-based star formation rate. Analysis of the stellar populations revealed a negative metallicity gradient, and the mass-weighted ages showed uniformly flat ages across the disc while the luminosity-weighted ages show a negative gradient. We find that the gas metallicity and star formation rate density also drop in the central region of the galaxy where the older luminosity-weighted stellar populations are found. Analysis of the WHAN and WHaD diagrams reveal that in fact the central region is retired while the rest of the disc is star forming. We conclude that W2246f is a nice example of a cLIER galaxy, where the central kpc is dominated by old, metal-poor stars with little star formation. The central LIER emission is primarily powered by hot evolved stars, while the rest of the disc displays ongoing star formation. These findings are consistent with a scenario of inside-out quenching.

### [2] [Modeling Solitonic Cores, Stabilization of Bar, and Suppression of Bar Dissolution in DDO 168 via GPP Formalism: A Detailed Analysis of Bose--Einstein Condensate/Fuzzy Dark Matter Halo Structure and Bar Dynamics in the Dwarf Galaxy DDO 168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29314)
*Saroj Khanal,Sanjay Kumar Sah,Kiran Khanal,Sapana Khanal*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: The paper shows that the dwarf irregular galaxy DDO 168 can be consistently described by the Bose-Einstein condensate/fuzzy dark matter (BEC/FDM) framework. Using SPARC rotation curve data, the authors infer an ultralight axion mass of ~1.3×10⁻²³ eV, yielding a solitonic core of radius ~2.4 kpc and mass ~1.5×10⁹ M☉. The model reproduces the observed flat inner rotation curve, resolves the cusp-core problem, and allows long-lived weak stellar bars due to reduced dynamical friction.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The cusp-core problem challenges ΛCDM: dwarf galaxies exhibit flat central density cores, whereas collisionless N-body simulations predict steep cusps (ρ∝r⁻¹). The authors aim to test whether the BEC/FDM model can reconcile this discrepancy for the specific case of DDO 168.

Method: The authors model DDO 168 under the BEC/FDM framework, solving the Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson (GPP) equations for the solitonic ground state. They numerically validate the ground-state solution and fit the inner rotation curve using SPARC data, enforcing the soliton mass–radius relation. The axion mass and soliton core parameters are inferred from the fit.

Result: Best-fit axion mass m = (1.3⁺⁰·³₋₀·₂)×10⁻²³ eV; solitonic core radius R_c = 2.40⁺⁰·²⁴₋₀·₂₂ kpc; enclosed mass M(<2.47 kpc) ≈ (1.5±0.2)×10⁹ M☉. The flat inner rotation curve is reproduced, and the weak H I bar is compatible with multigigayear survival timescales due to reduced Chandrasekhar dynamical friction.

Conclusion: The BEC/FDM framework provides an internally consistent description of DDO 168, simultaneously reproducing the observed rotation curve, alleviating the cusp-core tension, and allowing long-lived weak bars under conservative dynamical assumptions.

Abstract: The cusp-core problem remains a challenge to the $Λ$CDM model, since dwarf galaxies often exhibit flat central density cores rather than the steep cusps ($ρ\propto r^{-1}$) predicted by collisionless $N$-body simulations. We model the dark-matter-dominated dwarf irregular galaxy DDO 168 within the Bose--Einstein condensate (BEC) or fuzzy dark matter (FDM) framework, in which ultralight bosons form a solitonic core governed by the Gross--Pitaevskii--Poisson (GPP) equations, with the soliton mass--radius relation enforced. We numerically validate the ground-state solution of the GPP system as a consistency check and fit the inner rotation curve of DDO 168 using SPARC data. Within this framework, the data are consistent with an axion mass \[ m = (1.3^{+0.3}_{-0.2}) \times 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{eV}, \] and yield a solitonic core with characteristic radius \[ R_c = 2.40^{+0.24}_{-0.22}\,\mathrm{kpc}, \] enclosing a mass \[ M(<2.47\,\mathrm{kpc}) \simeq (1.5 \pm 0.2)\times10^{9}\,M_\odot. \] The observed flat inner rotation curve is reproduced and the presence of a weak H\,I bar is compatible with multigigayear survival timescales, consistent with reduced Chandrasekhar dynamical friction in a shallow central potential. These results demonstrate that the BEC/FDM framework provides an internally consistent description of DDO 168, simultaneously reproducing the observed rotation curve, alleviating the cusp-core tension, and allowing long-lived weak bars under conservative dynamical assumptions.

TL;DR: Using MINOS far detector muon data, the Sun's cosmic ray shadow was observed over 13 years (solar min, max, and full period). Simulations using Parker spiral HMF and dipole GMF models showed the Parker spiral matches best at solar minimum and worst at solar maximum, with better fit for harder CR spectra than actual, suggesting need for improved HMF models.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To locate and analyze the Sun's CR muon shadow using long-baseline MINOS data across different solar activity periods, and test the Parker spiral HMF model's ability to reproduce observed shadow positions.

Method: Muon data from the MINOS far detector (Northern Minnesota) was used to locate the Sun's CR shadow across three periods (solar minimum, solar maximum, full 13-year span). Particle distributions were simulated using Parker spiral HMF and dipole GMF models, then compared with observed shadow positions relative to the Sun.

Result: Parker spiral model is most consistent with solar minimum shadow and least consistent with solar maximum shadow. The model fits better assuming a harder CR spectrum than is actually present. Modifications to the Parker spiral model affecting the Sun's CR shadow shift are discussed.

Conclusion: The simple Parker spiral HMF model is insufficient to fully explain the Sun's CR shadow, particularly at solar maximum. A more detailed HMF model is needed, and plausible modifications to the Parker spiral are suggested.

Abstract: Shadows cast in the cosmic ray (CR) muon sky by the Sun were located using muon data from the MINOS far detector in Northern Minnesota. The shadows were observed independently across three time periods; near solar minimum, near solar maximum, and over the entire 13 year span of the data. A distribution of muon positions for each shadow was then sampled to simulate CR motions near the Sun using the Parker spiral model of the Heliospheric Magnetic Field (HMF) and a dipole model of the Geomagnetic Field (GMF). The resulting particle distributions were then compared to their position with respect to the Sun. Results show that the Parker spiral model is most consistent with the solar minimum shadow and least consistent with the solar maximum shadow, as expected. The simple Parker spiral is more consistent with the data for a harder CR spectrum than is actually present, indicating the need for a more detailed HMF model. Plausible modifications to the Parker spiral model which would affect the overall shift of the Sun's CR shadow are discussed.

### [4] [Stellar Population Astrophysics (SPA) with the TNG. CNO abundances in 28 open clusters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29666)
*Bruno Ćurjurić,Arnas Drazdauskas,Gražina Tautvaišienė,Angela Bragaglia,Natalia Alvarez-Baena,Valentina D'Orazi,Marina Dal Ponte*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper presents C, N, and O abundances for 88 giants in 28 open clusters and 2 stars in Theia 1214, derived from high-resolution spectra. The study finds that precise age dating requires separate [C/N] calibrations for first-ascent giants and red clump stars, and reports a flatter [C/N]-age relation for young stars compared to previous work, while also concluding the two Theia 1214 stars are likely field stars rather than part of NGC 752.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To enlarge the pool of open clusters with measured C, N, and O abundances in evolved giants, thereby advancing the use of chemical clocks (elemental abundance ratios) for stellar age determinations.

Method: High-resolution spectra were analyzed using a differential model atmosphere method. Carbon abundances were derived from spectral synthesis of C2 band heads at 5135 and 5635.5 Å. Nitrogen abundances were determined from CN features at 6470-6490 Å. Oxygen abundances were determined from the [O I] line at 6300 Å.

Result: C, N, and O abundances were provided for 88 giants in 28 open clusters and 2 stars in Theia 1214. Results show that observed C/N ratios in first-ascent giants with larger turn-off masses are slightly higher than standard model predictions. The [C/N]-age relation is flatter in the young age regime than in previous studies. The two Theia 1214 stars likely do not belong to NGC 752 and are probably field stars.

Conclusion: Precise age dating requires separate [C/N] age calibrations for first-ascent red giant branch stars and red clump stars to account for abundance changes after the luminosity bump. The study provides updated empirical relations that differ from standard models, particularly for younger populations.

Abstract: Aims. Our main aim with this work was to enlarge the pool of open clusters with determined carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen abundances in evolved giants to further advance chemical clocks in stellar age determinations. Methods. High-resolution spectra were analysed using a differential model atmosphere method. Carbon abundances were derived using spectral synthesis of the C2 band heads at 5135 and 5635.5 Å. The CN features at 6470-6490 Å were analysed to determine the abundances of nitrogen. The oxygen abundances were determined from the [O I] line at 6300 Å. Results. We provide abundances of C, N, and O for 88 giants in 28 open clusters and in two stars of the association Theia 1214. The results were compared with theoretical predictions and used for the analysis of the [C/N] relations with age, and we investigated the origin of the two Theia 1214 stars. Conclusions. Precise age dating requires separate age calibrations of [C/N] ratios for the first-ascent giants of the lower part of the red giant branch and for the red clump stars to account for the additional abundance alterations during the post-red giant branch luminosity bump evolution. In the first-ascent giant stars with larger turn-off masses, the observed C/N ratios are slightly higher than those predicted by standard models, and the obtained [C/N] versus age relation is flatter in the young age regime than in previous studies. We doubt that the two stars we investigated in the Theia 1214 association belong to the tail of NGC 752. Most probably, they are field stars.
