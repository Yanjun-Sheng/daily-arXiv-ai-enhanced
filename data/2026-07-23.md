### [1] [Large Magellanic Cloud Globular Clusters in the Near-infrared. I. RR Lyrae in Reticulum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19475)
*Anupam Bhardwaj,Susmita Das,Prashant Nishad,Marina Rejkuba,Giulia De Somma,Marcella Marconi,Chow-Choong Ngeow,Vincenzo Ripepi,Sarang Shah,Subhajit Kar,Shashi Kanbur,Matteo Monelli,Massimo Dall'Ora*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper presents homogeneous multi-epoch NIR (JHKs) observations of 32 RR Lyrae variables in the Reticulum globular cluster using Gemini South/Flamingos-2. Using light-curve templates, tight empirical period-luminosity relations are derived with ~0.05 mag dispersion. Adopting PLZ calibrations, a true distance modulus of μ₀ = 18.472 ± 0.035 mag is obtained, placing Reticulum near the LMC barycentric distance and establishing it as a potential anchor for the Population II distance ladder.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Reticulum is an old, metal-poor, sparsely populated LMC globular cluster with negligible metallicity spread and low reddening, making it an ideal laboratory for testing stellar pulsation models and calibrating Population II distance indicators. The authors aim to provide precise NIR characterization of its RR Lyrae population to derive accurate distances.

Method: Multi-epoch NIR (JHKs) observations of RR Lyrae stars in Reticulum were obtained with the Flamingos-2 imager on the 8.1-m Gemini South Telescope. NIR light-curve templates were used to derive intensity-averaged magnitudes and peak-to-peak amplitudes for 32 RR Lyrae stars (22 RRab, 4 RRc, 6 RRd). Empirical JHKs period-luminosity relations were constructed, and distance moduli were derived using empirical and theoretical PLZ calibrations.

Result: The empirical JHKs PL relations are very tight with dispersions ~0.05 mag, comparable to Galactic globular clusters but with shallower slopes. The derived true distance modulus is μ₀ = 18.472 ± 0.035 mag, in excellent agreement with the precise geometric distance to the LMC, placing Reticulum close to the LMC barycentric distance.

Conclusion: The well-characterized RR Lyrae population and precise distance make Reticulum a potential anchor for calibrating the Population II distance ladder, contributing to improved extragalactic distance measurements.

Abstract: Reticulum is an old, metal-poor, and sparsely populated globular cluster in the outer regions of the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) and hosts a rich population of RR Lyrae stars. Being as close as possible to a single stellar population with negligible metallicity spread and low reddening, Reticulum is an ideal laboratory for testing stellar pulsation models and calibrating population II distance indicators. We present homogeneous multi-epoch near-infrared (NIR, JHKs) observations of RR Lyrae variables in Reticulum obtained with the Flamingos-2 imager on the 8.1-m Gemini South Telescope. Using NIR light-curve templates, we derive accurate intensity-averaged magnitudes and peak-to-peak amplitudes for 32 RR Lyrae stars, including 22 fundamental-mode (RRab), 4 first-overtone (RRc), and 6 mixed-mode (RRd) pulsators. The empirical JHKs period-luminosity (PL) relations of Reticulum RR Lyrae are very tight, exhibiting dispersions (~0.05 mag) comparable to those observed in Galactic globular clusters. The derived PL slopes are shallower than those reported for Galactic cluster variables. Adopting recent empirical and theoretical period-luminosity-metallicity (PLZ) calibrations based on Galactic globular clusters and pulsation models, we derive a true distance modulus of $μ_0 = 18.472 \pm 0.035$ mag to Reticulum. This cluster distance is in excellent agreement with the precise geometric distance to the LMC and places Reticulum close to the LMC barycentric distance. The well-characterized RR Lyrae population and a precise distance make Reticulum a potential anchor for calibrating Population II distance ladder.

### [2] [The GOLIATH Survey: OVI Absorption Reveals CGM Evolution through the Starburst-to-Quiescent Transition in Massive Galaxies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19473)
*Derick Flores,Rongmon Bordoloi,Jason Tumlinson,J. Christopher Howk,Nicolas Lehner,Mary Rickel,Benjamin Oppenheimer,Joseph Burchett,Ahmed Shaban,John O'Meara,Andrew Fox,J. Xavier Prochaska,Simon Xinlin Wu,Jack Higginson,Robert A. Simcoe*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: The GOLIATH survey examines OVI absorption in the inner circumgalactic medium (CGM) of massive (M⋆ ∼ 10¹¹ M☉), blue starburst/post-starburst galaxies at z≈0.43. OVI column density rises with stellar mass and specific star-formation rate, with the most intense star-formers showing the strongest OVI absorption. Star-forming galaxies have ~3× higher OVI column density, ~1.5 dex more CGM OVI mass, and ~2.6× higher covering fraction than quiescent galaxies. OVI line properties are consistent with feedback-driven radiative cooling (tcool ~10–100 Myr), requiring continuous feedback. Residual OVI in quiescent systems may come from hot ambient gas.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To characterize the warm-hot phase of the CGM in massive star-forming and post-starburst galaxies via OVI absorption, and to understand the role of feedback in driving the star-forming-to-quiescent transition.

Method: Analyzed OVI absorption in the inner halo (R/Rvir ≤ 0.6) of massive (log M⋆/M☉ ≈ 11), blue (u−r < 1.65) galaxies at z≈0.43 using the GOLIATH survey, measuring column densities, line widths, covering fractions, and OVI mass.

Result: OVI column density rises ~1 dex from log M⋆/M☉~8 to ~11.5 and increases with sSFR. Highest-sSFR galaxies show log N_OVI ≥ 15 cm⁻². Star-forming galaxies exceed quiescent ones by ~3× in OVI column density, ~1.5 dex in OVI mass, and have ~62.5% vs 24% covering fraction. Cooling times are ~10–100 Myr.

Conclusion: OVI traces feedback-driven radiative cooling on short timescales and probes the star-forming–quiescent transition at log M⋆/M☉ ≳ 11. Continuous feedback is needed to sustain the OVI reservoir; residual OVI in quiescent galaxies may arise from hot ambient gas.

Abstract: We present the GOLIATH survey (Galaxies, Outflows, and the Lifecycle of Immense, Active, Transforming Halos), a study of the multiphase circumgalactic medium (CGM) of massive ($\langle\log M_\star/M_\odot\rangle \approx 11$), blue ($u-r < 1.65$) starburst and post-starburst galaxies at $\langle z\rangle \approx$ 0.43. This work characterizes the warm-hot CGM through OVI absorption in the inner halo ($R/R_{\rm vir} \leq 0.6$) of these rare systems. Across the star-forming population, OVI column density rises by nearly 1~dex from $\log M_\star/M_{\odot} \sim 8$ to $\sim 11.5$ and increases with specific star-formation rate (sSFR). Two GOLIATH galaxies with the highest sSFR show the strongest CGM OVI absorption ($\log N_{\rm O\,VI}[\rm cm^{-2}] \gtrsim 15$). In the $\log M_\star/M_{\odot} = [11,12)$ inner-CGM region, massive star-forming galaxies exceed quiescent galaxies on average by a factor of $\sim 3$ in OVI column density and $\sim 1.5$~dex in CGM OVI mass ($\log(M_{\rm O\, VI}/M_\odot) \approx 7.3$ versus $\approx 5.8$), with covering fractions roughly three times higher (62.5% versus 24% at $\log N_{\rm O\,VI}[\rm cm^{-2}] \geq 14$). The OVI line widths and column densities are consistent with feedback-driven radiative cooling, in which outflow shocks heat the CGM and the gas cools back through the OVI window; the short cooling time, $t_{\rm cool} \sim 10$-$100$~Myr, requires continuous replenishment by active feedback to sustain this reservoir. The residual OVI in quiescent systems may arise from ambient gas at the high-temperature end of the cooling curve. OVI thus traces feedback on short timescales and probes the star-forming--quiescent transition at $\log M_\star/M_{\odot} \gtrsim 11$.

### [3] [Anharmonic Infrared Emission of Cyano-Substituted Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbon Molecules: Cyanonaphthalenes as a Case Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20015)
*Tao Chen,Kaijun Li,Aigen Li*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper presents anharmonic IR cascade emission spectra for 1- and 2-cyanonaphthalene (neutral, cation, anion) using B3LYP/N07D with VPT2, showing that charge states strongly modulate CN nitrile band intensity (anion enhanced ~10× vs. neutral) and that 1-CNN exhibits a red-wing in the 3.3-µm C-H stretch from peri-hydrogen interactions, providing quantitative tools to constrain cyano-PAH abundances for JWST analysis.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Cyano-substituted PAHs have been detected as tracers of nitrogen heterocycles in the ISM, but their IR identification requires anharmonic vibrational radiative models that account for dynamic cooling across diverse environments.

Method: B3LYP/N07D with Second-order Vibrational Perturbation Theory (VPT2) for anharmonic properties; optimized microcanonical sampling algorithm to calculate vibrational density of states and model environment-dependent cascade spectra for neutral, cationic, and anionic 1- and 2-cyanonaphthalene.

Result: Charge states strongly tune CN nitrile band: anion intensity enhanced ~10× vs. neutrals, cation slightly weaker. 1-CNN shows complex "red-wing" in 3.3-µm C-H stretch due to peri-hydrogen interactions; 2-CNN yields more symmetric profile. Fractional energy emitted via CN stretch provides quantitative tool to constrain cyano-PAH abundances.

Conclusion: By bridging laboratory benchmarks with dynamic interstellar emission, this work delivers accurate anharmonic fingerprints to guide JWST observational analysis of cyano-PAHs in the ISM.

Abstract: Recent detections of cyano-substituted polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (cyano-PAHs) highlight them as key tracers of nitrogen heterocycles in the interstellar medium (ISM). However, their infrared (IR) identification requires anharmonic vibrational radiative models that account for dynamic cooling across diverse environments. We provide IR cascade emission spectra for neutral, cationic, and anionic 1- and 2-cyanonaphthalene (1- and 2-CNN) under representative astrophysical conditions, investigating how charge states and substitution positions modulate anharmonic profiles and energy redistribution during IR cascades. Using B3LYP/N07D with Second-order Vibrational Perturbation Theory (VPT2), we computed anharmonic properties and applied an optimized microcanonical sampling algorithm to calculate vibrational density of states and model environment-dependent cascade spectra. Results show that charge states strongly tune the CN nitrile band: anion intensity is enhanced by an order of magnitude compared to neutrals, whereas cation intensity is slightly weaker. 1- and 2-CNN present distinct isomeric signatures: 1-CNN displays a complex "red-wing" structure in the 3.3-micron C-H stretch due to peri-hydrogen interactions, whereas 2-CNN yields a more symmetric profile. We evaluated the fractional energy emitted via the CN stretch relative to total cascade emissivity across charge states and environments. Combined with observed CN fluxes, these fractions provide a quantitative tool to constrain cyano-PAH abundances. By bridging laboratory benchmarks with dynamic interstellar emission, this work delivers accurate anharmonic fingerprints to guide JWST observational analysis.
