### [1] [A Single Line-Emitter Identified in an Unbiased Search with JWST/NIRSpec: Multi-Object Coronagraphy for Slitless Spectroscopy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12553)
*Mason S. Huberty,Claudia Scarlata,Matthew J. Hayes,Alaina Henry,Danielle A. Berg,Vieri Cammelli,Richard Ellis,Anne E. Jaskot,Yu-Heng Lin,Vihang Mehta,Matilde Mingozzi,Pierluigi Monaco,Alberto Saldana-Lopez,Aayush Saxena,Jonathan C. Tan,Alice Young*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Using a new JWST NIRSpec slitless spectroscopy technique (MOCSS), a single strong emission line at 1.60 μm is detected in the Hubble Ultra Deep Field with no counterpart in deep imaging, implying an extreme equivalent width limit and challenging standard line identifications.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To search for faint emission-line objects in a wide field without contamination from bright low-redshift sources, enabling discovery of sources too faint for imaging detection.

Method: Multi-Object Coronagraphy for Slitless Spectroscopy (MOCSS) with G140M/F100LP: opening nearly all NIRSpec microshutter array shutters while closing those over known low-redshift objects, performing wide-field slitless spectroscopy. The line is confirmed across four dither positions and two position angles.

Result: A 1.60 μm emission line with flux [2.5±0.3]×10⁻¹⁸ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² and FWHM 13.2±2.0 Å, with no counterpart in the deepest HST and JWST imaging. An observed-frame equivalent width lower limit of 7620 Å is derived.

Conclusion: The line cannot be readily explained by [OII] at z=3.29, [OIII] at z=2.20, Hα at z=1.44, or Lyα at z=12.16; possible AGN and transient phenomena are discussed as explanations.

Abstract: We report the detection of a previously unknown object with a single emission line within the Hubble Ultra Deep Field, using a new JWST NIRSpec technique called Multi-Object Coronagraphy for Slitless Spectroscopy (MOCSS) with the G140M/F100LP observing mode. The MOCSS technique involves opening almost all the shutters on the NIRSpec microshutter array, while simultaneously closing the shutters that lie on top of known low redshift objects, facilitating a wide field slitless spectroscopic search without contamination from bright objects. This observational technique has enabled the detection of a strong emission line in four different dither positions and in two position angles, and thus cannot be artificial. This line is detected at 1.60 $\rm μm$ with a line flux of $[2.5\pm 0.3]\times10^{-18} \rm erg\ s^{-1} cm^{-2}$ and a FWHM of $13.2\pm2.0$Å, and does not correspond to any known source with some of the deepest HST and JWST imaging in existence. Based upon the absence of imaging detection, we place a lower limit on the observed frame equivalent width (EW) of 7620Å on this object, which strongly limits the number of possible lines that this line can be. Given the wavelength, an EW limit that is near the ceiling of expectation, and the lack of other lines in the bandpass, this line cannot be easily explained by [OII] at $z=3.29$, [OIII] at $z=2.20$, H$α$ at $z=1.44$, and Ly$α$ at $z=12.16$. We discuss possible AGN and transient phenomena that could explain the finding.

### [2] [When dust growth takes over: the origin of dust in post-reionization galaxies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12591)
*Omima Osman,Gabriella De Lucia,Andreas. L. Faisst,Lizhi Xie,Fabio Fontanot,Michaela Hirschmann*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Using the semi-analytic model GAEA, the authors show that ACJ galaxies' dust reservoirs in the post-reionization epoch require efficient interstellar-medium dust growth, with a transition to grain-growth-dominated dust formation around z~8-9, and that these galaxies favor overdense environments.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The post-reionization epoch (z~4-6) is a critical phase of rapid stellar mass assembly, metal enrichment, and dust build-up, but the physical processes driving such rapid dust enrichment remain uncertain.

Method: The authors use the semi-analytic galaxy formation model GAEA, which tracks galaxy assembly, chemical enrichment, and dust evolution. They select model galaxies matching the observed stellar masses, star formation rates, and metallicities of the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST (ACJ) sample, compare their dust properties with observed scaling relations, and trace enrichment histories and environments.

Result: The model reproduces the observed dust mass-stellar mass relation and the dust-to-stellar mass, dust-to-gas, and dust-to-metal ratios as functions of stellar mass and metallicity. Reproducing these constraints requires efficient dust growth in the interstellar medium; stellar dust production alone is insufficient even under optimistic assumptions. Stellar mass, star formation rate, and metallicity together predict galaxy dust content well. A transition around z~8-9 marks the point below which dust growth dominates dust formation. ACJ-like galaxies preferentially reside in locally overdense environments.

Conclusion: Dust build-up is rapid and coupled with stellar and metal enrichment in ACJ galaxies during the post-reionization era, with environment possibly contributing to their accelerated evolution.

Abstract: The post-reionization epoch ($z\sim4$-6) marks a critical phase in galaxy evolution, during which galaxies rapidly assemble their stellar mass, enrich their interstellar medium, and build up substantial dust reservoirs. The physical processes responsible for such rapid dust enrichment remain uncertain. We investigate the dust enrichment history of the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST (ACJ) sample using the semi-analytic galaxy formation model GAlaxy Evolution and Assembly (GAEA), which follows galaxy assembly, chemical enrichment, and dust evolution. We select model galaxies matching the observed stellar masses, star formation rates, and metallicities of the ACJ sample, compare their dust properties with observed scaling relations, and trace their enrichment histories and environments. The model reproduces the observed dust mass-stellar mass relation and the dust-to-stellar mass, dust-to-gas, and dust-to-metal ratios as functions of stellar mass and metallicity. Reproducing these constraints requires efficient dust growth in the interstellar medium; stellar dust production alone cannot account for the observed dust reservoirs, even under the most optimistic assumptions. The combination of stellar mass, star formation rate, and metallicity provides a good predictor of galaxy dust content. Our model suggests rapid dust build-up consistent with the previously noted rapid metal enrichment, with evidence for a transition around $z\sim8$-9 below which dust growth becomes the dominant dust formation channel. Finally, ACJ-like galaxies preferentially reside in locally overdense environments, suggesting that environment may have contributed to their accelerated evolution. Taken together, these results support rapid and coupled stellar, metal, and dust build-up in ACJ galaxies during the post-reionization era.
