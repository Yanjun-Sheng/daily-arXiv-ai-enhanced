### [1] [What's Missing in AGN Feedback? Lessons learnt from Magneticum, IllustrisTNG and Simba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21140)
*D. T. Mazengo,P. Popesso,I. Marini,L. M. Valenzuela,N. de Isídio,V. Toptun,Mirjana Pović,Petri Väisänen,J. M. Sunzu,R. -S. Remus,V. Biffi,K. Dolag,R. Davé,A. Fraser-McKelvie,A. Dev,S. Vladutescu-Zopp,A. Merloni,G. Ponti,C. Aydar,S. Shreeram,L. A. Kahinga,J. O. Chibueze,P. Privatus*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Three cosmological simulations (Magneticum, IllustrisTNG, SIMBA) cannot simultaneously reproduce observed galaxy star-forming fractions and hot gas content in halos. Simulations with strong AGN feedback match the fgas-Mh relation but overquench galaxies; weaker AGN feedback (IllustrisTNG) yields better galaxy properties but overpredicts hot gas masses. This reveals deficiencies in current subgrid feedback models regarding timing, location, and energy coupling.

Motivation: Accurately balancing gas reservoirs, star formation, and feedback across cosmic time remains a central challenge for galaxy formation models in modern hydrodynamical simulations. Pronounced discrepancies emerge in predictions for the hot gas content of dark matter halos.

Method: Examine three state-of-the-art cosmological simulations (Magneticum, IllustrisTNG, SIMBA) and confront their predictions with spatially resolved galaxy data from MaNGA and constraints on the fgas-Mh relation from eROSITA and SZ measurements.

Result: Magneticum and SIMBA match the observed fgas-Mh relation but predict an overabundance of quenched galaxies. IllustrisTNG implements weaker AGN feedback, yields more realistic star-forming fractions, but systematically overpredicts hot gas masses in massive groups and poor clusters.

Abstract: Accurately balancing gas reservoirs, star formation, and feedback across cosmic time remains a central challenge for galaxy formation models in modern hydrodynamical simulations. While different feedback prescriptions reproduce selected local galaxy properties with varying success, the most pronounced discrepancies emerge in predictions for the hot gas content of dark matter halos. We examine three state-of-the-art cosmological simulations: Magneticum, IllustrisTNG, and SIMBA, which struggle to simultaneously reproduce observed galaxy and halo gas properties in the local Universe. We confront their predictions with spatially resolved galaxy data from MaNGA and recent constraints on the hot gas mass fraction-halo mass (fgas-Mh) relation from eROSITA and Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) measurements. Reproducing the observed fgas-Mh relation requires strong active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback. However, such feedback often leads to excessive quenching in simulated galaxy populations. Magneticum and SIMBA match the observed gas fraction relation but predict an overabundance of quenched galaxies. In contrast, IllustrisTNG implements weaker AGN feedback, yielding more realistic star-forming fractions but systematically overpredicting hot gas masses in massive groups and poor clusters. Overall, these tensions indicate current feedback models remain incomplete, not only in the total energy injected but also in the timing, location, and coupling of this energy to the surrounding gas. Our results therefore highlight the need to revisit subgrid feedback prescriptions and develop more self-consistent models capable of simultaneously regulating galaxy growth and the thermodynamic properties of halo gas. Motivated by this discrepancy, a companion study will explore whether the feedback strengths required to match halo gas constraints inevitably lead to overquenching and distorted galaxy demographics.

### [2] [The Stellar Mass Function of Gas-Rich Galaxies and the Underlying $M_{\rm HI}-M_{\rm star}$ Scaling Relation in the Local Universe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21225)
*Tanya Tripty,Nishikanta Khandai,Saili Dutta,Khushi Sharma*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper estimates the Galaxy Stellar Mass Function (GSMF) for HI gas-rich galaxies using the ALFALFA catalog, recalibrating stellar masses with multi-band photometry. The HI-selected GSMF follows a single Schechter function. Gas-rich galaxies constitute ~33% of stellar mass density and ~39% of number counts in the local Universe. The red population is only ~18% by number but contributes ~54% of stellar mass. The bias-free M_HI-M_star relation is also derived.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To characterize the stellar mass distribution of HI gas-rich galaxies and understand their contribution to the overall galaxy population in the local Universe, using a purely HI-selected sample to avoid selection biases inherent in optical surveys.

Method: Uses the 100% ALFALFA catalog (~98% with SDSS counterparts), recalibrates optical stellar masses using UV+optical+IR data from GSWLC-2 with individual dust corrections, applies a non-parametric method for GSMF estimation, and compares HI-selected, optically-selected, and joint samples to correct for selection effects.

Result: The HI-selected GSMF is fit by a single Schechter function. Red gas-rich galaxies account for ~18% of number but ~54% of stellar mass. Gas-rich galaxies represent ~33% of total stellar mass density and ~39% of number counts. The bias-free M_HI-M_star relation is obtained.

Conclusion: HI-selected galaxies are a significant but minority component of the local galaxy population; red gas-rich galaxies are rare but massive; multi-band photometry improves stellar mass estimates; the derived M_HI-M_star relation is free from optical selection biases.

Abstract: We estimate the Galaxy Stellar Mass Function (GSMF) of HI gas-rich galaxies using the 100\% ALFALFA ($α$) catalog, $\sim 98\%$ of which have optical counterparts in the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) and a subset of them have counterparts in GALEX SDSS WISE Legacy Catalogue-2(GSWLC-2). We use the mass estimates from this subset which combines UV, optical and IR bands with individual dust corrections to recalibrate optical stellar mass estimates. We use a non-parametric method to estimate the GSMF of these gas-rich galaxies. The resulting, HI-selected GSMF is consistent with a single Schechter function with best-fit parameters $\left\{φ_* (10^{-3}\, h_{70}^{3}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}\,\mathrm{dex}^{-1}), \log_{10} (M_*/M_{\odot}) + 2\log_{10} h_{70}, α\right\} = \left\{2.30^{+0.12}_{-0.12}, \,10.83^{+0.01}_{-0.01},\, -1.14^{+0.02}_{-0.02}\right\} $. Additionally, the red and blue populations are each well described by a single Schechter function. After correcting for selection effects, we find that the red population accounts for only $\sim18\%$ of gas-rich galaxies by number, yet contributes $\sim54\%$ of the total stellar mass, with the blue population accounting for the rest. Using an optically selected sample and a joint optical-HI sample, we find gas-rich galaxies represent $\sim 33\%$ of the total stellar mass density and $\sim 39\%$ of the total galaxy number counts in the local Universe. We use the GSMF and the HI mass function (HIMF) of the HI-selected sample to obtain the $M_{\rm HI}-M_{\rm star}$ relation, which is free from selection bias.

TL;DR: This paper presents high-resolution (0.2", ~10 pc) ALMA observations of [CI] (³P₂-³P₁), CO (J=7-6), and 800-GHz continuum in the circumnuclear disks of Seyfert galaxies NGC 613 and NGC 1808, revealing neutral gas and dust distributions, excitation conditions, C/CO abundance ratios (~2 and ~0.6), and constraining the supermassive black hole mass in NGC 613 to ~2×10⁷ M☉.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate the distribution and physical conditions (density, temperature, C/CO ratio) of neutral gas and dust in the circumnuclear disks of nearby active galaxies at unprecedented high resolution (~10 pc), and to understand the gas kinematics and black hole masses.

Method: ALMA observations of [CI] (³P₂-³P₁), CO (J=7-6), and 800-GHz continuum at 0.2" resolution; LTE and non-LTE radiative transfer analysis of [CI] line ratios; simultaneous modeling of [CI] and multiple CO lines to constrain C/CO abundance, density, and kinetic temperature; kinematic analysis for SMBH mass estimation.

The maximum [CI]$({^3\mathrm{P}_2}\mathrm{-}{^3\mathrm{P}_1})/({^3\mathrm{P}_1}\mathrm{-}{^3\mathrm{P}_0})$ integrated-intensity ratio is $\approx1.1$ $(\mathrm{K~km~s}^{-1}$ scale) in the region of the torus, yielding an excitation temperature of $T_\mathrm{ex}\approx60~\mathrm{K}$ under the approximation of local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) and optically thin emission. C/CO tends to increase with $T_\mathrm{k}$ and decrease with $n_\mathrm{H_2}$ in the CND, which is consistent with models of photodissociation regions. Based on the kinematics of gas traced by [CI] $({^3\mathrm{P}_2}\mathrm{-}{^3\mathrm{P}_1})$ and CO $(J=7\mathrm{-}6)$, the mass of the supermassive black hole at the center of NGC 613 is estimated to be $\sim2\times10^7~M_\odot$.

*S. B. De Daniloff,D. Espada,S. Duarte Puertas,Y. K. González-Koda,G. Torres-Ríos,S. F. Sánchez,R. García-Benito,S. Verley,I. Pérez,M. Argudo-Fernández,A. Bongiovanni,M. Sánchez-Portal,U. Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This study examines resolved scaling relations (rSK, rMGMS, rSFMS) in 41 void galaxies using ALMA CO(1-0) and optical IFU data. The rMGMS is the tightest relation (σ=0.16 dex), least affected by galaxy-to-galaxy variations. The authors propose that rMGMS deviations trace long-term gravitational potential changes, while rSK and rSFMS deviations reflect rapid SFR variations, with void galaxies being particularly suitable for this distinction due to their isolation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Resolved scaling relations between SFR, molecular gas, and stellar mass are well-studied but have not been examined with emphasis on large-scale environments (LSEs), particularly the lowest-density void regions.

Method: Analyzed 41 void galaxies with high-resolution ALMA CO(1-0) and CAVITY optical IFU data at 2.5" (0.8-2.1 kpc) scales. Fitted resolved Schmidt-Kennicutt (rSK), resolved molecular gas main sequence (rMGMS), and resolved star-forming main sequence (rSFMS) relations.

Result: All three relations show similar parametrization to other environments, but rMGMS is the tightest (σ=0.16 dex) vs. rSK (0.21 dex) and rSFMS (0.24 dex). Galaxy-to-galaxy variations are a major source of scatter, but rMGMS is least affected.

Abstract: Scaling relations involving star formation rates (SFRs), molecular gas mass, and stellar mass are key to understand galaxy evolution, and have previously been explored at resolved scales. However, they have not been examined with particular emphasis on the large-scale environments (LSEs). In this work, we study the resolved Schmidt-Kennicutt relation (rSK), molecular gas main sequence (rMGMS) and star-forming main sequence (rSFMS) from a sample of 41 void galaxies (VGs) residing in the least dense regions of the Universe. Using high-resolution interferometric CO(1-0) data and optical IFU data from the ALMA CO-CAVITY and CAVITY surveys at scales of 2.5" (0.8-2.1 kpc), we study these relations for the full sample as well as for individual galaxies in voids. However, the rMGMS is the tightest of the three relations ($σ_{rMGMS}=0.16$ dex, $σ_{rSK}=0.21$ dex, and $σ_{rSFMS}=0.24$ dex), unlike in other samples. We find that a large source of deviations in the relations comes from galaxy-to-galaxy variations. However, the rMGMS is less affected by these variations. This distinction is particularly relevant for our sample of VGs because the 41 ALMA CO-CAVITY VGs are more isolated than galaxies in other LSEs, and therefore are less affected by events that can significantly alter the gas distribution or trigger SF on short time-scales. In this sense, the rMGMS is likely the most stable of these relations over time.

### [5] [Identifying and Distinguishing Quenching Galaxies with Spatially Resolved Star Formation in the Hubble Frontier Fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21560)
*Cameron Lawlor-Forsyth,Michael L. Balogh,Sean L. McGee,Gregory H. Rudnick*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This study analyzes 1,437 galaxies in the Hubble Frontier Fields, identifying 129 inside-out quenching galaxies and 70 outside-in quenching galaxies. Inside-out quenching galaxies are more massive and more prevalent in clusters, especially at high masses, while outside-in quenching shows no mass dependence and remains ≤10% of the non-quenched population in both cluster and field environments.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate the nature and prevalence of different quenching signatures (inside-out vs. outside-in) in galaxies using spatially resolved SED fitting and morphological metrics.

Method: Spatially resolved SED fitting with FAST++ was applied to 1,437 galaxies (M* ≥ 10^8 M⊙, SFR ≥ 10^−3 M⊙ yr^−1) in the Hubble Frontier Fields. Morphological metrics from prior work were used to quantify star formation distributions, and a k-nearest neighbors algorithm classified galaxies into inside-out or outside-in quenching pathways.

Result: 129 inside-out and 70 outside-in quenching galaxies were identified. Inside-out quenching galaxies are ~0.8 dex more massive than outside-in ones, and both populations are ~0.8 dex more massive in clusters than in the field. Inside-out galaxies are more common in clusters (106/129) vs. outside-in (25/70). The inside-out fraction strongly increases with cluster mass (~30% at high masses), while the outside-in fraction remains ≤10% independent of mass. Only massive inside-out galaxies show an increasing fraction with infall time.

Conclusion: Inside-out and outside-in quenching represent distinct pathways with different mass dependencies and environmental drivers. Inside-out quenching is strongly linked to cluster environment and galaxy mass, while outside-in quenching is less environmentally sensitive and mass-independent.

Abstract: We investigate the nature and prevalence of different quenching signatures for 1437 galaxies with $M_{*} \geqslant 10^{8}~\mathrm{M}_{\odot}$ and $\text{SFR} \geqslant 10^{-3}~M_{\odot}~\text{yr}^{-1}$ in the Hubble Frontier Fields through spatially resolved spectral energy distribution fitting with \texttt{FAST++}. We use the morphological metrics previously presented in our series of investigations to quantify the distribution of star formation, and use a $k$-nearest neighbors algorithm to classify quenching galaxies into different quenching pathways, including an inside-out pathway and an outside-in pathway. We find 129 galaxies have morphologies consistent with an inside-out quenching pathway, and 70 are consistent with an outside-in pathway. Inside-out quenching galaxies are $0.8^{+0.2}_{-0.1}~\text{dex}$ more massive compared to outside-in quenching galaxies, where both populations are more massive in clusters than the field, by $0.8^{+0.3}_{-0.1}~\text{dex}$. Inside-out quenching galaxies are found more often in clusters (106/129), compared to outside-in quenching galaxies (25/70). In clusters, the fraction of inside-out quenching galaxies strongly increases with mass, representing ${\sim}$30% of the non-quenched galaxy population at high masses. A milder evolution is seen in the field. The fraction of outside-in quenching galaxies is independent of mass, representing ${\lesssim}$10% of the non-quenched population, in both the cluster and field. We find no strong dependence for the fraction of any population with estimated infall time, except massive inside-out quenching galaxies, which increase in fraction with increasing infall time.

### [6] [Statistics on the population of distant substellar companions to nearby stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20673)
*Souleymane Ouedraogo,Étienne Artigau,Sié Zacharie Kam,Orlagh L. Creevey,Clémence Fontanive,Neil J. Cook,René Doyon,Fabrice Bado*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Using multi-epoch WISE imaging and Bayesian MCMC analysis on 2103 stars within 20 pc, we find that wide substellar companions (5–80 MJ, 1,000–20,000 AU) occur at a rate of ~1%, with mass and separation distributions consistent with stellar binaries, supporting their formation as a low-mass extension of binary systems rather than scaled-up close-in giant planets.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Wide substellar companions serve as important benchmarks for studying brown dwarf and giant planet formation and evolution, as their association with stellar primaries provides age and metallicity constraints unavailable for isolated objects.

Method: Multi-epoch WISE proper motion analysis of 2103 stars within 20 pc, sensitive to companions down to ~300 K at separations up to 20,000 AU; Bayesian MCMC analysis with power-law distributions to constrain companion mass and semi-major axis distributions while accounting for observational selection effects.

Result: α = −0.21⁺⁰·³¹₋₀.₂₇, β = −1.38 ± 0.32, occurrence rate 1.07⁺⁰·³¹₋₀.₂₄% for 5–80 MJ companions at 1,000–20,000 AU; occurrence increases toward smaller separations and more massive hosts.

Conclusion: Wide substellar companions are consistent with the low-mass extension of the stellar binary population rather than a direct extension of close-in giant planets, supported by the largest statistical sample to date.

Abstract: Context. Wide substellar companions provide important benchmarks for studying the formation and evolution of brown dwarfs and giant planets. Their association with stellar primaries also provides age and metallicity constraints that are difficult to obtain for isolated objects.
  Aims. We investigate the population of wide substellar companions to nearby main-sequence stars using proper motions derived from multi-epoch WISE imaging. Our sample includes 2103 stars within 20 pc and is sensitive to companions down to $\sim300$ K at separations up to 20,000 AU.
  Methods. We use a Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) analysis to constrain the companion mass and semi-major axis distributions while accounting for observational selection effects, including the reduced detectability of faint companions. We assume power-law distributions, $d^2n \propto M^αa^βdM da$.
  Results. We derive $α=-0.21^{+0.31}_{-0.27}$, $β=-1.38\pm0.32$, and an occurrence rate of $1.07^{+0.31}_{-0.24}\%$ for companions with masses of 5--80 $M_J$ at separations of 1,000--20,000 AU. The occurrence rate increases towards smaller separations and for more massive host stars.
  Conclusions. The inferred companion mass distribution is consistent with previous direct imaging surveys despite differences in target selection. The power-law distributions in both mass and separation also agree with those of stellar binaries. Our sample is about an order of magnitude larger than those of previous imaging surveys, providing the most robust statistical characterisation of wide substellar companions to date. These results support the interpretation that wide substellar companions represent the low-mass extension of the stellar binary population rather than a direct extension of close-in giant planets.
