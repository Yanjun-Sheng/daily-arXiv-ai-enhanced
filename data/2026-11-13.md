Motivation: To characterize the UV continuum slopes and dust attenuation of galaxies at z≳12 and test whether the attenuation-free regime extends to the highest redshifts, and to trace the redshift evolution of β across a wide range from z=4.

Method: Analysis of UV continuum slopes β for 55 galaxies at z≳12 selected from a wide-area (>0.6 sq. deg) search of public JWST/NIRCam imaging, spanning -21.6≤M_UV≤-17.3. Inverse variance-weighted mean β computed and compared with intrinsic stellar+nebular continuum limits. Sample augmented with prior JWST constraints to z=9 and M_UV-matched HST studies to z=4 to measure β(z) evolution.

Result: Inverse variance-weighted mean ⟨β⟩=-2.57±0.04, extremely blue and consistent with the intrinsic stellar+nebular limit in absence of UV dust attenuation. No evidence for a β–M_UV relation at z≳12. Smooth evolution in β(z) with dβ/dz≈-0.1, becoming progressively bluer at z>4. Scatter in individual β measurements is consistent with a small population of dust-reddened galaxies with β≳-2.

Conclusion: Galaxies at z≳12 are essentially attenuation-free across all observed UV magnitudes, implying dust-attenuated z≲10 galaxies become attenuation-free by z≈11. A smooth bluing of β evolves from z=4, while residual scatter suggests a minor dust-reddened component.

Abstract: We present an analysis of the ultraviolet (UV) continuum slopes $(β)$ of $55$ galaxies at $z\gtrsim 12$, selected from a wide-area ($>0.6$ sq. deg) search across publicly available JWST/NIRCam imaging. Our sample spans the absolute UV magnitude ($M_{\mathrm{UV}}$) range $-21.6 \leq M_{\mathrm{UV}} \leq -17.3$ and we find an inverse variance-weighted mean $\langleβ\rangle = -2.57\pm0.04$ indicating that, on average, the galaxy population is extremely blue at $z\gtrsim 12$. The average $β$ at these redshifts is consistent with the intrinsic stellar plus nebular continuum limit in the absence of UV dust-attenuation. Consistent with earlier JWST works, our analysis suggests that the moderately dust-attenuated galaxies at $z \lesssim 10$ become essentially attenuation-free by $z \simeq 11$. We find no evidence for a $β-M_{\mathrm{UV}}$ relation in our $z\gtrsim 12$ sample, indicating that the attenuation-free interpretation applies across all observed UV magnitudes. By augmenting our sample with previous JWST constraints down to $z=9$, and incorporating $M_{\mathrm{UV}}$-matched HST studies down to $z=4$, we find a smooth evolution in $β(z)$ over a wide range in redshift. The average value of $β$ becomes progressively bluer at $z>4$, with $\mathrm{d}β/\mathrm{d}z \simeq -0.1$. Finally, although we find that the typical galaxy at $z \gtrsim 12$ is consistent with the attenuation-free limit, the scatter in our individual $β$ measurements is consistent with the existence of a small population of dust-reddened galaxies with $β\gtrsim -2$.
