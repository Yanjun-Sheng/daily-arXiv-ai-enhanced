### [1] [The Balmer Absorber Strength Problem of Little Red Dots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28720)
*Hanpu Liu,Eliot Quataert,Bingjie Wang,Jenny E. Greene,Yan-Fei Jiang*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Observed LRD spectra show weaker Balmer absorption in Hα than in Hβ, which conflicts with standard attenuation models; the authors show this is resolved by including emission from the absorbing medium itself (a line-dependent source function).


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Standard attenuation models ignore emission from the absorbing gas, so they cannot explain why some Little Red Dot spectra exhibit weaker Hα absorption than Hβ absorption—an apparent violation of atomic physics predictions.

Method: Proof-of-concept photoionization models of the Balmer-absorbing medium, accounting for emission from the absorber itself; radiative trapping populates the n=3 level of neutral hydrogen (efficient when the medium is optically thick to Hα but not very optically thick to Paschen-α), producing a line-dependent source function.

Result: The Hα source function can be systematically stronger than that of Hβ, filling in the Hα absorption trough and reducing its equivalent width, selectively weakening Hα absorption in qualitative agreement with observations. The magnitude depends on the absorber's distance and internal structure.

Conclusion: Line-dependent source functions provide significant corrections to expected Balmer EWs and offer a new diagnostic of the environment around LRDs, potentially enabling estimates of the central engine mass in future modeling.

Abstract: Little Red Dots (LRDs) commonly show Balmer absorption features. Some observed LRD spectra have weaker absorption in H$α$ than in H$β$, in apparent contradiction to atomic physics according to standard attenuation models. This tension is naturally resolved when one considers emission from the absorbing medium itself, which is ignored in the standard model: when the Balmer absorption is optically thick, a non-negligible source function could fill in the H$α$ absorption trough and reduce its observed absorption equivalent width (EW). With proof-of-concept photoionization models we show that the source function of H$α$ could be systematically stronger than that of H$β$. This is due to radiative trapping preferentially populating the $n=3$ level of neutral hydrogen, which operates efficiently when the medium is optically thick to H$α$ but not very optically thick to Paschen-$α$. Such line-dependent source functions could lead to significant corrections to the expected EW and selectively weaken H$α$ absorption, consistent with observations. Quantitatively, this depends on the distance of the absorbing medium and its internal structure, providing a new diagnostic of the environment around LRDs. Future modeling of the absorbers may also potentially open a new avenue for estimating the mass of the central engine.

*Chuizheng Kong,Hui Li,Long Wang,Yunwei Deng,Elizabeth Moreno-Hilario*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Star formation is hierarchical and clustered, and close stellar encounters strongly affect cluster evolution and feedback, but gravitational softening in typical hydrodynamic GMC simulations limits how such encounters are treated.

Method: A framework coupling collisional star-by-star N-body dynamics to Arepo's moving-mesh MHD: star-star interactions use unsoftened gravity while gas/collisionless interactions remain softened; compact stellar subsystems are identified on the fly and evolved with a fourth-order forward symplectic integrator plus slow-down algorithmic regularization tied to Arepo's native timestep hierarchy. Conclusion: Arepo-CD is a practical, computationally efficient framework for studying collisional stellar dynamics and its coupling to gas evolution and feedback during cluster formation in realistic galactic environments.

Abstract: Star formation is a hierarchical, clustered process in turbulent giant molecular clouds (GMCs), where close stellar encounters can strongly affect cluster evolution and feedback. However, gravitational softening in many hydrodynamic simulations of GMCs limits the treatment of these encounters. We present Arepo-CD (Collisional Dynamics), a framework that couples star-by-star collisional $N$-body dynamics to the moving-mesh magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) code Arepo. In the fiducial GMC comparison, the collisional and softened-gravity runs yield similar star formation histories and integrated star formation efficiencies. Nevertheless, close gravitational encounters during the embedded phase generate a substantial runaway population, with a velocity tail extending beyond 100 km/s. Ejected massive stars redistribute radiative feedback into the surrounding lower-density medium and produce a more extended distribution of heated gas. Arepo-CD provides a practical and computationally efficient framework for studying collisional stellar dynamics and its coupling to gas evolution and feedback during star cluster formation in realistic galactic environments.
