### [1] [Stellar J-Harvesting: a novel angular momentum technosignature and first search in the Kepler field](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07781)
*Sahin Torlakcik*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper introduces Stellar J-Harvesting (SJH), a technosignature where an engineered system extracts rotational angular momentum from a star, detectable as an anomalously slow-rotating star compared to peers. After analyzing 6,725 FGK main-sequence Kepler stars, two >4σ slow rotators were found but explained by ordinary astrophysical causes (binaries, low metallicity), yielding no detection claim and a conservative upper limit f_SJH < 4.5e-4. The main contribution is a search framework for angular-momentum technosignatures using existing stellar rotation catalogues.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To propose and search for a new class of technosignature—Stellar J-Harvesting—where advanced civilizations extract rotational energy from stars, which would be detectable as anomalously slow rotation without the mid-infrared excess typical of Dyson spheres.

Method: Derived the energy-period relation for SJH, outlined coupling channels, and performed a population-relative slow-rotator search on Kepler stars using colour-gravity bins with eight false-positive filters, analyzing 6,725 FGK main-sequence stars with Gaia DR3 and WISE follow-up.

Result: Two >4σ slow rotators were identified but explained by unresolved binaries and low metallicity; no detection was claimed. A conservative pilot upper limit on strong SJH-like signals was placed at f_SJH < 4.5e-4.

Conclusion: Angular-momentum technosignatures are testable with existing stellar rotation catalogues, and the strongest outliers define concrete targets for spectroscopic, imaging, and radio follow-up. The main result is a search framework rather than a detection.

Abstract: We introduce Stellar J-Harvesting (SJH), a technosignature in which an engineered system extracts rotational angular momentum from a star. Unlike a classical Dyson sphere, such a system need not produce a detectable mid-infrared excess; its clearest signature would be a star rotating more slowly than comparable peers. We derive the energy-period relation for this effect, outline several possible coupling channels, and apply a population-relative slow-rotator search to Kepler stars using colour-gravity bins. After eight false-positive filters, the clean sample contains 6,725 FGK main-sequence stars. We find two >4 sigma slow rotators, but Gaia DR3 indicators and WISE imaging point to more ordinary explanations, including unresolved binaries and low metallicity. We therefore make no detection claim and place a conservative pilot upper limit on the occurrence of strong SJH-like signals, f_SJH < 4.5e-4. The main result is a search framework: angular-momentum technosignatures are testable with existing stellar-rotation catalogues, and the strongest outliers define concrete targets for spectroscopic, imaging, and radio follow-up.

### [2] [The incidence of eROSITA X-ray AGN in the local Universe: from dwarf to massive galaxies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07795)
*Z. Igo,A. Merloni,A. Georgakakis,J. Buchner,R. Arcodia,M. Salvato,J. Aird,K. Nandra,B. Trakhtenbrot,P. G. Boorman,J. Comparat,G. Lamer,B. Laloux,M. Kluge,W. Roster,E. Bulbul,F. Balzer,T. Dwelly,W. N. Brandt,R. Seppi,S. Morrison,E. Kyritsis,J. Gelfand,S. F. Anderson,D. P. Schneider*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Using eROSITA All Sky Survey data and Legacy Survey DR10, this paper presents the largest catalogue of X-ray AGN in low-redshift galaxies (including low-mass galaxies with logM*/Msol<10). The study identifies 874 X-ray AGN in low-mass galaxies, constrains specific accretion rate distributions via Bayesian framework, detects a break at high λ_SAR indicating Eddington-limited growth, and finds a cumulative AGN fraction of ~1% for low-mass galaxies, providing a lower limit on black hole occupation fraction and revealing mass-dependent AGN growth.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To leverage deep, wide-area X-ray surveys combined with multi-wavelength data to study rare, highly-accreting AGN and low-mass galaxies at low redshift—potential local analogues of early Universe galaxies—and to probe the specific accretion rate distribution and AGN fraction in the previously unexplored low-mass regime.

Method: Selection of ~5.35 million galaxies from Legacy Survey DR10 with z-band < 20 mag and 0.03<z<0.2; cross-matching with four-pass eROSITA All Sky Survey; detailed characterization of sample including unbiased estimation of physical properties, rigorous cleaning/validation of X-ray aperture photometry and host-galaxy associations; Bayesian framework to constrain specific accretion rate distribution p(logλ_SAR | M*, z) across all parent sample galaxies.

Result: Identified 874 X-ray AGN in low-mass galaxies, most newly discovered as X-ray emitters; detected a break at high λ_SAR suggesting Eddington-limited self-regulated BH growth; cumulative AGN fraction ~1% for low-mass galaxies (λ_SAR > 10^-3), placing a firm lower limit on BH occupation fraction in this regime.

Conclusion: The specific accretion rate distributions, sampling down to the unexplored low-mass regime, reveal a more nuanced, mass-dependent view of AGN growth and accretion history that must be incorporated into future theoretical modelling.

Abstract: Combining deep, wide-area X-ray surveys with multi-wavelength catalogues provides insights into rare, highly-accreting AGN and low-mass galaxies at low redshift, the latter potentially representing local analogues of the first galaxies in the early Universe. We use the four-pass eROSITA All Sky Survey to select the largest catalogue of X-ray AGN in a highly complete sample of low-redshift galaxies, including low-mass (logM*/Msol<10) ones. We probe their distribution of specific accretion rates, $λ$_SAR, and the cumulative AGN fraction above varying $λ$_SAR thresholds. Our parent sample consists of ~5.35 million galaxies selected from the Legacy Survey DR10 with z-band fluxes brighter than 20 mag and redshifts 0.03<z<0.2. We place particular emphasis on the detailed characterisation of our sample, including estimating unbiased physical galaxy properties and rigorous cleaning and validation of the X-ray aperture photometry and host-galaxy associations. We identify 874 X-ray AGN in low-mass galaxies, most of them newly discovered as X-ray emitters. Thanks to a Bayesian framework that makes use of the X-ray information from all parent sample galaxies, we constrain the specific accretion rate distribution, p(log$λ$_SAR | M*, z), across a wide range of $λ$_SAR and uncover second-order mass-dependent effects. We detect a break at high $λ$_SAR, possibly indicating Eddington-limited, self-regulated black hole growth. Integrating p(log$λ$_SAR | M*, z) above $λ$_SAR>10^-3, we find a cumulative AGN fraction of ~1% for low-mass galaxies, placing a firm lower limit on the black hole occupation fraction in this regime. Overall, our specific accretion rate distributions, sampling down to the as-of-yet unexplored low-mass regime, highlight a more nuanced, mass-dependent view of AGN growth and accretion history that must be taken into account in future modelling.

### [3] [Force convergence in Monte Carlo Lyman-alpha radiative transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08726)
*Joshua Kasiri,Aaron Smith,Kevin Lorinc,Olof Nebrin,Kazutaka Kimura*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper develops a moment-based convergence framework for Monte Carlo radiative transfer (MCRT) simulations of Lyman-alpha radiation, focusing on internal momentum-transfer estimators (radiative acceleration, force multiplier) in static spherical clouds. It establishes diffusion-limit benchmarks, proposes a three-tier convergence hierarchy (mean accuracy, sampling uncertainty, variance stability), and evaluates three estimators (event-based scattering, gradient-of-energy-density, divergence-of-radiation-pressure) against core-skipping, source geometry, estimator construction, and spatial resolution.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Convergence of Lya MCRT is difficult to assess in optically thick media. Internal quantities like radiative acceleration and force multiplier depend on momentum deposition throughout the gas, not just emergent spectra, yet no systematic convergence framework exists for these estimators.

Method: Establish diffusion-limit benchmarks for radial acceleration and force multipliers; develop a moment-based convergence hierarchy (zeroth-order: mean accuracy; first-order: sampling uncertainty; second-order: variance stability); apply to three estimators with varying core-skipping, source geometry, estimator construction, and spatial resolution.

Result: Zeroth-order convergence assessed via profile comparisons, integrated force-multiplier bias, and finite-group relative error. First-order convergence quantified by fractional error, required photon number for target precision, and runtime. Second-order convergence tested via coefficient of variation of variance. Core-skipping, source geometry, estimator construction, and spatial resolution affect each hierarchy level differently.

Conclusion: Provides a practical convergence framework for internal Lya MCRT force calculations, demonstrating that statistical precision, computational cost, and physical accuracy must be evaluated separately.

Abstract: Monte Carlo radiative transfer (MCRT) is widely used to model Lyman-alpha (Lya) resonant-line transport, but convergence is difficult to assess in optically thick media where photons undergo many scatterings before escape. This is especially important for internal quantities such as radiative acceleration and the force multiplier, which depend on momentum deposition throughout the gas rather than only on emergent spectra. We study the convergence of Lya MCRT momentum-transfer estimators in static spherical clouds. We first establish diffusion-limit benchmarks for radial acceleration profiles and integrated force multipliers, then develop a moment-based framework for diagnosing convergence from the photon-packet contribution distribution. This framework separates three distinct questions: whether the estimator converges to the correct mean, how large its finite-sampling uncertainty is, and whether the estimated uncertainty is itself stable. We apply this hierarchy to the direct event-based scattering estimator, a gradient-of-energy-density estimator, and a divergence-of-radiation-pressure estimator. Zeroth-order convergence is assessed with profile comparisons, integrated force-multiplier bias, and finite-group relative error. First-order convergence is quantified with fractional error, the photon number required to reach a target precision, and the corresponding runtime requirement. Second-order convergence is tested with the coefficient of variation of variance, which measures the reliability of the variance estimate used in the first-order diagnostics. Core-skipping prescriptions, source geometry, estimator construction, and spatial resolution enter this hierarchy in different ways. Our results provide a practical convergence framework for internal Lya MCRT force calculations and show why statistical precision, computational cost, and physical accuracy must be evaluated separately.

### [4] [Deep Spectroscopic Follow-Up of Maisie's Galaxy -- A Typical Galaxy in the Early Universe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08749)
*Rebecca L. Larson,Taylor A. Hutchison,Steven L. Finkelstein,Pablo Arrabal Haro,Casey Papovich,Weida Hu,Javier Álvarez-Márquez,Ruqiu Lin,Jorge A. Zavala,Volker Bromm,Nikko J. Cleri,Abdurro'uf,Brittany Vanderhoof,Bren E. Backhaus,Dan Coe,Henry C. Ferguson,Ananya Ganapathy,Norman A. Grogin,Michaela Hirschmann,Intae Jung,Jeyhan S. Kartaltepe,Anton M. Koekemoer,Dale D. Kocevski,Ray A. Lucas,Alexa M. Morales,Pablo G. Pérez-González,Nor Pirzkal,Jonathan R. Trump,L. Y. Aaron Yung*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: JWST Cycle 3 NIRSpec G395M and MIRI/LRS observations of Maisie's Galaxy (z=11.408) reveal it to be a typical star-forming galaxy on the SFMS (SFR=1.3 M☉/yr), with subsolar metallicity (Z/Z☉=0.17) and modest ionization parameter, challenging the view that early galaxies are universally extreme.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Early JWST observations found unexpectedly large numbers of bright z>10 galaxies, with spectroscopy suggesting extreme star formation or AGN activity. The authors aim to determine whether these sources are truly extreme or more typical by conducting deep follow-up observations of Maisie's Galaxy.

Method: Combination of two deep Cycle 3 NIRSpec G395M programs (19+ hours total) plus MIRI/LRS observations. Measurements include redshift, [OII] doublet (for electron density and SFR), [OIII]λ5008, [NeIII]λ3869, and derived Ne3O2, O32 ratios. Metallicity and ionization parameter estimated via Ne3O2.

Result: Redshift z=11.408±0.005; electron density n_e=108.56^{+873.9}_{-35.37} cm⁻³; SFR([OII])=1.3±0.35 M☉/yr (on SFMS); log(Ne3O2)=-0.219±0.145; log(O32)=0.724±0.191; Z/Z☉=0.17±0.05; log(U)=-2.26±0.13.

Conclusion: Maisie's Galaxy is a typical, not extreme, source at z>10, lying on the star-formation main sequence. Deeper observations are needed to characterize the average galaxy population at these early epochs.

Abstract: The first several years of JWST observations have yielded surprisingly large numbers of bright $z>10$ galaxies, with follow-up spectroscopy of many of these sources implying extreme star formation activity and/or AGN content. Here, we present a combination of two deep Cycle 3 NIRSpec G395M programs, totaling over 19 hours of exposure time, plus MIRI/LRS observations for one such high-redshift source: Maisie's Galaxy. We provide an updated redshift measurement of $z = 11.408 \pm 0.005$ for this source. Measurements of the [OII] doublet in these data yield an electron density ($n_e = 108.56^{+873.9}_{-35.37}$) and a star-formation rate (SFR$_{[OII]} = 1.3 \pm 0.35$), placing it along the star-formation main sequence (SFMS) and indicating that this is a much more typical, rather than extreme, source in the early Universe. We also report fluxes for the [OIII]$λ$5008 and [NeIII]$λ$3869 lines that provide us with a $\log$(Ne3O2) $= -0.219 \pm 0.145$ and a $\log$(O32) $=0.724 \pm 0.191$. We estimate the metallicity ($Z/Z_{\odot} = 0.17 \pm 0.05$) and ionization parameter ($\log$(U) $= -2.26 \pm 0.13$) from the Ne3O2 ratio. We place this galaxy in the context of other $z>10$ sources with similar line detections and compare the results to those obtained from SED fitting. The results suggest that we should go deeper with our observations to better understand the average galaxy population at these early times.
