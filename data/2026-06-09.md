### [1] [The quasi-star model for Little Red Dots: potential and challenges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06575)
*Fabrizio Gentile,Mauro Giavalisco,Emanuele Daddi,David Elbaz,Jean-Baptiste Billand,Maximilen Franco,Benjamin Magnelli,Guillermo Barro,Yingjie Cheng,Nikko J. Cleri,Kelcey Davis,Ivan Delvecchio,Mark Dickinson,Steven L. Finkelstein,Giovanni Gandolfi,Michaela Hirschmann,Weida Hu,Dale Kocevski,Anton M. Koekemoer,Ray Lucas,Sara Mascia,Lorenzo Napolitano,Casey Papovich,Borja Pérez-Díaz,Pablo Perez-Gonzalez,Jonathan R. Trump,Xin Wang,L. Y. Aaron Yung*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper tests whether the quasi-star model (an accreting SMBH seed ~10^5-6 M⊙ with a convective blackbody layer T~5000 K, reprocessed by a thick dense gas shell and diffuse clumpy medium) can explain the spectral features of Little Red Dots (LRDs) observed by JWST. The results highlight significant degeneracy among LRD models, impacting our understanding of early black hole growth.

Motivation: To test whether the quasi-star model can reproduce the spectral features of Little Red Dots (LRDs) observed by JWST, and to understand the physical nature and evolutionary stage of these sources as potential intermediate stages in SMBH seed evolution into classic AGN.

Method: Radiative-transfer simulations using Cloudy code, modeling an accreting SMBH (M_BH~10^5-6 M⊙) with a convective blackbody layer (T~5000 K, L~10^44.4 erg/s), reprocessed by a concentric thick gas shell (ΔR~1000 AU, n_H~10^11 cm^-3) and a diffuse clumpy medium. Model fits are compared to JWST/NIRSpec spectra of LRDs from the literature.

Result: The quasi-star model can reproduce the UV-to-NIR continuum shape, Balmer break, and hydrogen line luminosities of LRDs when coupled with host galaxy UV emission. However, it does not natively account for broad helium lines or hot dust emission, requiring additional components. The model demonstrates significant degeneracy among different LRD interpretations.

Conclusion: Some LRDs can be modeled as quasi-stars, but the significant degeneracy among LRD models has important implications for understanding black hole growth mechanisms in the early Universe. Additional components are needed to fully explain all observed LRD spectral features.

Abstract: (Abridged) Little Red Dots (LRDs) are a class of sources discovered by JWST observationally defined by a "V-shaped" rest-frame UV-Optical SED, a compact or unresolved morphology, and for having, frequently, broad hydrogen emission lines. Among various models, those involving a quasi-star interpret LRDs as an intermediate stage in the evolution of a super-massive black hole (SMBH) seed into a classic AGN. In this paper, we employ the radiative-transfer code \texttt{Cloudy} to study whether this model is able to reproduce the spectral features commonly observed in LRDs. The model consists of an accreting SMBH ($M_{\rm BH}\sim10^{5-6} \ M_\odot$) surrounded by a convective layer where a black-body (BB) spectrum with $T\sim5000 \ {\rm K}$ and $L\sim10^{44.4} \ {\rm erg \ s}^{-1}$ is produced. This BB is then reprocessed by a concentric thick ($ΔR\sim1000 \ {\rm AU}$) shell of dense ($n_{\rm H}\sim10^{11} \ {\rm cm}^{-3}$) gas partially ionised by thermal collisions. The emerging radiation is further reprocessed by a diffuse clumpy medium surrounding the quasi-star. We fit this model to JWST/NIRSpec spectra of LRDs from the literature, deriving the main physical parameters and the SMBH masses. Once coupled with the UV emission from a host galaxy, this model is able to reproduce the shape of the UV-to-NIR continuum, including the presence of a Balmer break, as well as the luminosity of the hydrogen emission lines. However, this quasi-star model does not natively account for the presence of broad helium lines and for the possible presence of hot dust, needing additional components to match these observables. Our main result is to show how some LRDs can be modeled as quasi-stars, highlighting that a significant degeneracy exists among different LRD models. This has important consequences for our understanding of the mechanisms driving black hole growth in the early Universe.

### [2] [A Population of Red Galaxies with Very Strong Emission Lines at $z > 5$ Revealed by the NIRCam Medium Bands: ''Classic'' LRDs, Dusty Star-Forming Galaxies, and a Missing Population of LRDs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06585)
*Sunna Withers,Adam Muzzin,Swara Ravindranath,Chris J. Willott,Nicholas S. Martis,Roberta Tripodi,Yoshihisa Asada,Maruša Bradač,Maya Merchant,Lamiya Mowla,Gaël Noirot,Ghassan T. E. Sarrouh,Marcin Sawicki,Jacqueline Antwi-Danso,Anishya Harshan,Naadiyah Jagga,Danilo Marchesini,Katherine Myers,Visal Sok*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: Using medium-band imaging from CANUCS, Technicolor, and JUMPS surveys, this paper identifies Emission Line Galaxies (ELGs) at z~4.9-8.9, finds a correlation between continuum color and emission line strength, identifies 26 outlier "Red Emission Line Galaxies" (REGs) comprising classic Little Red Dots (LRDs), extended Dusty Star Forming Galaxies, and compact REGs missed by standard LRD selections, arguing that medium-band selection yields more complete LRD samples.

Motivation: To exploit the efficiency of NIRCam medium-bands in identifying high-EW Hα and [OIII]+Hβ ELGs at high redshift, and to uncover populations of red, strong-emission-line galaxies that may be missed by conventional selection criteria.

Method: Identification of ELGs at 4.9≲z≲8.9 using medium-band imaging from the CANUCS, Technicolor, and JUMPS surveys; analysis of continuum color vs. emission line strength; outlier identification; morphological and color-based classification of REGs into three categories.

Result: ELGs show a strong correlation between bluer continuum and stronger emission lines. 26 outliers (REGs) are identified with red continua and strong lines: classic LRDs, extended DSFGs, and compact REGs (unresolved in F444W but not classified as LRDs due to faint continua, line contamination, or flatter colors).

Conclusion: Compact REGs are likely LRDs missed by classic selection criteria; medium-band selection can provide more complete samples of such objects.

Abstract: The NIRCam medium-bands have proven to be efficient at identifying Emission Line Galaxies (ELGs) with high equivalent width (EW) H$α$ and [OIII]+H$β$ emission lines. In this paper we exploit this efficiency to identify a sample of ELGs at $4.9 \lesssim z \lesssim 8.9$ using medium-band imaging from the CANUCS, Technicolor, and JUMPS surveys. We find that the ELGs exhibit a strong correlation between continuum color and emission line strength, such that galaxies with bluer UV/optical continua have stronger H$α$ and [OIII]+H$β$ emission lines. We identify 26 galaxies that are outliers from this relation, which we call the Red Emission line Galaxies (REGs), because of their red continuum color and strong emission lines. We classify the REGs into three categories: 1) ''classic'' Little Red Dots (LRDs) selected with common literature criteria, 2) extended REGs, resolved in F444W and consistent with being Dusty Star Forming Galaxies (DSFGs), and 3) compact REGs, unresolved in F444W but not classified as LRDs. The compact REGs fail common LRD selections for several reasons, including faint continuua, contamination from emission lines (very strong [OIII]+H$β$), and UV/optical colors that are flatter than those of LRDs. We conclude that the compact REGs are likely LRDs that ''classic'' selection criteria miss, and are therefore missing from existing samples. Our results suggest that medium-band selection can provide more complete samples of these objects.
