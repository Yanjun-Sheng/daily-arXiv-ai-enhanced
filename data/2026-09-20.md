### [1] [Effects of radial conductivity variation on the Ponomarenko dynamo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19187)
*Shipra Verma,Kannabiran Seshasayanan*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The Ponomarenko dynamo's threshold and growth rate depend sensitively on radial conductivity variation near the velocity discontinuity: sharp monotonic profiles map to a conductivity-jump dynamo, sinusoidal profiles raise Rm_c via effective diffusivity, and far from threshold the growth-rate correction scales as the local conductivity gradient times Rm^{-1/2}.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The Ponomarenko dynamo is a canonical kinematic dynamo model, but the role of spatially varying conductivity—rather than uniform conductivity—on its dynamo threshold and growth rate is not well understood. This work examines how radial conductivity profiles modify the dynamo, particularly near the velocity discontinuity where shear dominates.

Method: The kinematic dynamo problem is solved with monotonically increasing/decreasing and sinusoidally varying radial conductivity profiles, evaluating the critical magnetic Reynolds number Rm_c and the growth rate. Sharp-variation limits are mapped to a conductivity-jump dynamo, and an analytical expression for the growth rate is derived in the large-Rm limit (Rm >> 1). Results are also tested with realistic smooth velocity profiles and different magnetic boundary conditions.

Result: Rm_c depends strongly on the conductivity variation near the velocity discontinuity. Monotonic profiles in the sharp-variation limit map to the conductivity-jump dynamo; sinusoidal profiles with large variation/wavenumber raise the threshold due to effective diffusivity from rapid conductivity changes. Far from threshold, the growth rate depends only on the local conductivity gradient near the discontinuity, with opposite signs of shear and conductivity gradient yielding a larger growth rate, aiding regeneration of B_phi from B_r. The large-Rm correction is proportional to the local conductivity gradient times Rm^{-1/2}.

Conclusion: Conductivity variation introduces a correction to the Ponomarenko dynamo that depends solely on the local conductivity gradient at the discontinuity, scaling as Rm^{-1/2}. The findings are robust for smooth velocity profiles and different magnetic boundary conditions.

Abstract: We study the effects of conductivity variation on the Ponomarenko dynamo. Taking monotonically increasing/decreasing and sinusoidally varying radial profiles, we study the kinematic dynamo problem. The threshold of the dynamo, given by the critical magnetic Reynolds number Rm$_c$, is found to strongly depend on the form of conductivity variation near the discontinuity of the velocity field where the shear is dominant. For monotonically varying profiles in the limit of sharp variation, this threshold maps to the problem of the dynamo with a jump in conductivity, while for the sinusoidal profile with large variations/wavenumbers, the threshold increases due to effective diffusivity arising from the rapid changes in conductivity. Far from the threshold, it is found that the growth rate depends only on the local gradient of the conductivity near the discontinuity, with the opposite sign of shear and conductivity gradient leading to a larger growth rate, thereby aiding the dynamo instability in regenerating B$_φ$ from B$_r$. We calculate the expression for the growth rate in the limit of large Rm$(\gg 1)$ and find that the conductivity variation leads to a modification proportional to the local gradient of conductivity times Rm$^{-1/2}$. This correction is found to depend only on the local gradient of the conductivity at the discontinuity. Similar dependencies are found for realistic, smooth velocity profiles and for different magnetic boundary conditions.
