### [1] [The Sun's chemical peculiarity: disentangling Galactic chemical evolution and planetary engulfment in solar twins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01699)
*Mia Babatsikos,Fan Liu,Amanda Karakas,Ilya Mandel,Lachlan Passenger,Megan Bedell,Sharon Wang,Zimo Cheng*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The Sun's apparent chemical peculiarity relative to solar twins is largely explained by Galactic chemical evolution (GCE) rather than planetary ingestion, with only 2–6 candidates showing possible engulfment signatures.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To test whether the Sun's chemical peculiarity arises from Galactic chemical evolution or planetary ingestion, re-analysing high-resolution spectra of 79 solar twins.

Method: Differential spectroscopic analysis using Korg, Bayesian framework to disentangle GCE and planetary engulfment signatures from other processes, with 18 elements measured at 0.015 dex precision.

Result: 62.3±5.8% of solar twins show abundance patterns well-described by GCE trends; 2–6 candidates show chemical signatures consistent with planetary engulfment.

Abstract: Recent observational studies have suggested that the Sun may be chemically peculiar relative to the majority of solar twins. Here, we re-analyse high-resolution, high signal-to-noise spectra of 79 nearby solar twins using a differential spectroscopic approach and Bayesian framework to test whether the Sun's chemical peculiarity arises from Galactic chemical evolution (GCE) or planetary ingestion. Using the spectroscopic tool \texttt{Korg}, we obtain highly precise, validated atmospheric parameters and abundances for 18 elements, with an average abundance precision of 0.015\,dex (3.5\%). Employing an independent Bayesian indicator, we disentangle GCE and planetary engulfment signatures from other processes influencing stellar composition, including intrinsic abundance scatter. Our results indicate that the chemical peculiarity of the Sun relative to the average solar twin is largely driven by GCE effects, with 62.3$\pm$5.8\% of our sample exhibiting abundance patterns well-described by GCE trends. We further identify 2--6 solar twin candidates exhibiting chemical signatures consistent with planetary engulfment that warrant further investigation. These findings reinforce the importance of accounting for GCE effects when interpreting solar twin abundance patterns, and suggest that the Sun may not be chemically peculiar relative to the majority of solar twins.

### [2] [Extreme outflow velocities and weak UV emission lines indicate quasars shedding their dust cocoons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01330)
*Guozhen Ma,Stefan J. Geier,Johan P. U. Fynbo,Lise Christensen,Andrei Berdyugin,Rasmus Frederiksen,Kasper E. Heintz,Phillip D. Henriksen,Jens-Kristian Krogager,Cédric Ledoux,Vilppu Piirola,Palle Møller,Simone Vejlgaard,Hyunseop Choi*

Main category: astro-ph.GA

TL;DR: This paper presents observations of six quasars similar to the rare LoBAL quasar GQ 1309+2904, characterized by high-speed outflows (up to ~0.16c), massive black holes (10^8.7–10^9.4 M☉), Eddington ratios of 0.14–0.34, reddened continua, and low polarization. The authors propose these are weak emission-line quasars emerging from dust cocoons, where dust grain shattering in disc winds produces UV-steeper extinction.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the nature of rare quasars exhibiting very broad, highly blueshifted low-ionization broad absorption lines (LoBALs) and absent broad emission lines, exemplified by GQ 1309+2904, by identifying and analyzing a sample of similar objects.

Method: Observations of six quasars at z≈2.07–3.28; systemic redshifts measured from Hα; spectral fitting with SMC-like and steeper extinction laws; BVR polarimetry for two objects plus GQ 1309+2904; black hole mass and Eddington ratio estimates from Hα emission.

Result: All six are quasars with highly blueshifted BALs (outflows up to ~0.16c); five are LoBALs. BH masses ~10^8.7–10^9.4 M☉, Eddington ratios ~0.14–0.34. All show flat/reddened continua; three fit SMC-like extinction, three require steeper law. Low polarization indicates face-on orientation.

Conclusion: These objects are likely weak emission-line quasars observed through disc winds, caught emerging from dust cocoons. Dust grain shattering in outflows produces UV-steeper extinction. A schematic shedding process is proposed to explain the observed spectral features.

Abstract: The recently discovered low-ionisation broad absorption line (LoBAL) quasar GQ 1309$+$2904 is unusual due to its very broad, highly blueshifted absorption troughs and an absence of broad emission lines except for ${\mathrm{H} α}$. In this paper, we present observations of six quasars that appear very similar to GQ 1309$+$2904 in the rest-frame ultraviolet (UV). We measure the systemic redshifts of these quasars to be $z\approx$ 2.07--3.28 from detected ${\mathrm{H} α}$ emission lines. We confirm that all targets are quasars with highly blueshifted BALs possessing high-speed outflows with velocities up to $\sim 0.16\,c$, and five of them are confidently identified as LoBAL quasars. Based on ${\mathrm{H} α}$ emission, black hole masses and Eddington ratios of these quasars are $M_{\mathrm{BH}} \approx 10^{8.7}$--$10^{9.4}\,M_{\odot}$ and $L_{\mathrm{bol}} / L_{\mathrm{Edd}} \approx$ 0.14--0.34, indicating that their central black holes are very massive and active. Every quasar in our sample exhibits a very flat or reddened continuum. The spectral shapes of three objects are well-fitted by a normal quasar composite reddened by a Small-Magellanic-Cloud-like (SMC-like) extinction curve, while the other three require a steeper extinction law. Broad-band ($BVR$) polarimetry for two of the latter group (plus GQ 1309$+$2904) reveals their low polarisations, consistent with low inclination (more face-on) angles. We propose that these objects are weak emission-line quasars (WLQs) observed through the disc wind, caught emerging from their dust cocoons. As quasars shed their cocoons, dust grains in the disc wind are shattered into smaller particles, producing the UV-steeper extinction curve observed along the outflow. We present a schematic illustration of this shedding process that can account for the peculiar spectral features observed in our sample.
